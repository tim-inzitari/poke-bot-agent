STRAIGHT DRAGAPULT EX — CURRENT STANDARD HUMAN PILOT GUIDE

Guide identity: dragapult
Research status: proposal for production-controller validation
Format reviewed: public Standard 2026 (H-on), through 2026-07-28

IDENTITY AND SCOPE

This guide is for the project's retained plain `dragapult` specialist: a
straight Dragapult ex deck built around Dreepy, Drakloak, Dragapult ex,
Munkidori, Budew, and a flexible Basic-Pokemon toolbox. It is not Hammer Pult,
Dragapult/Blaziken, Dragapult/Dudunsparce, or Dragapult/Dusknoir.

That distinction matters because public tournament sites often place multiple
straight-looking shells under the broad "Dragapult" label. The project's
classifier treats a list with at least three Crushing Hammer plus the Munkidori,
Budew, Unfair Stamp, and Dragapult signatures as `hammer-pult`. This guide
therefore does not import Crushing Hammer sequencing into plain Dragapult.
Likewise, it does not borrow Seething Spirit, Run Away Draw, or Cursed Blast
plans from the three partner variants.

The current public evidence still supports this straight-deck identity. A May
2026 strategy article explicitly separates "Straight Dragapult" from Blaziken
and Dudunsparce variants and describes its plan as early Dreepy development,
Budew item lock, and board disruption with Munkidori and Yveltal. Current
tournament aggregation continues to show the 4-4-3-ish Dragapult line,
Munkidori, Budew, Meowth ex, Fezandipiti ex, Lillie's Determination, Boss's
Orders, Crispin, Poffin, Poké Pad, Ultra Ball, and Night Stretcher as the core.
Recent broad "Dragapult" consensus lists often include four Crushing Hammer;
those are useful metagame evidence but are Hammer Pult under this project's
stricter identity and are not the representative here.

WHAT THE DECK IS TRYING TO DO

Dragapult ex is a 320-HP Stage 2 whose Phantom Dive costs one Fire and one
Psychic Energy, deals 200 to the Active, and places six damage counters across
the opponent's Bench. The attack is not merely "200 plus 60." It is a prize-map
engine. A good attack should improve at least two clocks:

1. the clock on the current Active Pokémon; and
2. a later knockout, forced evolution, awkward bench retreat, or multi-Prize
   turn created by the six counters.

Drakloak is both the bridge to the attacker and the draw engine. Recon Directive
looks at the top two cards, keeps one, and bottoms the other. Keeping one
Drakloak unevolved can be stronger than evolving every available line
immediately, especially when an attacker is already online. Evolve enough to
attack and survive the next exchange; keep enough Recon Directive capacity to
find the next energy, gust, recovery card, or evolution.

Straight Dragapult trades the partner engines' explosive ceilings for deck
space and flexibility. Its extra Basics are not random inclusions:

- Budew buys a setup turn with Item lock.
- Munkidori moves up to three damage counters off one of your Pokémon and onto
  an opposing Pokémon when Darkness Energy is attached.
- Meowth ex converts a bench slot into the exact Supporter needed now.
- Fezandipiti ex can refill after the opponent takes a knockout.
- Lillie's Clefairy ex changes the Weakness of opposing N's Pokémon to Psychic
  and supplies a bench-count-scaling alternative attacker.
- Pecharunt ex can provide a switch route for Darkness Pokémon, but volunteering
  an extra two-Prize target is a real cost.
- Moltres and Yveltal provide specialized single-Prize pressure rather than
  replacing the main Dragapult plan.

The operating principle is "core before toolbox." Establish the Dreepy line,
typed Energy, and next attacker first. Bench a tech only when it fixes a visible
problem or creates a concrete prize route.

SETUP: THE FIRST TWO TURNS

Before drawing, inspect the opening hand in this order:

1. Can I put at least two Dreepy into play?
2. Can I evolve at least one Dreepy next turn?
3. What is my first Phantom Dive energy sequence?
4. Do I need Budew to prevent an opposing explosive Item turn?
5. Is a Meowth ex bench slot worth the Supporter it finds?
6. Which optional Basic would merely expose Prizes without solving a current
   problem?

An ideal opening board is usually two Dreepy plus one useful support Basic, not
a full bench. Two Dreepy make a single gust knockout less likely to end your
attacker chain. A third Dreepy is often reasonable against decks that can remove
evolving Basics or when your hand already has multiple Drakloak. It is less
urgent when bench space is needed for Munkidori, Meowth ex, Fezandipiti ex, or a
matchup attacker.

Going first

Going first is the default preference for this Stage 2 deck. You cannot attack,
so the turn is about making turn two stable:

- Use Buddy-Buddy Poffin or Poké Pad to establish Dreepy redundancy.
- Attach a useful Energy if doing so does not strand the wrong type. A Fire or
  Psychic attachment to the Dreepy most likely to survive keeps Phantom Dive
  on schedule. Darkness usually belongs on Munkidori once that ability has an
  actual damage source and target.
- Bench Meowth ex only when Last-Ditch Catch finds a Supporter that materially
  improves the next turn. The card is not free: it occupies a bench slot and
  offers two Prizes.
- Do not bench Fezandipiti ex "for later" unless a knockout is reasonably live
  or the matchup makes a draw reset especially likely.
- Preserve at least one search route for Drakloak or Dragapult ex rather than
  emptying every search card during setup.

Going second

Going second adds the option to use Budew's Itchy Pollen immediately. Choose
between setup and item lock based on the opposing board:

- Against an Item-dense deck that needs a turn of search or Rare Candy, Budew
  can be your first attacker while Dreepy develop behind it.
- Against a deck already established from its first turn, a low-impact Budew
  attack may simply concede tempo. Prioritize the fastest possible Dragapult.
- If you open Dreepy Active, do not spend multiple resources escaping it unless
  the Active is in immediate knockout danger and the switch preserves your only
  evolution line.
- A Meowth ex search can turn a weak hand into Crispin or Lillie's
  Determination, but only if you can still develop the board after paying the
  bench cost.

THE FIRST PHANTOM DIVE

The first Phantom Dive is the deck's main tempo checkpoint. To reach it, you
need:

- a surviving Dreepy that can become Drakloak and then Dragapult ex;
- one Fire and one Psychic Energy on the attacking line;
- a legal path to the Active Spot; and
- a meaningful plan for all six Bench counters.

Crispin is the cleanest acceleration tool. It searches two different Basic
Energy types, puts one in hand, and attaches the other to a Pokémon. Combine
that attachment with the normal attachment for the turn. Before playing
Crispin, decide which of Fire and Psychic must be attached from the deck and
which must enter the hand. Do not choose mechanically: account for the Energy
already attached, the manual attachment still available, and whether a future
Munkidori needs Darkness.

If a Dragapult line already holds one attack Energy, Crispin commonly finds the
missing attack type plus a Darkness Energy. The missing Fire/Psychic can be
accelerated or manually attached, while Darkness is retained for Munkidori.
That is only a default pattern. If the available attachment destination or hand
state does not complete Phantom Dive, choose the line that actually produces
an attack.

Rosa's Encouragement appears in current straight lists even though it is not in
the Campinas anchor. When you are behind on Prizes, it can attach up to two
Basic Energy from discard to one Stage 2 Pokémon. This creates a recovery line
after disruption or a knockout, but it requires being behind and having the
right Energy already discarded. Do not discard Energy blindly in anticipation
of Rosa; first confirm the card is in the list, accessible, and likely to be
legal when needed.

SEQUENCING THE DRAW AND SEARCH ENGINE

Use information-gaining effects before irreversible commitments.

A robust midgame sequence is:

1. Establish which Pokémon must evolve and which Energy types are missing.
2. Use Recon Directive before playing a shuffle effect if the top two cards can
   solve the turn. If neither is wanted, bottom the worse card and then shuffle.
3. Use Poké Pad for non-Rule-Box Pokémon such as Dreepy, Drakloak, Munkidori,
   Budew, Moltres, or Yveltal.
4. Use Ultra Ball when you need Dragapult ex or a Rule Box Pokémon, or when its
   discard cost deliberately enables Night Stretcher or Rosa.
5. Bench Meowth ex only after you know which Supporter solves the turn.
6. Play the Supporter after extracting any value that would be lost to a hand
   shuffle.
7. Make Energy attachments after the full turn's attacker and pivot route are
   known.

Lillie's Determination shuffles the hand and draws six, or eight while exactly
six Prizes remain. Its first-turn value is especially high, but "draw eight"
does not justify shuffling away a hand that already has the complete turn. Use
available search, Recon Directive, and Last-Ditch Catch deliberately so that
the shuffle improves rather than destroys your plan.

Ultra Ball's two-card discard is a resource-conversion decision. Good discards
include redundant Stadiums, a Supporter that cannot be used this turn when
another copy remains accessible, and an Energy that Night Stretcher or Rosa can
recover for a defined purpose. Bad discards include the only copy of a matchup
attacker, the last recovery card, and the only Energy type that completes the
next Phantom Dive.

Poké Pad cannot find Dragapult ex, Meowth ex, Fezandipiti ex, Pecharunt ex, or
Lillie's Clefairy ex because they have Rule Boxes. Preserve Ultra Ball or
Meowth's Supporter search when one of those is the real need.

ENERGY ACCOUNTING

The representative has only nine Energy: three Fire, three Psychic, and three
Darkness. Treat every Energy as a named resource.

At the start of each turn, count:

- Fire and Psychic already attached to each Dragapult line;
- Fire and Psychic in discard and therefore recoverable;
- Darkness attached to each Munkidori;
- known Energy spent or stranded on a support Pokémon;
- the number of remaining attackers you expect to field; and
- Night Stretcher copies remaining or already used.

The first Dragapult needs Fire plus Psychic. The replacement needs the same.
Munkidori needs Darkness to use Adrena-Brain. A Darkness Energy on Munkidori can
create repeated value, so avoid retreating or discarding it casually.

Do not split one Fire and one Psychic across two Dreepy unless you have a clear
acceleration plan. A half-powered pair often attacks later than one completed
Dragapult plus a searched replacement. Conversely, do not stack excess Energy
onto the Active if the opponent can take it and you have no replacement.

Night Stretcher returns a Pokémon or Basic Energy from discard. Decide whether
the turn is constrained by body or Energy:

- Recover Dreepy/Drakloak/Dragapult ex if an evolution link is missing.
- Recover Fire/Psychic if the attacker exists but cannot reach Phantom Dive.
- Recover Darkness if an exact Adrena-Brain move changes the prize map.
- Recover a one-of tech only when that tech has a concrete immediate role.

PHANTOM DIVE COUNTER PLACEMENT

Before attacking, write the prize map in damage, not in hope.

For every opposing Pokémon, note:

- current HP and current damage;
- whether it will evolve and gain HP;
- whether it is worth one, two, or three Prizes;
- whether it has a damage-prevention effect;
- whether Boss's Orders or Prime Catcher can expose it later;
- whether Munkidori can supply an additional 10–30 damage; and
- whether the counters create a knockout now or only after another attack.

Strong counter placements usually do one of four things:

1. take an immediate Bench knockout;
2. put an evolving Basic or support Pokémon into range before it can leave play;
3. convert the next 200-damage Active hit into a multi-Prize turn; or
4. create two simultaneous threats the opponent cannot both heal or evolve.

Avoid aesthetically even spreads. Three counters on each of two targets are
good only when both placements change a real threshold. Six counters on one
target can be correct when it secures a Prize or forces a costly response.

Remember that Phantom Dive places damage counters; it does not deal attack
damage to the Bench. Effects that prevent damage may not prevent counter
placement, while effects that prevent effects of attacks or damage counters may
matter. Read the exact card in play rather than generalizing from its archetype.

MUNKIDORI AND DAMAGE REPAIR

Adrena-Brain moves up to three damage counters from one of your Pokémon to one
of the opponent's Pokémon when Munkidori has Darkness attached. This is both
healing and offense. Good uses:

- remove 30 damage so the Active Dragapult survives a known attack number;
- finish a 10–30 HP Bench target without spending Phantom Dive counters there;
- adjust a target so Phantom Dive's six counters create a two-knockout turn;
- move damage from a support Pokémon that the opponent planned to finish.

Do not activate merely because you can. Confirm an exact source with damage,
an exact legal target, and a threshold that changes. Moving counters onto a
high-HP target without changing its knockout clock may reveal no value. The
Darkness attachment also competes with Fire/Psychic setup, so power Munkidori
only when the ability is part of the active plan.

BENCH MANAGEMENT

Area Zero Underdepths expands the Bench while a Tera Pokémon is in play. That
extra room enables Dreepy redundancy plus Munkidori and toolbox Basics, but it
is conditional. If the Stadium leaves play, you may have to discard Benched
Pokémon until the normal limit is restored.

Before filling an expanded bench, choose which Pokémon would be discarded if
Area Zero disappears. Never make the answer "my only replacement attacker."
Prioritize:

1. current attacker and replacement Dreepy/Drakloak line;
2. powered Munkidori when its counters matter;
3. a support Pokémon whose ability is live now;
4. matchup attacker with a defined Prize target;
5. redundant or already-spent liabilities last.

Meowth ex is strongest when Last-Ditch Catch finds the turn's exact Supporter.
It is weakest when benched preemptively and then stranded as a two-Prize target.
Fezandipiti ex has the same discipline: bench it after the opponent has taken a
knockout and its draw ability is available, not solely because it is in hand.

Pecharunt ex can switch a Darkness-type Pokémon from the Bench into the Active
Spot, at the cost of Poisoning it, when its ability is applicable. In this
anchor that can promote Yveltal or Fezandipiti ex, but not Psychic-type
Munkidori merely because Munkidori has a Darkness Energy attached. The switch
can still expose a support Pokémon and another two-Prize liability. Use it to
complete a known attack or escape a trap, not as routine board decoration.

PRIZE MAPPING

The deck commonly wins with three two-Prize knockouts, but Phantom Dive lets it
build better maps:

- 2 + 2 + 2: straightforward Rule Box exchange.
- 1 + 1 + 2 + 2: Bench counters collect small supports while Active attacks
  handle Rule Box Pokémon.
- 2 + 3 + 1: a list-specific matchup attacker handles a three-Prize target
  while Phantom Dive prepares the last Prize.
- 1 + 2 + 3 in one compressed endgame: a Bench knockout plus a gusted Active
  can close before the opponent gets another attack.

At six Prizes, decide whether your intended final turn uses Boss's Orders,
Prime Catcher, Munkidori, or raw Phantom Dive spread. Preserve the unique card
that enables that ending. Spending Prime Catcher for minor tempo is wrong when
it is the only way to force the final target Active; holding it forever is also
wrong when using it now protects two setup turns.

FIRST- AND SECOND-PLAYER MIDGAME PLANS

When you went first, your advantage is evolution timing. Aim to attack first
with Dragapult while retaining a Drakloak engine. Force the opponent to choose
between removing the Active attacker and disrupting the replacement.

When you went second, your compensation is an optional Budew attack and the
first Supporter. If Budew delays the opponent, use that borrowed turn to build
two Dragapult lines. If Budew is unlikely to matter, accelerate immediately.
Do not remain in Item lock after the opponent's board is established merely
because it was useful on turn one.

RECOVERY LINES

If the first Dreepy is knocked out:

- promote the lowest-cost pivot, not automatically the next Dreepy;
- preserve the surviving Dreepy's attachment;
- use Poffin/Poké Pad/Night Stretcher to restore two lines if possible;
- count whether Crispin plus the manual attachment still produces an attack.

If the first Dragapult is knocked out:

- activate Fezandipiti ex only after confirming its ability is live;
- decide whether Night Stretcher needs to recover a Pokémon or Energy;
- use a single-Prize attacker only if it changes the prize exchange or buys a
  full setup turn;
- avoid benching every recovery target and creating an easy Boss route.

After Judge or another hand reset:

- use Recon Directive before committing a shuffle;
- use Meowth ex to recover the exact Supporter if a bench slot is safe;
- identify the minimum winning turn rather than rebuilding the entire ideal
  board.

If Area Zero is removed:

- discard spent support liabilities first;
- keep the replacement attacker and powered Munkidori;
- reassess whether replacing the Stadium is worth the card and tempo.

MATCHUP PLANS

The public matchup table is observational, not a guarantee. Use it to choose
testing priorities; play the board in front of you.

Mirror and other Dragapult variants

The broad public mirror is close to even. Protect multiple Dreepy and avoid
offering low-HP Basics that let the opponent turn Phantom Dive into free
Prizes. Keep track of opposing Munkidori and possible counter movement before
declaring a target safe. Lillie's Clefairy ex does not create Psychic Weakness
on ordinary Dragapult ex; Fairy Zone applies to opposing N's Pokémon. Bench it
in the mirror only if Full Moon Rondo's visible bench count creates a better
attack or exchange. Against a Hammer build, typed-Energy redundancy matters
more; against Dusknoir, damage thresholds can change without an attack. Those
are opponent adaptations, not permission to treat your own deck as those
variants.

Rocket's Mewtwo

Public results put this matchup near even. Deny a clean multi-Prize map:
develop two Dreepy, limit unused Rule Box supports, and spread only toward
targets that remain relevant after evolution. Save gust for an engine Pokémon
or a final Prize rather than a low-value hit. Watch for stadium interactions
before overfilling Area Zero.

Alakazam/Dudunsparce

The public aggregate is also near even. Multiple small Pokémon can make
Phantom Dive attractive, but Run Away Draw can remove a damaged Dudunsparce
from play and erase your setup. Place counters on targets that cannot simply
shuffle themselves away, or take the immediate knockout. Hand disruption is
best when paired with board pressure; Judge without a threatening attack gives
the draw engine time to recover.

Starmie/Froslass

Public results slightly favor Dragapult. The opponent punishes ability-heavy
benches, so every Drakloak, Munkidori, Meowth ex, and Fezandipiti ex must earn
its place. Get the first meaningful Phantom Dive and use counters to remove
small Froslass or developing Staryu when that changes the damage engine. Do not
bench both draw supports by habit.

Festival Lead

Public results favor Dragapult. The opposing deck presents many single-Prize,
low-HP targets, making exact Phantom Dive placement decisive. Build turns that
take an Active Prize and a Bench Prize together. Respect stadium competition:
losing Area Zero can collapse your bench, while Festival Grounds enables the
opponent's engine.

Crustle

The public table shows this as a severe problem. Check the exact defensive
effect before attacking. Phantom Dive's Active damage and Bench counter
placement interact differently with prevention text. Preserve Yveltal,
Munkidori, or another list-specific answer if it bypasses the visible lock.
Do not repeatedly spend Phantom Dive into an immune Active when the Bench
counters do not create a winning route. This matchup needs dedicated testing;
the guide does not claim an automatic answer.

Cynthia's Garchomp

Public results favor Dragapult strongly, but do not become careless. Remove
evolving Basics before they become durable attackers, keep a replacement line,
and avoid exposing a toolbox Pokémon for no value. Straight pressure plus
targeted spread is usually better than an elaborate tech route.

COMMON MISTAKES

1. Starting one Dreepy and filling the rest of the bench with support Pokémon.
2. Evolving every Drakloak and losing Recon Directive before the replacement
   attacker is secure.
3. Using Crispin without first deciding which Energy is attached and which is
   kept.
4. Attaching Darkness to Munkidori before an exact Adrena-Brain use exists.
5. Splitting Fire/Psychic across attackers so neither can Phantom Dive.
6. Spreading six counters evenly without changing any knockout threshold.
7. Benching Meowth ex or Fezandipiti ex before their ability is live.
8. Discarding one-of techs or the last needed Energy type with Ultra Ball.
9. Treating Area Zero's expanded bench as permanent.
10. Using Boss's Orders for damage that does not improve the Prize map.
11. Forgetting that current public "Dragapult" lists with the project's Hammer
    signature are Hammer Pult, not this specialist.
12. Borrowing partner-variant lines that the actual 60 cards cannot execute.

TURN CHECKLIST

Before actions

- What is my exact Prize map?
- Which Dragapult attacks this turn, and which one attacks next turn?
- How many Fire, Psychic, and Darkness Energy are visible?
- Which opposing Bench targets change thresholds with 10–60 damage?
- What happens to my bench if Area Zero leaves play?

Before search or draw

- Can Recon Directive improve the information first?
- Does Poké Pad find the card, or does its Rule Box require Ultra Ball?
- Is Meowth ex worth two Prizes and a bench slot for this Supporter?
- Which cards become recoverable if discarded now?

Before attacking

- Does the Active 200 damage produce or set up a Prize?
- Where do all six counters go, and what exact future threshold does each
  placement create?
- Can Munkidori take a Prize or preserve the Active first?
- Am I preserving the gust/recovery card required for the final turn?

SHORT TRAINING-SYSTEM APPENDIX

The human guide above is intentionally broader than any safe heuristic teacher.
The proposed sparse teacher may label only complete legal-action stages whose
prompt origin, current options, and relevant public/acting-seat state are fully
resolved. It may express opening Basic priorities, exact visible search gaps,
and conservative current-turn draw-ability use. It must abstain on typed-Energy
routing, manual attachment, Ultra Ball discards, Phantom Dive counter
placement, Munkidori routing, gust, attack choice, prize-map forecasts,
matchup-specific tactics, hidden zones, future draws, and outcome-conditioned
choices. Unsupported, ambiguous, malformed, incomplete, or low-margin stages
are masked, never given a zero target.

EVIDENCE LIMITS

- Public tournament labels do not enforce this project's Hammer-Pult signature.
  Broad Dragapult aggregates mix project identities and cannot prove a plain-
  only matchup percentage.
- The exact representative predates NAIC but remains the locally declared
  plain representative. A newer pure, non-Hammer, non-partner 60-card selection
  requires a production identity audit rather than a research-agent guess.
- Public sources explain principles, not every exact prompt encoding in the
  simulator. Unsupported prompt types remain masked until replay-backed schema
  validation exists.
- Matchup percentages are observational and variant-mixed. They prioritize
  tests; they do not become per-action teacher labels.
