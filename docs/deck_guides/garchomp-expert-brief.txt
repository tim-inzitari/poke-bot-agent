CYNTHIA'S GARCHOMP EX
PRACTICAL PILOT GUIDE FOR EXPERT REVIEW

Specialist ID: garchomp
Guide version: cynthias-garchomp-north-star-v1
Prepared: 2026-07-26
Audience: world-champion and equivalent Pokémon TCG subject-matter experts
Status: research pilot; not yet expert-approved


HOW TO READ THIS PILOT

This document is a practical guide to piloting Cynthia's Garchomp ex. It is
not a claim that one 60-card list, turn order, or matchup plan is universally
correct.

The evidence is date-sensitive. The public PokéBeach article was published
April 17, 2026. The Limitless tournament and deck pages were reviewed July 26,
2026. The community matchup report was published June 30, 2026. Lists and
matchups can change rapidly, so verify every card against the exact list and
event format.

Labels used below:

SOURCE: directly supported by a source listed at the end.

INFERENCE: strategic reasoning from sourced card text, successful lists, or
the deck's public structure. Experts should challenge these statements.

LIST CHECK: advice that applies only if the registered list contains the card.

SME CHECK: an unresolved question intentionally left for expert review.

Use current printed card text and current rulings for exact costs, legality,
damage, and timing. This guide paraphrases mechanics rather than replacing the
cards or rulebook.


QUICK START: THE DECK IN ONE MINUTE

SOURCE: Cynthia's Garchomp ex is a 330-HP Stage 2. Corkscrew Dive attacks for
100 and may refill the pilot's hand to six. Draconic Buster attacks for 260
and discards all Energy from Garchomp. Cynthia's Gabite can search one
Cynthia's Pokémon once during each turn. Cynthia's Roserade makes attacks by
Cynthia's Pokémon deal 30 additional damage to the opposing Active. Cynthia's
Power Weight adds 70 HP to the Cynthia's Pokémon holding it.

The deck's basic plan is:

1. Open multiple Cynthia's Gible while keeping enough bench space for
   Roselia/Roserade.
2. Evolve at least one Gible into Gabite quickly. Use Champion's Call to
   chain more Cynthia's Pokémon and stabilize the whole board.
3. Establish the number of Roserade needed for the relevant damage threshold.
4. Attach Energy every turn. Protect the attachments and evolution pieces
   with Power Weight where that changes the opponent's route.
5. Use Corkscrew Dive to attack, refill, and bridge to the next turn.
6. Use Draconic Buster only when the larger hit produces enough value to repay
   the Energy discard.
7. Always prepare the next Garchomp before the current one is removed.

The deck wins by combining three kinds of pressure:

- consistency from Gabite chains and Corkscrew Dive;
- durability from naturally high HP plus Power Weight; and
- damage thresholds from one or more Roserade.

The deck loses structure when it misses the first Gabite, benches too few
Gible to replace an attacker, exposes every Roserade to an easy gust route, or
uses Draconic Buster without a credible next Energy turn.


STRATEGIC IDENTITY

SOURCE: Grant Manley's public PokéBeach article describes the deck as simple,
consistent, and naturally strong into much of the April 2026 field. It
identifies Gabite's search chain, Garchomp's attack-based draw, and Power
Weight's protection as the central engine. It recommends prioritizing
consistency over speculative tech cards when the natural matchup spread is
already favorable.

SOURCE: Neddy Kosek's third-place Prague Regional list played four Gible, four
Gabite, three Garchomp ex, four Roselia, three Roserade, one Spiritomb, four
Power Weight, nine Energy, and a dense consistency package. That result
supports a linear, redundant board as a competitive baseline, not as the only
valid build.

INFERENCE: Think of Cynthia's Garchomp as a resilient tempo deck. It is not a
pure one-hit-knockout deck, even though Draconic Buster can deliver a large
hit. The usual advantage comes from making the opponent solve a 330- or
400-HP attacker while your first attack repairs the hand and your Stage 1s
search the next pieces.

At the start of a game, answer four questions:

- How many Garchomp attackers will this prize race require?
- How many Roserade are needed for the important damage threshold?
- Which Pokémon must receive early Energy and Power Weight?
- Is the win based on repeated Corkscrew Dive pressure, a timed Draconic
  Buster, or a Spiritomb counterattack?

If the answers require more bench slots than exist, reduce the plan before
placing the next Basic.


THE CORE ENGINE AND CARD ROLES

The Gible-Gabite-Garchomp line

SOURCE: Successful lists repeatedly use a thick 4-4-3 evolution line. The
public PokéBeach article says the deck wants many Gible and at least one Gabite
as soon as possible. Gabite's Champion's Call searches any Cynthia's Pokémon,
including another Gabite or the Garchomp needed next.

Practical role:

- Gible is both the future attacker and the container for early Energy.
- Gabite is the deck's search engine. One Gabite starts the chain; multiple
  Gabite make the board resilient and turn future Cynthia's Pokémon into
  known access rather than hopeful draws.
- Garchomp is the attacker, hand repair, and wall.

INFERENCE: Do not evolve every Gabite into Garchomp merely because the option
is available. An unevolved Gabite may be the search action that finds the
replacement Garchomp or missing Roserade. Evolve according to the next attack
and prize map, while preserving enough Champion's Call access for the turn.

The Roselia-Roserade line

SOURCE: Cynthia's Roserade's Cheer On to Glory adds 30 damage to attacks used
by Cynthia's Pokémon against the opposing Active. The Prague list used a 4-3
line. Manley's public article emphasizes consistency rather than narrow techs.

Practical thresholds from the printed Garchomp attacks:

- no Roserade: Corkscrew Dive 100; Draconic Buster 260;
- one Roserade: 130 and 290;
- two Roserade: 160 and 320;
- three Roserade: 190 and 350;

before Weakness, Resistance, and other modifiers.

INFERENCE: Build backward from the target, not forward from the maximum number
of Roserade. A second Roserade is valuable when 320 rather than 290 changes a
knockout, or when redundancy matters because the opponent can gust the first.
A third is valuable when 350 is a real threshold or when prizes/search make
two unreliable. It is not free: each Roserade consumes a bench slot that
could hold the replacement Garchomp line.

Cynthia's Power Weight

SOURCE: Power Weight adds 70 HP to the Cynthia's Pokémon holding it. Manley
highlights its value on both Garchomp and the pre-evolutions: it can protect an
Energy-bearing Gible or Gabite from early pressure and later make Garchomp a
400-HP attacker.

INFERENCE: The default attachment is not automatically the Active Garchomp.
Attach where the extra 70 HP changes the opponent's next public damage route:

- an Energy-bearing Gible that otherwise disappears before evolving;
- the only Gabite and search engine;
- the Garchomp expected to absorb the next attack; or
- a Roserade whose survival preserves an exact knockout threshold.

Do not spend the Tool merely because a target is legal. Name which knockout,
gust line, or damage threshold it denies.

Energy package

SOURCE: Kosek's Prague list used five basic Fighting Energy and four Rocky
Fighting Energy. Later lists vary and may use Neo Upper Energy, Fighting Gong,
or recovery cards. Garchomp's second attack discards all Energy attached to
it, so the timing of the second hit matters.

INFERENCE: Treat every early attachment as part of a two-attacker plan:

- Energy one establishes the first Corkscrew Dive.
- The next attachment either enables Draconic Buster on the current Garchomp
  or begins the replacement attacker.
- If Draconic Buster is scheduled, identify the exact method of rebuilding
  Energy on the following turn before committing.

LIST CHECK: Rocky Fighting Energy, Neo Upper Energy, Fighting Gong, and
recovery effects have list- and card-specific conditions. Do not assume they
are interchangeable.

Cynthia's Spiritomb

SOURCE: Spiritomb's Raging Curse does 10 damage for every damage counter on
all of the pilot's Benched Cynthia's Pokémon, without applying Weakness. The
Prague list included one Spiritomb. A June 2026 community pilot report
describes it as a retaliation option after spread damage accumulates.

INFERENCE: Spiritomb is a conditional closer or tempo reversal, not a required
opening bench. It is strongest when public damage already exists on multiple
Cynthia's Pokémon and the opponent cannot remove or neutralize the setup
before Spiritomb attacks. Bench it too early and it may become an easy prize
without changing the game.

Consistency and disruption

SOURCE: The Prague list used Lillie's Determination, Larry's Skill,
Buddy-Buddy Poffin, Poké Pad, Fighting Gong, Pokégear, Boss's Orders, and
Unfair Stamp. Manley argues that consistency cards effectively function as
matchup techs when the deck's natural board is already strong.

INFERENCE: Use search in dependency order:

1. legal Basic and at least one Gible line;
2. first Gabite;
3. a second Gible/Gabite line;
4. Roserade count for the immediate damage threshold;
5. Garchomp and Energy for the current attack;
6. recovery, gust, or disruption for the next exchange.

LIST CHECK: A supporter that discards or replaces cards should be sequenced
only after identifying the Garchomp, Energy, recovery, and future supporter
that must survive the cost.


SETUP AND OPENING PRIORITIES

Opening board target

SOURCE: Manley says the deck needs many Gible and at least one Gabite quickly.
The successful Prague list supports high counts of both the Garchomp and
Roserade lines.

INFERENCE: A robust generic opening aims for:

- two Gible when possible, with a third when the matchup threatens early gust
  or spread;
- one or two Roselia according to the known threshold and expected targeting;
- at least one future Gabite route;
- one open bench slot until the attack and matchup plan are clear.

Avoid opening Spiritomb unless the matchup and damage map already justify it.

Prize checking

The first search effect should answer:

- How many Garchomp, Gabite, Roserade, and Power Weight are available?
- Is the ACE SPEC available?
- Is Spiritomb available if the matchup wants it?
- How many Energy and recovery cards remain?

INFERENCE: Adjust immediately. If one Garchomp is prized, protect the other
lines. If Roserade is constrained, do not plan damage that only the missing
copy creates. If Energy is constrained, value Corkscrew Dive and avoid an
unnecessary Draconic Buster discard.

Bench discipline

Every placement must serve one of five jobs:

- current attacker;
- next attacker;
- search engine;
- damage modifier;
- conditional closer or recovery line.

A common ideal-looking board of three Gabite and three Roserade is impossible.
Choose the balance that serves the matchup:

- three attacker/search slots plus two damage-modifier slots;
- four attacker/search slots plus one damage-modifier slot; or
- a reduced attacker or modifier count with Spiritomb as a scheduled closer.

Do not fill the last slot without naming the next two attacks.


GOING FIRST

The reviewed authoritative sources do not declare a universal first-or-second
choice. The following is an inference for expert review.

INFERENCE: Going first favors the natural Gabite engine because Gible placed
on the first turn can evolve on the next turn. Prioritize:

- two or more Gible when public options permit;
- a protected Energy attachment to a Gible likely to survive;
- only the Roselia count the initial threshold plan requires;
- a hand or known search path to Gabite; and
- an open bench slot.

The first strong attacking turn should ideally contain:

- at least one Champion's Call before evolving the attacker further;
- a Garchomp ready for Corkscrew Dive;
- another Gible or Gabite in development;
- the needed Roserade threshold; and
- Power Weight placed where it changes the response.

Common going-first error:

Attaching Energy to the Active Gible by habit when a Benched Gible is safer
and Switch/free-retreat resources can later promote the attacker.

SME CHECK: Under the current official first-turn rules and common field, is
going first the default choice? Which matchup exceptions prefer second?


GOING SECOND

INFERENCE: Going second trades the earliest natural evolution advantage for
access to the format's legal turn-one supporter and attack options. It may be
correct when the exact list has a strong supporter-driven setup or when an
early single-Prize attack changes the opponent's development.

Prioritize:

- maximum legal Basic development without consuming all bench space;
- a supporter line that guarantees, rather than merely hopes for, the first
  Gabite;
- a protected Energy attachment;
- an Active that can retreat or attack without trapping the board; and
- a next-turn hand that reaches Garchomp plus continued search.

Do not force an attack that leaves only one Gible and no replacement. A small
turn-one attack is valuable only if it advances prizes or protects the future
board better than pure setup.

Common going-second error:

Using every available search card before deciding how many Roselia are
actually required, then lacking a slot for the second Gible.


TURN-BY-TURN SEQUENCING

Turn 1: build a chain, not a display

1. Identify the target number of Garchomp attacks.
2. Count the Roserade needed for the first meaningful damage threshold.
3. Establish multiple Gible.
4. Establish Roselia only to that target plus justified redundancy.
5. Attach Energy to the line most likely to survive and attack.
6. Preserve an open slot when the matchup is not fully identified.

Turn 2: use every Champion's Call before hiding it

1. Evolve available Gible into Gabite.
2. Use each available Champion's Call in an order that leaves alternative
   searches open.
3. Find the missing attacker, next Gabite, Roserade, or Spiritomb required by
   the public board.
4. Evolve only the Gabite that must become Garchomp now.
5. Attach Power Weight after comparing all legal targets.
6. Attach Energy according to current and replacement attacks.
7. Choose Corkscrew Dive or Draconic Buster from the prize map, not maximum
   printed damage.

INFERENCE: If the hand is already functional, Champion's Call should often
build the next board rather than overfill the current hand. If the hand is
fragile, search the piece that makes Corkscrew Dive legal and lets the attack
repair it.

Midgame: preserve the loop

At the start of every turn:

- use Champion's Call before evolving away the final Gabite;
- count Energy in play, discard, and known recovery;
- count Roserade currently contributing;
- update damage after any Roserade is removed;
- name the current attack and the next attack;
- identify whether the opponent wins faster by gusting Roserade, Gabite, or a
  damaged Garchomp.

Closeout

Stop building once an exact legal winning route exists. Use only the search,
gust, Energy, switch, Tool, and attack needed to make that route resilient to
the currently observable reply.


CORKSCREW DIVE VERSUS DRACONIC BUSTER

Corkscrew Dive

SOURCE: Corkscrew Dive does 100 damage and may draw until the hand reaches six
cards.

Prefer it when:

- its modified damage takes the needed knockout;
- the hand refill is important;
- preserving Energy maintains the next turn;
- a two-hit route forces the opponent through 400 HP; or
- Draconic Buster's extra damage does not change prizes.

INFERENCE: Sequence playable low-value cards before Corkscrew Dive only when
doing so improves the refill without weakening recovery. Do not burn a useful
Boss, Energy, Power Weight, or evolution piece merely to draw one extra card.

Draconic Buster

SOURCE: Draconic Buster does 260 and discards all Energy from Garchomp.

Prefer it when:

- the modified damage takes a high-value knockout;
- the extra damage denies an opponent's next attack or draw engine;
- the game ends;
- another Garchomp is already funded; or
- a verified recovery/acceleration line restores tempo.

Avoid it when:

- Corkscrew Dive already takes the same prizes;
- the target survives both attacks;
- discarding Energy removes the only next-turn attack;
- the opponent can answer with an easy two-Prize knockout while the
  replacement is unready; or
- the attack is being chosen only because 260 is larger than 100.

Decision checklist:

- Exact damage with current Roserade and visible modifiers?
- Exact opposing HP and damage?
- Prizes taken now?
- Energy discarded?
- Next legal attacker?
- Opponent's easiest response?
- Does Corkscrew refill prevent a later failure?


RESOURCE AND ATTACK PLANNING

Energy

Plan attachments over three turns:

- current attack;
- post-knockout replacement;
- recovery after Draconic Buster.

Protect the attachment with Power Weight when that changes an exact threshold.
Do not attach the special Energy required for a later burst to a line likely to
be gusted before evolving.

Power Weight

Before attaching:

- Which legal Cynthia's Pokémon is most likely to be attacked from the public
  board?
- Does +70 HP cross the opponent's exact visible damage?
- Does protecting this Pokémon preserve Energy, Champion's Call, Roserade
  damage, or the current attacker?
- Is a different Tool required on that target?
- Does Stadium/tool removal make this line unreliable?

Search

Champion's Call is flexible. Keep the choice tied to a named action:

- Gabite to create another search;
- Garchomp to attack now or next;
- Roserade to hit an exact threshold;
- Gible/Roselia to rebuild;
- Spiritomb only for a calculable Raging Curse.

Gust

Use Boss's Orders when the target:

- produces more prizes than attacking the Active;
- removes an engine that breaks the durability plan;
- removes the Roserade/Gabite hunter before it acts;
- creates an exact two-turn win; or
- prevents the opponent from resetting the prize exchange.

Do not gust a small target if doing so gives up the durable Active exchange and
does not improve the final prize count.


BENCH AND PRIZE MAPPING

Before placing each Basic, write the opponent's easiest six prizes. Garchomp
gives two. Roserade, Gabite, Gible, and Spiritomb usually give one, but their
value as gust targets differs.

INFERENCE: The opponent often does not need to defeat two 400-HP Garchomp if
the bench offers four or six easier prizes. Therefore:

- protect the only Gabite when it controls future search;
- build Roserade redundancy only to the threshold needed;
- delay Spiritomb until its attack is live;
- do not strand damaged pre-evolutions after their role is gone;
- prepare a second Garchomp before promoting the first.

Your prize map should list:

- target order;
- prizes per target;
- exact modified damage;
- Energy cost and discard;
- gust required;
- earliest winning turn.

The opponent's map should list:

- Garchomp knockouts required;
- easier bench prizes;
- ability to remove Roserade and lower damage;
- ability to remove Gabite and stop search;
- Grass Weakness pressure;
- earliest winning turn.

Update both after every knockout, gust, evolution, Tool attachment, and
Roserade change.


RECOVERY LINES

SOURCE: Competitive lists differ on recovery. The Prague list was very lean,
while later public lists commonly include Night Stretcher or other recovery.
Treat recovery as list-specific.

After losing Garchomp:

1. Identify the closest surviving Gible/Gabite line.
2. Use remaining Champion's Call before evolving it.
3. Count Garchomp and Energy in every causally visible zone.
4. Recover the missing link, not automatically the highest-stage Pokémon.
5. Recalculate Roserade damage after the opponent's turn.
6. Decide whether the next attack is a rebuilding Corkscrew Dive or a decisive
   Draconic Buster.

After losing Gabite:

- determine whether another Champion's Call remains;
- use direct search or recovery to restore the Stage 1;
- do not evolve the final Gabite away unless the immediate attack is worth
  losing the search engine.

After losing Roserade:

- reduce all planned damage by 30 before choosing actions;
- rebuild only if the restored threshold changes prizes;
- otherwise spend the turn on the attacker and Energy chain.

After Draconic Buster:

- promote or prepare the already funded attacker;
- recover Energy only if the list permits it;
- avoid using a recovery supporter if Corkscrew Dive can rebuild the hand and
  an ordinary attachment keeps the attack loop alive.


MATCHUP PLANS

These are dated plans, and several rely on one community pilot rather than
large controlled samples. Use observed cards, not the archetype label alone.

Grass decks

SOURCE: Garchomp is Grass-weak. Manley identified Meganium as the clearest
April 2026 obstacle. A June 2026 community pilot described Grass matchups as
extremely unfavorable.

Plan:

- avoid investing in a Power Weight plan that Weakness still defeats;
- establish the fastest meaningful damage threshold;
- take easy multi-Prize targets if the opponent exposes them;
- use non-Garchomp attackers only when the exact list contains a credible
  route;
- force the opponent to have repeated Grass attackers rather than one.

SME CHECK: Which current Grass archetypes are merely unfavorable and which are
functionally unwinnable? Which tech changes enough games to justify reducing
core consistency?

Dragapult variants

SOURCE: The June community pilot calls the matchup close and notes that two
Roserade raise Draconic Buster to 320. It also highlights Spiritomb's damage
after the opponent spreads damage. This is anecdotal, not a formal matchup
rate.

Plan:

- establish multiple Gible so spread does not remove the only line;
- protect vulnerable pre-evolutions with Power Weight where it crosses the
  spread threshold;
- build at least the Roserade count needed for the relevant knockout;
- expect Roserade to be gusted and preserve redundancy;
- calculate Spiritomb only from exact current Benched damage.

Alakazam

SOURCE: One June community pilot reported poor results and identified Energy
denial as a problem. This is weak evidence and should not be generalized
without controlled games.

Plan:

- prefer basic Energy when special-Energy removal is publicly established and
  the list permits;
- spread attachments across viable attackers when one removal would otherwise
  end the turn;
- use Corkscrew Dive to recover hand quality after disruption;
- do not attach or discard Energy without mapping the next attack.

Mega Lucario ex and fast Fighting pressure

SOURCE: Manley specifically cites Power Weight as protection against fast
attacks such as Mega Lucario ex's Aura Jab.

Plan:

- Power Weight the evolution piece whose survival preserves the next turn;
- build a second Gible immediately;
- avoid exposing an unprotected sole Gabite;
- use the first Corkscrew Dive to stabilize rather than overextend.

Dudunsparce and ability engines

SOURCE: A June community list recommends Team Rocket's Watchtower partly
because Dudunsparce-style draw engines were common. The Prague discussion also
notes that such disruption can matter but may be nonessential when Garchomp is
already established.

LIST CHECK:

- use Stadium disruption only when the list contains it and the observed
  opposing engine depends on it;
- do not delay the Garchomp engine merely to deploy a marginal tech;
- remove or strand the opponent's engine when it produces a concrete tempo
  gain.

Generic two-hit opponents

SOURCE: Garchomp plus Power Weight reaches 400 HP, and Manley says much of the
field struggles to remove it in one attack.

Plan:

- force the opponent to spend two attacks on one Garchomp;
- use Corkscrew Dive between hits to keep the hand functional;
- prepare the replacement while the first absorbs damage;
- deny easy bench prizes that bypass the wall.

Generic one-hit opponents

Plan:

- do not assume Power Weight saves Garchomp;
- trade Draconic Buster for the highest-value knockout;
- pre-fund the next attacker;
- reduce expendable one-Prize liabilities;
- use disruption only when it changes the opponent's next attack.


COMMON MISTAKES

1. Opening only one Gible when more are legally available.

Correction: build attacker redundancy before optional support.

2. Evolving every Gabite into Garchomp.

Correction: use every Champion's Call and preserve a search engine when the
immediate attack does not require all Garchomp.

3. Benching maximum Roselia without checking thresholds.

Correction: calculate 260/290/320/350 and 100/130/160/190 first.

4. Attaching Power Weight automatically to the Active.

Correction: protect the Pokémon whose survival changes the opponent's route.

5. Using Draconic Buster whenever it is legal.

Correction: compare prizes and the next attack after discarding Energy.

6. Using Corkscrew Dive before playing safe expendable cards.

Correction: sequence for a useful refill, but never burn resources only to
increase draw count.

7. Treating Spiritomb as an opening consistency Pokémon.

Correction: bench it only when exact public damage makes Raging Curse relevant.

8. Forgetting that losing Roserade changes all damage math.

Correction: recalculate before selecting the attack or gust target.

9. Filling all five bench slots before the opponent's plan is visible.

Correction: keep one slot open unless every role is named.

10. Planning around a prized or discarded evolution piece.

Correction: prize-check on the first search and count visible zones each turn.

11. Letting the opponent bypass 400 HP through one-Prize bench targets.

Correction: map the opponent's easiest six prizes before placing a Basic.

12. Adding narrow techs that reduce the first-Gabite rate.

Correction: demand matchup evidence strong enough to justify lost consistency.


DECISION CHECKLISTS

Before setup

- How many Garchomp attacks are required?
- How many Gible can I establish?
- How many Roserade does the first threshold need?
- Which bench slot must remain open?
- Is Spiritomb part of a real plan or merely available?

Before Champion's Call

- Have I used every Gabite Ability this turn?
- Which exact next action does the searched Pokémon enable?
- Do I need another Gabite before Garchomp?
- Does Roserade cross a real damage threshold?
- Can I preserve a Gabite after evolving the attacker?

Before Power Weight

- Which target is threatened?
- Does 70 HP change exact public damage?
- What Energy, Ability, or threshold survives?
- Is another Tool required?
- Can the opponent remove or ignore the Tool?

Before Corkscrew Dive

- Does 100 plus modifiers take the needed knockout?
- Which safe cards should be played before drawing?
- What must remain in hand?
- Does the refill repair disruption or just add volume?
- Is preserving Energy worth more than extra damage?

Before Draconic Buster

- Exact damage after all current modifiers?
- Exact prizes gained?
- Energy discarded?
- Next funded attacker?
- Recovery route?
- Would Corkscrew Dive produce the same prize result?

Before benching Roselia

- Which damage threshold needs it?
- Can it evolve in time?
- Can the opponent gust it?
- Does it displace the second Garchomp line?
- Is existing Roserade enough?

Before benching Spiritomb

- Exact total damage counters on Benched Cynthia's Pokémon?
- Is Raging Curse legal and relevant next turn?
- Can the opponent remove Spiritomb first?
- Does it improve the prize exchange?
- Does it consume the recovery slot?

Before ending the turn

- Name the opponent's best reply.
- Name the next attacker.
- Count current Roserade and exact attack damage.
- Count Energy required after a knockout.
- Count the opponent's easiest remaining prizes.
- Confirm that the final Gabite Ability was used.

SHORT APPENDIX: TRAINING-HEURISTIC BOUNDARY

This human guide is intentionally broader than the staged machine contract.

The staged causal contract may label only decisions derived from the exact
current legal stage, exact visible board/discard/log history, and current
public counters. It can conservatively prefer:

- Cynthia's Gible in supported opening or Basic-search stages while the
  visible board lacks a resilient attacker line;
- Cynthia's Gabite evolution and Champion's Call targets that fill an exact
  visible evolution gap;
- Cynthia's Roserade evolution when its +30 creates an exact immediate attack
  threshold;
- Draconic Buster when its exact current damage produces a knockout and the
  action is legal;
- Corkscrew Dive when it produces the same prizes without the Energy discard;
- Spiritomb only when exact current Benched damage makes Raging Curse
  calculable.

It must mask Power Weight destination, Energy placement, future prize mapping,
matchup-specific techs, recovery, and attack choices whenever the necessary
facts are unavailable or the preference margin is ambiguous.

The guide is auxiliary only. It never overrides the flat or fused policy,
never authorizes runtime selection, and never consumes hidden opponent state
or future outcomes. All legal combinations in a supported decision stage must
be scored; one unresolved option masks the entire stage.
