TEAM ROCKET'S MEWTWO EX
PRACTICAL PILOT GUIDE FOR EXPERT REVIEW

Specialist ID: rockets-mewtwo
Guide version: rockets-mewtwo-north-star-v1
Prepared: 2026-07-26
Audience: world-champion and equivalent Pokémon TCG subject-matter experts
Status: research pilot; not yet expert-approved


HOW TO READ THIS PILOT

This is a practical guide to piloting the current Team Rocket's Mewtwo ex /
Team Rocket's Spidops archetype. It is not a claim that one 60-card list is
universally correct.

The concrete reference list is Juho Kallama's seventh-place list from NAIC
2026. It plays a 4-4 Team Rocket's Tarountula/Spidops line, two Team Rocket's
Mewtwo ex, two Team Rocket's Mimikyu, two Team Rocket's Articuno, one Lillie's
Clefairy ex, a dense Team Rocket Supporter package, Maximum Belt, and eleven
Energy. Lists change, so confirm every card against the registered list and
event format.

Labels used below:

SOURCE: directly supported by a source listed at the end.

INFERENCE: strategic reasoning from sourced card text, current successful
lists, or public results. Experts should challenge these statements.

LIST CHECK: advice that applies only if the registered list contains the card.

SME CHECK: an intentionally unresolved question for expert review.

Use current printed card text and current rulings for exact costs, legality,
damage, and timing. This guide paraphrases mechanics; it does not replace the
cards or rulebook.


QUICK START: THE DECK IN ONE MINUTE

Team Rocket's Mewtwo is a board-building and prize-trading deck, not simply a
deck that rushes a 280-HP two-Prize attacker.

SOURCE: Mewtwo's Power Saver prevents it from attacking unless at least four
Team Rocket's Pokémon are in play. Mewtwo counts itself. Erasure Ball costs
two Psychic and one Colorless Energy, starts at 160 damage, and may discard up
to two Energy from Benched Pokémon for 60 more damage per card.

SOURCE: Team Rocket's Spidops is the deck's renewable single-Prize attacker
and Energy engine. Charging Up attaches one Basic Energy from the discard to
that Spidops once per turn. Rocket Rush does 30 damage for each Team Rocket's
Pokémon in play. With a full six-Pokémon Team Rocket board, that is 180 damage
before Weakness, Resistance, Tools, or other modifiers.

The default plan is:

1. Establish four or more Team Rocket's Pokémon immediately, with multiple
   Tarountula whenever possible.
2. Evolve Tarountula into Spidops and use Charging Up every turn that a useful
   Basic Energy is available in the discard.
3. Trade with Spidops in matchups where a stream of single-Prize attackers
   forces the opponent to take five or six knockouts.
4. Build Mewtwo behind that exchange. Use it when 160, 220, or 280 damage
   changes the prize race enough to justify exposing a two-Prize Pokémon and
   spending Benched Energy.
5. Keep Articuno in play when its Repelling Veil matters. It prevents effects
   of opposing attacks done to Basic Team Rocket's Pokémon; it does not
   prevent damage.
6. Use Mimikyu as a free-retreat pivot and, against a Tera Active, as a
   possible attacker through Gemstone Mimicry.
7. Bench Lillie's Clefairy only for a specific Dragon-Weakness or attack line.
   A non-Team-Rocket Pokémon turns Ariana's enhanced draw-to-eight into
   draw-to-five.
8. Sequence Team Rocket Supporters before using Team Rocket's Factory's
   optional draw-two effect.

The deck rewards exact arithmetic and restraint. The pilot must count Team
Rocket Pokémon, Energy, available attacks, prizes, and bench slots before
committing to a line.


STRATEGIC IDENTITY

SOURCE: Kallama's NAIC Top-8 list uses eight Spidops-line cards and only two
Mewtwo. That construction is strong evidence that Spidops is not incidental:
it is the repeatable engine and often the first attacker.

SOURCE: A current public strategy discussion independently recommends
streaming Spidops in most matchups, using Spidops's Energy acceleration to
support Mewtwo, making the opponent take five or six knockouts, and treating
Mimikyu as both a narrow attacker and free-retreat pivot. Because this is
community testimony rather than controlled analysis, it is supporting
evidence, not authority.

SOURCE: Limitless reports five Regional Top-8 finishes and one International
Top-8 finish for the archetype in the TEF-CRI format. At Indianapolis in May
2026, the aggregate was 200 wins, 249 losses, and 75 ties over 524 recorded
results. That 42.94% aggregate warns against describing the deck as
universally favored even though it has elite finishes.

INFERENCE: Think of the deck as a single-Prize tempo shell with a two-Prize
threshold breaker. Spidops asks the opponent to keep taking modest-value
knockouts. Mewtwo appears when the board and Energy make its larger hit worth
the two-Prize liability. The strongest turns often combine several small
advantages:

- a Team Rocket Supporter selected by Transceiver;
- Factory draw after that Supporter;
- one or more Charging Up activations;
- a free-retreat Mimikyu pivot or Giovanni switch;
- an exact Spidops or Mewtwo damage threshold; and
- a protected Basic bench under Articuno.

Before playing the first search card, answer:

- Does this game want a Spidops-first or Mewtwo-first prize map?
- How many single-Prize knockouts can the opponent be forced to take?
- Which opposing attacker requires Mewtwo's 220/280 ceiling?
- Is Articuno's effect protection relevant to the opposing attack text?
- Is Clefairy worth turning off Ariana's draw-to-eight?
- Which Energy can be spent by Erasure Ball without breaking the next attack?


CARD ROLES AND ENGINE

Team Rocket's Tarountula and Spidops

SOURCE: The current NAIC list plays four of each. Spidops has 130 HP. Charging
Up retrieves a Basic Energy from the discard to itself. Rocket Rush costs one
Grass and one Colorless and deals 30 damage per Team Rocket's Pokémon in play.

Practical role:

- Tarountula is both a future attacker and a Team Rocket body for Power Saver
  and Rocket Rush.
- Spidops is a single-Prize attacker.
- Each Spidops is a renewable Benched Energy source for Erasure Ball.
- Multiple Spidops create redundant attackers and multiple once-per-turn
  Charging Up abilities.

INFERENCE: Filling the bench is functional here. Every additional Team
Rocket's Pokémon can increase Rocket Rush by 30 until the board is full, and
the fourth Team Rocket body unlocks Mewtwo. However, a full bench is not an
excuse to bench randomly. Preserve room for the attacker, the next Spidops,
Articuno when relevant, and Mimikyu as a pivot. Clefairy consumes a slot
without counting for Rocket Rush or Power Saver.

Do not evolve every Tarountula automatically. If the board already has the
Spidops needed for the current attack and an opponent can gust a Stage 1,
keeping a second Basic may diversify the board. Conversely, evolve when the
new Charging Up activation, extra attacker, or 130-HP body matters now.

Team Rocket's Mewtwo ex

SOURCE: Power Saver requires four Team Rocket's Pokémon in play. Erasure Ball
does 160, 220, or 280 depending on whether zero, one, or two Benched Energy are
discarded.

Practical role:

- a durable 280-HP attacker;
- a 220/280 damage ceiling unavailable to an unmodified Spidops;
- a way to convert Energy stored on Benched Pokémon into immediate damage;
- a Fighting-resistant attacker in matchups where that changes the exchange.

INFERENCE: Do not assume that 280 is always correct. Choose the smallest
Energy discard that takes the intended prizes:

- zero discarded: 160;
- one discarded: 220;
- two discarded: 280;
- Maximum Belt against an opposing Pokémon ex: 210, 270, or 330 before
  Weakness and Resistance.

An unnecessary second discard is not "free" merely because Spidops can recover
Basic Energy later. It costs a future Charging Up activation and can break the
next attack. Conversely, refusing to discard Energy when 220 or 280 takes a
critical knockout wastes Mewtwo's reason to be in the deck.

Team Rocket's Articuno

SOURCE: Repelling Veil prevents all effects of opposing attacks done to Basic
Team Rocket's Pokémon. Existing effects remain, and damage is explicitly not
an effect. Articuno's Dark Frost does 60, or 120 if it has Team Rocket's
Energy attached.

Practical role:

- protects Basic Team Rocket Pokémon from attack effects such as damage
  counter placement or other non-damage effects when the exact opposing text
  falls under Repelling Veil;
- supplies another Team Rocket body;
- offers an emergency 120-damage attack in lists with compatible Energy.

INFERENCE: Do not call Articuno a generic Bench Barrier. It protects Basic
Team Rocket's Pokémon from effects of attacks; it does not prevent ordinary
damage, does not protect non-Team-Rocket Pokémon, and does not protect
Spidops, which is a Stage 1. Confirm the exact interaction before relying on
it.

Team Rocket's Mimikyu

SOURCE: Mimikyu has free retreat. Gemstone Mimicry can use one attack of the
opponent's Active Tera Pokémon for one Psychic and one Colorless Energy.

Practical role:

- free-retreat pivot;
- Team Rocket body for Power Saver and Rocket Rush;
- searchable switch partner for Team Rocket's Giovanni;
- matchup-specific attacker against a Tera Active when the copied attack is
  legal and valuable.

INFERENCE: Its most common value can be positional rather than offensive. A
free-retreat pivot lets the pilot choose the attacker after drawing, using
Charging Up, or resolving Giovanni. Do not attach two Energy to Mimikyu merely
because Gemstone Mimicry exists; inspect the opponent's current Active attack,
its costs and effects, and the exact damage before committing.

Lillie's Clefairy ex

SOURCE: Fairy Zone makes the Weakness of opposing Dragon Pokémon Psychic.
Full Moon Rondo does 20 plus 20 for every Benched Pokémon on both sides.

LIST CHECK: The NAIC Top-8 list plays one Clefairy and one basic Psychic
Energy.

INFERENCE: Clefairy is a precision card, not a default bench. It can create
Psychic-Weakness attacks into Dragon Pokémon and can exploit crowded benches,
but it is a two-Prize non-Team-Rocket Pokémon. While it remains in play:

- it does not count toward Mewtwo's four;
- it does not add 30 to Rocket Rush;
- Ariana draws only to five rather than eight;
- Giovanni cannot use it as the pilot's Team Rocket switch target.

Bench it only after naming the exact Dragon or attack threshold it enables.
If that threshold never arrives, the opportunity cost may exceed its value.

Team Rocket Supporters, Transceiver, and Factory

SOURCE: Team Rocket's Proton searches up to three Basic Team Rocket's Pokémon.
It is specifically legal on the pilot's first turn when going first.

SOURCE: Team Rocket's Ariana draws to five, or to eight if every Pokémon in
play is a Team Rocket's Pokémon.

SOURCE: Team Rocket's Giovanni switches the pilot's Active Team Rocket's
Pokémon with a Benched Team Rocket's Pokémon, then switches an opposing
Benched Pokémon Active.

SOURCE: Team Rocket's Petrel searches any Trainer.

SOURCE: Team Rocket's Transceiver searches a Team Rocket Supporter.

SOURCE: Team Rocket's Factory lets a player draw two once during their turn
after playing a Team Rocket Supporter from hand that turn.

These cards turn Transceiver into a decision point:

- Proton builds the opening board.
- Ariana repairs a low hand, especially while the board is all Team Rocket.
- Giovanni combines a switch and gust in one Supporter.
- Petrel becomes access to a required Trainer, including a Tool, Stadium,
  Item, or another situation-specific Trainer in the registered list.

INFERENCE: Decide the Supporter before consuming flexible cards. Then play the
chosen Team Rocket Supporter and remember the Factory draw. Factory is
optional, so pause if drawing would matter for deck-out or another rare
constraint.


SETUP, PRIZE CHECKING, AND BENCH MAPPING

Opening board

A robust generic opening aims for:

- at least two Tarountula;
- four total Team Rocket's Pokémon as soon as practical;
- Mewtwo only when it belongs in the intended prize map;
- Articuno when exact attack-effect protection matters;
- Mimikyu when a pivot or Tera-copy line matters; and
- one bench slot held until the matchup plan is clear.

SOURCE: Current community sequencing advice recommends filling the bench
quickly, going first, using Proton on the first turn whenever possible, and
keeping Factory in play for draw. This aligns with Proton's exact card text and
the deck's four-body and Rocket Rush requirements, but the recommendation has
not been validated by controlled first/second data.

Prize checking

Use the first deck search to determine:

- how many Mewtwo, Tarountula, and Spidops remain;
- whether both Articuno and both Mimikyu are available;
- whether Clefairy and the basic Psychic Energy are available;
- whether Maximum Belt is available;
- how many Team Rocket's Energy and basic Grass Energy remain;
- whether Energy Switch and recovery cards are available; and
- which Supporter routes are live through Transceiver.

Then rebuild the plan. If one Mewtwo is prized, avoid exposing the other
without purpose. If Spidops are constrained, preserve the remaining Stage 1s
and recovery. If Maximum Belt is prized, do not plan around 330 from Erasure
Ball.

Bench map

Every occupied slot should have a declared job:

- present Spidops attacker;
- replacement Tarountula/Spidops;
- Mewtwo threshold attacker;
- Articuno protection;
- Mimikyu pivot/copier; or
- Clefairy Dragon solution.

INFERENCE: The all-Team-Rocket board is the cleanest default because it
maximizes Rocket Rush and Ariana. Clefairy should displace a Team Rocket body
only when its exact contribution is larger.


GOING FIRST AND GOING SECOND

Going first

SOURCE: Proton explicitly bypasses the usual first-turn Supporter restriction
when the pilot goes first. Current community advice therefore recommends
going first and using Proton immediately.

Priorities:

1. Find Proton directly or through Transceiver.
2. Search the Basic Team Rocket Pokémon needed for four bodies and redundant
   Tarountula.
3. Attach Energy to the line least likely to strand it.
4. Establish Factory if doing so is legal and useful.
5. On turn two, evolve into Spidops, use Charging Up if a Basic Energy is in
   the discard, and attack if possible.

Going second

Going second permits an attack on the first turn but does not provide Proton's
exclusive first-turn exception because ordinary Supporters are already legal.
The list's actual turn-one attack depends on the opening Pokémon, Energy, and
current legal actions.

Priorities:

1. Build four Team Rocket bodies if Mewtwo is the planned attacker.
2. Use Proton if board development is worth more than Ariana, Giovanni, or
   another Supporter.
3. Take a useful attack only if it improves the prize map; do not damage the
   setup merely to attack.
4. Prepare two Tarountula so that turn-two evolution is resilient.

SME CHECK: No authoritative, controlled first-versus-second performance study
for the current NAIC-style list was found. "Go first" is a strong mechanical
default, not a universal matchup law.


SEQUENCING A TURN

Use this order unless exact information requires a deviation:

1. Count prizes remaining on both sides.
2. Identify the intended attacker and exact knockout threshold.
3. Count Team Rocket's Pokémon in play. Confirm Power Saver and Rocket Rush.
4. Check every Spidops. Use each relevant Charging Up before an action could
   remove, evolve away from, or strand that Spidops.
5. Use deterministic search before random draw when prize knowledge matters.
6. Choose the Supporter. If using Transceiver, know whether it is fetching
   Proton, Ariana, Giovanni, or Petrel before playing it.
7. Play the Team Rocket Supporter.
8. Use Factory's draw-two after the Supporter when beneficial.
9. Attach the manual Energy according to the current and replacement attack.
10. Resolve switching and gust. Giovanni requires both sides of the switch.
11. Recalculate damage after the final board, Tool, Weakness, Resistance, and
    Energy state.
12. If using Erasure Ball, discard only the number of Benched Energy required
    for the selected prize outcome.
13. Before attacking, verify the next attacker and remaining Energy.

The most expensive sequencing mistakes are playing Ariana before thinning,
forgetting Factory, evolving without using a relevant Charging Up, benching
Clefairy before Ariana, or spending two Energy for 280 when 220 already takes
the same prizes.


ATTACK AND RESOURCE PLANNING

Spidops-first plan

Choose Spidops when:

- its exact Rocket Rush damage takes the intended knockout;
- forcing another single-Prize exchange improves the opponent's required
  knockout count;
- Mewtwo's extra damage is unnecessary;
- a Darkness-Weak opponent makes the Grass attack efficient; or
- Mewtwo must remain protected while Energy accumulates.

Keep the bench populated with Team Rocket Pokémon. Prepare the next
Tarountula/Spidops before the current one falls. If a Spidops survives, its
Charging Up can continue supplying Energy or repair its own attack cost.

Mewtwo plan

Choose Mewtwo when:

- 160, 220, or 280 reaches a prize threshold Spidops cannot;
- Mewtwo's 280 HP or Fighting Resistance changes the exchange;
- the game can absorb the two-Prize liability;
- enough Benched Energy exists without destroying the following turn; and
- Power Saver remains satisfied after all switches or knockouts.

Maximum Belt raises damage against an opposing Pokémon ex by 50. The key
thresholds become 210/270/330 before Weakness and Resistance. Do not attach it
merely because Mewtwo is Active; attach it when the extra 50 changes the
current prize result or when the registered plan has a specific protected
future target.

Energy ledger

At the start of every turn, record:

- basic Grass in hand, play, and discard;
- basic Psychic in hand, play, and discard;
- Team Rocket's Energy in visible zones;
- Energy attached to every Benched Pokémon;
- which Spidops have used Charging Up;
- the current Mewtwo attack cost; and
- the Energy that Erasure Ball would consume.

INFERENCE: Basic Energy on Spidops is the most naturally renewable fuel, but
recovery still costs future abilities and is not guaranteed after a knockout
or board disruption. Team Rocket's Energy may enable other attacks and is not
retrieved by Charging Up because it is not Basic Energy. Preserve it when a
specific attacker requires it.

Recovery

LIST CHECK: Kallama's NAIC list uses three Night Stretcher. Other successful
lists may use Sacred Ash or different recovery counts.

Prioritize recovery by the board's actual bottleneck:

1. the only Basic or Stage 1 that restores the attacker stream;
2. the Energy that creates the current attack;
3. Mewtwo when its damage tier is required;
4. Articuno or Mimikyu only when their exact role is live.

Do not recover a high-profile Pokémon simply because it was knocked out.
Recover the card that changes the present prize race.


MATCHUP PLANNING

The public matchup numbers below are descriptive, not prescriptions. They are
from one Indianapolis event and include ties; small rows are noisy.

Dragapult variants

SOURCE: Indianapolis reported 47.87% into straight Dragapult over 94 records,
51.85% into Dragapult/Dusknoir over 36, and 44.93% into
Dragapult/Blaziken over 23.

Plan:

- identify whether Articuno's Repelling Veil prevents the exact attack effect
  aimed at Basic Team Rocket Pokémon;
- keep in mind that Articuno does not protect Spidops and does not prevent
  direct damage;
- preserve multiple Tarountula rather than one fragile line;
- consider Mimikyu only when the opposing Active is Tera and its copied attack
  is legal and useful;
- bench Clefairy only if Fairy Zone creates an exact Psychic-Weakness knockout
  against a Dragon Pokémon.

Mega Lucario and Fighting decks

SOURCE: Indianapolis reported 65.22% over 23 results into Lucario/Hariyama and
64.29% over 14 into Mega Lucario. Mewtwo resists Fighting according to its
card text.

Plan:

- calculate Mewtwo's Resistance into the exact opposing damage;
- use Mewtwo when its survival and return knockout justify two-Prize exposure;
- retain the Spidops route so the opponent cannot map only two-Prize
  knockouts; and
- do not infer that the matchup is automatically won from a small event
  sample.

Darkness and Rocket mirrors

Mewtwo is Weak to Darkness. Spidops is Grass and can be the safer primary
attacker into Darkness attackers when damage permits. In the mirror, count
which player can maintain more Spidops, renewable Energy, and single-Prize
attacks.

SOURCE: Indianapolis's Rocket's Mewtwo mirror row was 10-10-4. This is too
small and too tie-heavy to support a deterministic matchup script.

High-HP, low-Energy attackers

SOURCE: Current strategy discussion identifies large, efficient attackers as
a core problem because they can remove Mewtwo while asking for little Energy.
TCGplayer similarly notes that Mewtwo can be difficult to repower after a
one-hit knockout and that Maximum Belt is important for non-Weak targets.

Plan:

- prize-map before benching Mewtwo;
- use Maximum Belt only at an exact ex knockout threshold;
- force single-Prize exchanges with Spidops where possible;
- preserve Energy Switch and recovery for rebuilding;
- accept that some boards cannot be solved by raw Mewtwo damage alone.

Alakazam

SOURCE: The Indianapolis aggregate showed 72.22% over 12 results into
Alakazam/Dudunsparce. Community discussion also describes Articuno as relevant
to attack effects on Basic Team Rocket Pokémon.

Plan:

- verify the exact Alakazam attack and Articuno ruling rather than relying on
  the matchup label;
- maintain an all-Team-Rocket board so Ariana and Rocket Rush remain strong;
- avoid overcommitting Mewtwo if Spidops can carry the prize exchange.

Mega Starmie and other fast efficient attackers

Evidence is conflicting and sparse. Indianapolis's pure Mega Starmie row was
3-2 over five games, while Starmie/Froslass was 1-8 over nine. Current
community reports call Starmie difficult. Treat the matchup as unresolved,
not as a known percentage.

Plan:

- identify whether Spidops can trade before the opponent establishes repeated
  knockouts;
- preserve Maximum Belt for an exact ex threshold;
- minimize unnecessary two-Prize liabilities;
- do not tune a fixed line from tiny samples.


RECOVERY WHEN THE IDEAL SETUP FAILS

Fewer than four Team Rocket Pokémon

Mewtwo cannot attack. Use Proton, Transceiver-to-Proton, Pokémon search, or
recovery to restore the fourth body. If that cannot happen this turn, pivot to
a legal Spidops, Mimikyu, or Articuno attack rather than attaching to a locked
Mewtwo without a plan.

No Spidops on turn two

Protect Tarountula, use Ariana or another draw route, and avoid discarding the
remaining evolution cards. A Mewtwo attack can bridge only if four Team Rocket
bodies and its Energy cost are already satisfied.

No Energy in the discard

Charging Up has no target. Do not manufacture a discard unless the same action
also advances the turn. Ultra Ball, Prism Tower, or another exact cost can
place a Basic Energy in the discard, but the discarded cards and resulting
search/draw must justify the action.

Mewtwo knocked out after Erasure Ball

Recount Energy before choosing the replacement. Multiple Spidops may recover
multiple Basic Energy, but each ability belongs to its own Spidops. Use Energy
Switch only when the exact transfer restores the needed attack. If the large
attack cannot be rebuilt, revert to a single-Prize Spidops route.

Ariana reduced to five

Check whether Clefairy or another non-Team-Rocket Pokémon is in play. Do not
assume the enhanced draw-to-eight condition. Use the actual printed condition
and current board.

Factory replaced

The deck has no guaranteed on-board draw engine. Conserve Transceiver and
draw Supporters, use deterministic search before consuming random draw, and
do not discard the last recovery or Supporter access without a reason.


COMMON MISTAKES

1. Treating Mewtwo as the only attacker. The current elite list's 4-4 Spidops
   line indicates otherwise.
2. Forgetting that Mewtwo counts itself toward Power Saver.
3. Forgetting that Clefairy does not count toward Power Saver or Rocket Rush.
4. Benching Clefairy early and accidentally reducing Ariana from eight to
   five.
5. Assuming Articuno prevents damage. It prevents effects of attacks to Basic
   Team Rocket Pokémon, not damage.
6. Assuming Articuno protects Spidops. Spidops is a Stage 1.
7. Discarding two Benched Energy with Erasure Ball when one or zero takes the
   same prizes.
8. Discarding Team Rocket's Energy as if Charging Up could recover it.
9. Missing one or more Charging Up activations.
10. Playing Transceiver without deciding which Supporter the turn needs.
11. Playing a Team Rocket Supporter and forgetting Factory's draw-two.
12. Using Giovanni as simple gust while forgetting that the pilot must also
    switch between Team Rocket Pokémon.
13. Filling the bench without reserving the correct attacker or pivot slot.
14. Treating Mimikyu's copied attack as automatically legal or useful.
15. Taking matchup percentages from very small samples as certainty.


DECISION CHECKLISTS

Opening

- Did I prize-check both Mewtwo, the Spidops line, Articuno, Mimikyu,
  Clefairy, Maximum Belt, and Energy?
- Can I establish four Team Rocket Pokémon?
- Can I establish two Tarountula?
- Is Proton available directly or through Transceiver?
- Does Articuno prevent a specific opposing attack effect?
- Does Clefairy enable a specific Dragon knockout, or should it stay off the
  bench?

Before search or draw

- What exact card category is missing?
- Can deterministic search answer the prize check first?
- Which Team Rocket Supporter does Transceiver need to become?
- Should I thin the hand before Ariana?
- Have I preserved Factory's draw condition?

Before evolving

- Have I used the relevant Charging Up ability?
- Does another Spidops create a current attacker or Energy source?
- Do I still have a replacement Tarountula?

Before attaching Energy

- Which Pokémon attacks this turn?
- Which attacks next turn if the Active is knocked out?
- Is the Energy Basic and therefore recoverable by Charging Up?
- Is Team Rocket's Energy required for Articuno or another registered attack?
- Will Energy Switch be needed?

Before Giovanni

- Is my current Active a Team Rocket Pokémon?
- Which Benched Team Rocket Pokémon becomes Active?
- Which opposing Benched Pokémon becomes Active?
- Does the final attacker take exact prizes after both switches?
- Will Power Saver remain satisfied?

Before attacking

- How many Team Rocket Pokémon are in play?
- What is Rocket Rush's exact damage?
- Is Power Saver satisfied?
- What are Erasure Ball's exact 160/220/280 outcomes?
- Does Maximum Belt apply to the opposing Active?
- What do Weakness and Resistance do?
- What is the smallest Energy discard that takes the same prizes?
- What is the next attacker?

End of turn

- Did every usable Spidops use Charging Up?
- Did I use Factory after a Team Rocket Supporter?
- Is a free-retreat Mimikyu available for the next turn?
- How many knockouts must the opponent still take?
- Did I expose an unnecessary two-Prize target?

SHORT TRAINING-HEURISTIC APPENDIX

The companion machine contract extracts only sparse preferences that can be
computed from the exact current legal options and causally available state.
It does not encode hidden deck contents, future draws, opponent intent, or
game outcomes.

Examples include:

- prefer exact Proton combinations that establish four Team Rocket bodies and
  redundant visible Tarountula;
- prefer the smallest Erasure Ball Energy discard that takes the same current
  prizes;
- prefer Giovanni switch pairs only when exact current damage proves a better
  prize result;
- prefer Maximum Belt only when its current +50 crosses an exact knockout
  threshold;
- prefer Ariana from a Transceiver prompt only when the exact current board
  grants a materially larger draw;
- prefer Mimikyu's copied attack only when the engine can resolve its current
  effect and prize result exactly.

Every supplied legal combination in a stage must be scored. If any option,
prompt origin, damage result, or identity is unresolved, the entire stage is
masked. The guide is an auxiliary objective, has no runtime authority, and
cannot override the flat or fused policy.
