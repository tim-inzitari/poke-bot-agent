ARCHALUDON EX / DUDUNSPARCE
PRACTICAL PILOT GUIDE FOR EXPERT REVIEW

Specialist ID: archaludon-ex
Guide version: archaludon-ex-north-star-v1
Prepared: 2026-07-28
Audience: world-champion and equivalent Pokémon TCG subject-matter experts
Status: research pilot; not yet expert-approved


HOW TO READ THIS PILOT

This guide covers the current Archaludon ex archetype built around Duraludon,
Archaludon ex, Basic Metal Energy, and a Dudunsparce draw engine. It does not
claim that every deck containing Archaludon ex belongs to this specialist.

The concrete reference is Brian Peltz's NAIC 2026 list. It is the most recent
large-event Masters list located during review and contains the current
Perfect Order and Chaos Rising support package. Antoni M.'s fifth-place
Juniors list from the June 2026 Turin Special Event is an important alternate:
it uses a 4-4 Dudunsparce line and one single-Prize Archaludon. The alternate
is particularly relevant to anti-Pokémon-ex walls.

Labels used below:

SOURCE: a printed card, published deck list, official article, or public
results page directly supports the statement.

INFERENCE: practical play reasoning derived from public card text and lists.
It is proposed for expert review, not an official ruling or causal result.

LIST CHECK: the advice applies only if the registered sixty cards include the
named card.

SME CHECK: a deliberately exposed question for expert correction.

Card text and event rulings control exact legality. This guide paraphrases
cards and supplies arithmetic; it does not replace either.


EXECUTIVE IDENTITY

SOURCE: Archaludon ex is a 300-HP Stage 1. When it evolves from hand, Assemble
Alloy may attach up to two Basic Metal Energy from the discard pile to Metal
Pokémon in any distribution. Metal Defender costs three Metal, deals 220, and
removes that Archaludon ex's Weakness during the opponent's next turn.

SOURCE: Duraludon SCR 106 is a 130-HP Basic with Hammer In for 30 and Raging
Hammer for 80 plus 10 for each damage counter on itself. Relicanth's Memory
Dive lets evolved Pokémon use attacks from their previous Evolutions while
still paying the printed costs. A damaged Archaludon ex can therefore use
Duraludon's Raging Hammer.

SOURCE: Dudunsparce's Run Away Draw draws three, then shuffles Dudunsparce and
all attached cards into the deck if cards were drawn. This supplies repeated
draw, removes a fragile single-Prize body from play, and reopens a bench slot.

INFERENCE: The deck is a resource-conversion tank deck, not simply a 220-damage
linear attacker. It repeatedly converts:

- Metal in hand into Metal in the discard through Ultra Ball or Prism Tower;
- an unevolved Duraludon into acceleration through Assemble Alloy;
- a Dunsparce evolution into three new cards and a reopened bench slot;
- damage on an Archaludon into Raging Hammer damage through Relicanth;
- a damaged or expendable board slot into a reset through Scoop Up Cyclone;
- and the opponent's prize progress into disruption through Special Red Card.

The normal plan is to make an Archaludon ex attack immediately on the turn it
evolves, force an awkward two-hit exchange against 300 HP, and have a second
Duraludon ready to inherit discarded Energy after a knockout. The strongest
turns do several jobs at once: draw with Dudunsparce, seed exactly enough
Metal into the discard, evolve and accelerate, disrupt or gust, then take a
prize while preserving the next attacker.

The deck becomes brittle when it treats every optional Pokémon as free.
Meowth ex and Fezandipiti ex each expose two prizes. Mega Mawile ex exposes
three. Relicanth and Fan Rotom expose easy single prizes. The five bench slots
must first protect the attack stream and Dudunsparce engine.


CURRENT PUBLIC EVIDENCE

SOURCE: The reviewed POR Standard 2026 Play Limitless aggregate recorded 135
wins, 97 losses, and 5 ties across 237 Archaludon Dudunsparce matches, a
reported 56.96% win rate. That page included 19 Dragapult Dusknoir matches,
17 plain Dragapult, 14 Alakazam Dudunsparce, 12 N's Zoroark, 10 Rocket's
Mewtwo, 9 Raging Bolt Ogerpon, 9 Grimmsnarl Froslass, 8 Lucario Hariyama, 7
Crustle, and only 3 Rocket's Spidops matches.

INFERENCE: Those are observational online results, not matchup truth. Lists,
players, event structures, and sample sizes differ. Use the aggregate to
choose testing priorities, never to substitute a percentage for a board-state
decision. In particular, an 8-1 row or a 1-2 row is too small to support a
confident matchup claim.

SOURCE: The official Pokémon strategy article from December 2024 explains the
core tank, discard-and-recover, Relicanth, Raging Hammer, and damaged-attacker
reset principles. Its example list belongs to an older format, so rotated
support cards from that list are historical context rather than current-list
instructions.

QUICK START

If time is short, pilot from these rules:

1. Prefer going first by default. The deck wants to evolve and attack on its
   second turn; going first reaches that evolution turn before the opponent's
   second attack turn.
2. Start Duraludon when practical. Establish a second Duraludon before exposing
   optional support Pokémon.
3. Establish one or two Dunsparce early. Fan Call and Buddy-Buddy Poffin find
   the draw engine, not the 130-HP Metal Duraludon.
4. On the first deck search, inventory the Duraludon line, Basic Metal, Scoop
   Up Cyclone, recovery, and matchup Stadium. Infer prizes only from legal
   searches; never pretend to know face-down Prize identities.
5. Before evolving, place up to two useful Basic Metal in the discard. Do not
   discard Metal merely because Assemble Alloy can recover it.
6. Evolve only after choosing where each accelerated Energy goes. Default to
   a current three-Energy attacker plus progress toward a replacement.
7. Use Metal Defender by default. Choose Raging Hammer only when its exact
   knockout or prize conversion is worth giving up refreshed Weakness removal.
8. Keep a replacement Duraludon in play whenever the opponent can knock out
   the Active. An Archaludon in hand is not a replacement attacker without a
   legal Duraludon and an Energy route.
9. Do not bench Meowth ex, Fezandipiti ex, or Mega Mawile ex speculatively.
   Name the immediate Supporter, draw, attack, or prize-map gain first.
10. Against damage-counter strategies, prioritize Battle Cage but remember
    that it does not prevent ordinary damage to the Bench.
11. Against a Pokémon-ex damage wall such as Crustle DRI, preserve a
    single-Prize attack route. The reference list has Duraludon; variants may
    also register single-Prize Archaludon.
12. Before Scoop Up Cyclone, identify the new Active, the standing Duraludon,
    and how returned Energy will re-enter play. Returned Energy goes to hand,
    not to the discard.


SETUP AND OPENING BOARD

INFERENCE: The default opening board is:

- one Duraludon Active or ready to become Active;
- a second Duraludon as the replacement lane;
- one or two Dunsparce;
- and no optional support liability without a named job.

The priority is resilience, not maximum occupancy. Two Duraludon protect
against gust and let successive Archaludon evolutions accelerate. Two
Dunsparce protect the draw engine and allow consecutive Run Away Draw turns.
The fifth bench slot is contested by Relicanth, Fan Rotom, Meowth ex,
Fezandipiti ex, Mega Mawile ex, or a newly recycled Dunsparce.

Opening Active order

INFERENCE:

1. Duraludon is the default. It can evolve, attack, and become a damaged
   Raging Hammer source later.
2. Dunsparce is an acceptable single-Prize pivot. Going second, Trading Places
   can move it to the Bench after setup if paying one Colorless is useful.
3. Fan Rotom is functional because Fan Call matters only on the first turn,
   but it can become a stranded easy prize.
4. Relicanth and the Rule Box support Pokémon are generally poor voluntary
   openers. Mega Mawile is especially costly because a Mega Evolution
   Pokémon ex yields three prizes when Knocked Out.

First-turn search order

SOURCE: Fan Rotom's Fan Call can be used only on the first turn and finds up
to three Colorless Pokémon with 100 HP or less. Buddy-Buddy Poffin places up
to two Basic Pokémon with 70 HP or less directly onto the Bench. Poké Pad
finds a Pokémon without a Rule Box.

INFERENCE:

1. Use Fan Call before the first turn expires. It can find Dunsparce and
   another Fan Rotom, but it cannot find the 130-HP Metal Duraludon.
2. Use Poffin only after counting bench slots; it can establish Dunsparce
   directly.
3. Use Poké Pad to fill the role the other searches miss. In the reference
   list that often means Duraludon, Dudunsparce, Relicanth, or Fan Rotom.
   Poké Pad cannot find Archaludon ex, Meowth ex, Fezandipiti ex, or Mega
   Mawile ex because those Pokémon have Rule Boxes.
4. Preserve Ultra Ball when its two-card discard is the cleanest route to
   both Archaludon ex and Assemble Alloy fuel.

Prize check

INFERENCE: During the first legal deck search, count rather than merely look.
Confirm how many of these remain in deck:

- Duraludon and Archaludon ex;
- Dunsparce and Dudunsparce;
- Basic Metal Energy;
- Night Stretcher;
- the one-of Scoop Up Cyclone;
- the relevant Stadium;
- Relicanth and Mega Mawile if the matchup may need them;
- and the one-of Supporters that Meowth can fetch.

Human pilots may infer which cards are prized from the registered list and
the legally viewed deck. The heuristic teacher must not use face-down Prize
identities; that distinction is intentional.


GOING FIRST

INFERENCE: Going first is the default because Duraludon cannot evolve on the
first turn it enters play. The desired second turn is evolve, Assemble Alloy,
and Metal Defender. Going first also denies the opponent a full extra turn to
attack the unevolved 130-HP line before that evolution.

First turn goals, in order:

1. Put two Duraludon in play when the hand and prizes allow.
2. Establish at least one Dunsparce, preferably two against gust or spread.
3. Use Fan Call now if available.
4. Make the normal attachment where it survives and advances a known attack.
5. Preserve a discard outlet and Archaludon access for the next turn.

Do not over-discard Metal on turn one. If one Metal is manually attached and
two are available to discard next turn, the attack is already funded. Sending
four or five of the deck's ten Metal to discard without a recovery plan makes
later attackers fragile.

On turn two, draw before committing to the exact line. If Dudunsparce is
available, decide whether its three cards can reduce the cost of the turn.
Then establish the necessary discard Metal, evolve, distribute Assemble Alloy,
retreat if needed, and attack. The sequencing section below covers exceptions.


GOING SECOND

INFERENCE: Going second gives access to a first-turn Supporter and attack, but
it does not remove the evolution wait. Choose it only for a matchup-specific
reason or when the registered list's first-turn consistency outweighs the
tempo of evolving first.

The first-turn Supporter is usually Lillie's Determination when the opening
hand needs repair. At exactly six remaining prizes it draws eight after
shuffling the hand, so it is at peak value before either side has taken one of
your prizes. Hilda is useful only when the searched Evolution and Energy have
a next-turn route; an Archaludon in hand does not protect a lone Duraludon
from the opponent's immediate turn.

Available attacks are setup tools, not obligations:

- Duraludon's Hammer In does 30 for one Metal. Take it when the 30 changes a
  later threshold and the Active Duraludon is not being volunteered into an
  easy knockout.
- Dunsparce's Trading Places can switch itself with a Benched Pokémon. Use it
  when the pivot protects a key body or improves the next-turn evolution.
- Passing can be superior when an attack would strand the wrong Active,
  consume the only attachment, or place damage that ruins a planned clean
  Huge Bite target.

The last point is nonlinear but important. Mega Mawile's Huge Bite has a
260-damage base only if the opposing Active has no damage counters before the
attack. A casual Hammer In can reduce that future base to 30.


TURN SEQUENCING

Start-of-turn verbal check

Before playing a card, answer:

1. How many prizes does each player need, and what public prize values are
   available?
2. Which attacker takes the current prize?
3. Which Pokémon attacks after the opponent's most likely knockout?
4. How many Basic Metal are in hand, deck, discard, and visibly attached?
5. How many Assemble Alloy activations remain possible this game?
6. Is Metal Defender's Weakness protection required for the next opponent
   turn?
7. Does Relicanth create a better exact Raging Hammer number?
8. Which bench slot must remain open for Meowth, Fezandipiti, Dunsparce, or a
   replacement Duraludon?
9. Which Stadium matters now: Battle Cage, Prism Tower, or the opponent's?
10. If the Active is removed, what becomes Active and how does it retreat?

Information before destruction

INFERENCE: Use safe current-turn information before irreversible discards.
Run Away Draw and a legal Flip the Script can reveal cards that change the
Ultra Ball discard or Supporter choice. Search effects reveal the remaining
deck and improve prize inference. Exceptions are deliberate:

- If the deck must contain a particular card before Run Away Draw shuffles
  Dudunsparce back, search first.
- If a Dudunsparce currently occupies the only bench slot needed for the
  turn, Run Away Draw first.
- If Lillie's Determination will shuffle the whole hand, consume useful Items,
  abilities, and searches first without discarding resources the new hand
  needs.
- If Judge or Special Red Card is the chosen disruption, complete your own
  searches before changing hands unless a known ordering rule demands
  otherwise.

The acceleration sequence

INFERENCE:

1. Decide the final attacker and Energy allocation.
2. Count already attached Metal.
3. Put only the missing amount of Basic Metal into the discard with Ultra Ball
   or Prism Tower.
4. Search Archaludon ex if needed.
5. Evolve a Duraludon.
6. Resolve Assemble Alloy, attaching zero, one, or two according to the plan.
7. Make the manual attachment to the current or replacement attacker.
8. Retreat or gust only after confirming the attack remains legal.

Assemble Alloy may split two Energy across Metal Pokémon. The default is not
automatically "both to the new Archaludon." If the current attacker already
has enough, one or both Energy can prepare a second Duraludon, another
Archaludon, or Mega Mawile. Conversely, splitting is wrong when it leaves both
attackers short.

Dudunsparce sequencing

SOURCE: Run Away Draw shuffles Dudunsparce and all attached cards into the
deck after drawing.

INFERENCE:

- Avoid attaching Energy to Dudunsparce without a concrete attack or retreat
  reason; Run Away Draw removes that Energy from the board and returns it to
  the deck, not the discard.
- Rebench Dunsparce only after confirming it does not consume the replacement
  Duraludon or matchup-tech slot.
- A second Dunsparce lets the engine alternate, but two Dunsparce are not
  mandatory when prizes, bench pressure, or spread make them liabilities.
- Near deck-out, count before using the ability. Dudunsparce shuffles itself
  back, but a pilot should not rely on an improvised endgame loop without
  verifying every mandatory draw and legal board requirement.

Supporter sequencing

SOURCE: Hilda searches an Evolution Pokémon and an Energy card. Philippe
attaches up to two Basic Metal from the discard to one Metal Pokémon. Black
Belt's Training adds 40 damage to attacks against the opponent's Active
Pokémon ex for that turn.

INFERENCE:

- Lillie's Determination is the broad reset, especially at six prizes.
- Boss's Orders is correct when the gust changes the prize map now, not merely
  because a target is attractive.
- Hilda is an exact two-resource bridge. It is strongest when the Evolution
  and Energy both complete the same turn or preserve the next attacker.
- Philippe is the recovery acceleration when no useful Assemble Alloy remains.
  Unlike Assemble Alloy, both attached Energy go to one Metal Pokémon.
- Black Belt is a threshold card. Name the target and arithmetic before using
  the Supporter for the turn.
- Judge is symmetrical disruption; use your draw/search abilities first when
  doing so does not reveal a new reason to choose another Supporter.


ENERGY AND RESOURCE ACCOUNTING

The reference list has ten Basic Metal. Treat them as a finite system with
five locations: deck, hand, discard, attached, and face-down prizes. Only
publicly established or legally inferred counts belong in the plan.

INFERENCE: Assign each Metal one of four jobs:

1. the current attack;
2. the next attacker;
3. retreat or a specific auxiliary attack;
4. recoverable discard fuel.

Metal in the discard is not wasted, but it is not attached until an actual
Assemble Alloy or Philippe resolves. Metal in hand is not acceleration fuel
until discarded. Metal returned by Scoop Up Cyclone is also in hand, so a
reset Archaludon does not automatically reaccelerate itself.

Discard outlets

SOURCE: Ultra Ball discards two cards as part of its cost and searches a
Pokémon. Prism Tower lets each player once per turn discard two cards from
hand to draw one.

INFERENCE:

- Ultra Ball is the best combined bridge when it discards needed Metal and
  finds Archaludon ex.
- Prism Tower is a resource-negative filter, not free draw. Discarding two to
  draw one costs a net card and gives the opponent access to the Stadium too.
- Do not discard Night Stretcher, Scoop Up Cyclone, or the only matchup Stadium
  merely to reach the aesthetically ideal two-Metal discard.
- Do not use Prism Tower before deciding whether Battle Cage is essential;
  playing one Stadium discards the other Stadium in play.

Recovery

SOURCE: Night Stretcher returns either a Pokémon or a Basic Energy from the
discard to hand. The reference list runs four.

INFERENCE: Name the bottleneck before choosing:

- recover Duraludon when no evolution seed remains;
- recover Archaludon ex when a live Duraludon and discard Energy already exist;
- recover Basic Metal when the manual attachment is the missing attack cost;
- recover Dudunsparce when draw is the bottleneck;
- recover Relicanth only when an exact Raging Hammer line matters;
- recover a Rule Box support Pokémon only when its immediate value justifies
  the exposed prize liability.

Multiple Night Stretchers do not excuse careless discards. They are also how
the deck rebuilds after knockouts, so using all four as early consistency
cards can leave no late attack stream.


ATTACKS AND EXACT THRESHOLDS

Metal Defender

SOURCE: 220 damage for three Metal; the attacking Archaludon ex has no
Weakness during the opponent's next turn.

INFERENCE: This is the default attack even when 220 misses a knockout. The
Weakness-removal clause is part of its value. Against Fire attackers, a turn
of Raging Hammer or another attack does not refresh Metal Defender's
protection. Check whether the extra damage wins now or simply exposes a
two-Prize knockout.

Black Belt's Training changes Metal Defender from 220 to 260 against an
Active Pokémon ex. It does not add damage against a non-ex target. Recheck
Weakness, Resistance, Tools, and other effects after setting the base.

Raging Hammer through Relicanth

SOURCE: Raging Hammer is 80 plus 10 per damage counter on the attacking
Pokémon. Memory Dive allows the evolved Archaludon ex to use it for its
printed three-Energy cost.

Exact examples before other modifiers:

0 damage on Archaludon ex: 80
40 damage: 120
80 damage: 160
120 damage: 200
140 damage: 220
180 damage: 260
220 damage: 300
260 damage: 340

INFERENCE: Raging Hammer is not automatically better once it exceeds 220. It
requires a Relicanth bench slot and gives up the new Metal Defender protection.
Use it for an exact knockout, a game-ending prize, or a prize swing that
outweighs the defensive loss.

Mega Mawile ex

SOURCE: Gobble Down costs two Metal and deals 80 times the number of prizes
you have already taken. Huge Bite costs three Energy and normally has a
260-damage base, but its base becomes 30 if the opposing Active already has
any damage counters.

Gobble Down table:

0 prizes taken: 0
1 prize taken: 80
2 prizes taken: 160
3 prizes taken: 240
4 prizes taken: 320
5 prizes taken: 400

INFERENCE: Mega Mawile is a finisher, not a default opening bench Pokémon.
At three prizes taken, Gobble Down reaches 240; with Black Belt it reaches 280
against an Active ex. At four prizes taken it reaches 320 without a modifier.
Huge Bite plus Black Belt reaches 300 against a clean Active ex, exactly the
printed HP of Archaludon ex. Any prior damage counter collapses Huge Bite's
base to 30, so plan the target before Hammer In, spread, or incidental damage.

Mega Mawile gives up three prizes. Bench it only when the current attack,
next-turn attack, or forced prize map justifies that liability.

Duraludon and auxiliary attacks

SOURCE: Unevolved Duraludon has Hammer In for 30 and Raging Hammer using the
same damage-counter formula. Fezandipiti ex can attack any opposing Pokémon
for 100. Meowth ex's three-Energy Tuck Tail does 60 and returns Meowth and its
attached cards to hand.

INFERENCE:

- Preserve Duraludon as the reference list's non-ex attack route into an
  ex-immune wall.
- Fezandipiti's attack can take an exact Bench prize, but funding three
  Colorless on a two-Prize support Pokémon must not break the Metal stream.
- Tuck Tail can recycle Last-Ditch Catch and remove Meowth from play, but it
  is a niche three-Energy attack rather than the normal recovery plan.


BENCH AND PRIZE MAPPING

Default prize map

INFERENCE: The clean default asks the opponent to take three Archaludon ex
knockouts: 2-2-2. That is not always achievable, but it is the baseline against
which optional benching should be judged.

A Duraludon or Dunsparce knockout can make the opponent take seven or more
prizes in total if it does not also break the engine. Conversely, a gusted
Meowth ex or Fezandipiti ex preserves the opponent's 2-2-2 route. A gusted
Mega Mawile can compress the game into 3 plus 2 plus 1.

Before benching a Rule Box support Pokémon, ask:

1. How many prizes does it expose?
2. Can the opponent gust and knock it out now?
3. Does its immediate effect create at least equivalent tempo or prizes?
4. Can Scoop Up Cyclone or its own attack remove it later?
5. Which core body loses the bench slot?

Bench roles

INFERENCE: A typical five-slot allocation is:

- current or replacement Duraludon lane;
- second Duraludon lane;
- Dunsparce engine body;
- second Dunsparce or recycled engine body;
- one flex slot.

Relicanth belongs in the flex slot only when Raging Hammer is live or likely.
Fan Rotom belongs there only while first-turn setup value exceeds its later
liability. Meowth belongs there when a named Supporter changes the turn.
Fezandipiti belongs there after an opponent-turn knockout when three cards are
worth exposing two prizes. Mega Mawile belongs there for a funded threshold.

Scoop Up Cyclone

SOURCE: Scoop Up Cyclone puts one of your Pokémon and all cards attached to it
into your hand.

INFERENCE: Four common jobs:

1. Reset a damaged Archaludon ex and deny an imminent two-Prize knockout.
2. Remove Meowth ex or Fezandipiti ex from a gustable bench.
3. Return Meowth ex so Last-Ditch Catch can be used on a later replay.
4. Reopen a bench slot at the exact turn it is needed.

The Archaludon reset requires preparation. When an evolved Archaludon and its
Energy return to hand, the Energy is not in discard and the Duraludon under
the evolution returns too. A different Duraludon must already be in play if
you want to evolve again that turn. Confirm the promoted Active and retreat
path before scooping the Active.


MATCHUP PLANS

These plans prioritize mechanics. The public percentages are descriptive
testing context only.

Dragapult and Dragapult Dusknoir

SOURCE: In the reviewed aggregate, plain Dragapult was 11-6 for Archaludon,
while Dragapult Dusknoir was 8-9-2. Battle Cage prevents damage counters from
being placed on Benched Pokémon by effects of the opponent's attacks and
Abilities. It explicitly does not prevent damage from attacks.

INFERENCE:

- Prioritize Battle Cage before the opponent's damage-counter turn.
- Keep at least two Duraludon when a single counter-placement sequence can
  remove an evolution seed.
- Metal Defender's 220 and 300 HP make two-hit tanking plausible, but count
  all public modifiers rather than assuming survival.
- Use Run Away Draw to remove a damaged Dudunsparce when legal, but do not
  expose a new 60- or 70-HP Dunsparce without a reason.
- Against Dusknoir, account for counter placement before choosing the next
  attacker and prize map.
- Battle Cage can be replaced by another Stadium. Do not call the bench safe
  beyond the current public board.

Raging Bolt Ogerpon

SOURCE: The aggregate row was 8-1 across only nine matches.

INFERENCE:

- Treat the row as a testing hypothesis, not an 89% matchup.
- Force the opponent to spend the full resources required to clear 300 HP.
- Metal Defender's Weakness clause is usually not the core here; its stable
  220 pressure and the second attacker matter more.
- Gust a two-Prize engine only when it changes the race now.
- Avoid volunteering Meowth, Fezandipiti, or an unfunded Mega Mawile as an
  easier two- or three-Prize route.

Rocket's Mewtwo and Rocket's Spidops

SOURCE: The aggregate recorded 6-4 against Rocket's Mewtwo and 1-2 against
Rocket's Spidops, with the Spidops sample only three matches.

INFERENCE:

- Against either Rocket deck, count the public Team Rocket bodies and Energy
  before assuming the Active survives.
- Take efficient one-Prize knockouts on engine pieces when they break the next
  attack; otherwise prioritize the funded attacker or exposed Rule Box target.
- Do not give the single-Prize Spidops plan a free compressed prize map through
  Mega Mawile.
- Battle Cage does not prevent Sneasel's ordinary attack damage to a Benched
  Pokémon. Do not misapply its damage-counter wording.
- Preserve a second Duraludon because gusting or removing the only 130-HP seed
  can strand every Archaludon in hand.

Alakazam Dudunsparce and other Dudunsparce engines

SOURCE: The aggregate recorded 8-6 against Alakazam Dudunsparce.

INFERENCE:

- Track how many Dunsparce remain in play after each Run Away Draw.
- Gust only when removing the draw engine or attacker changes the following
  turn; a recycled Dudunsparce can make an unfocused Boss low value.
- Preserve Judge or Special Red Card for a turn when the opponent cannot
  immediately redraw through multiple Dudunsparce.
- Protect your own draw engine from counter placement with Battle Cage when
  that mechanic is present.

Lucario Hariyama and other single-Prize attackers

SOURCE: The reviewed row was 3-5 across eight matches.

INFERENCE:

- Expect the opponent to trade one prize into a two-Prize Archaludon. You need
  tanking, missed attacks, or multi-prize conversions to recover the exchange.
- Do not worsen the map with Mega Mawile unless it takes decisive prizes.
- Boss a multi-Prize support target when available; otherwise remove the
  exact attacker or engine that prevents the next attack.
- A damaged Archaludon using Raging Hammer can create a needed one-hit
  knockout, but verify whether losing Weakness protection matters.

Crustle and other anti-ex walls

SOURCE: Crustle DRI 12 prevents attack damage to itself from opposing Pokémon
ex. The aggregate row was 2-5 across seven matches. The reference list has no
single-Prize Archaludon; the Turin alternate has one.

INFERENCE:

- Do not evolve every Duraludon into Archaludon ex.
- Preserve and fund an unevolved Duraludon as the reference list's legal
  damage route.
- LIST CHECK: If single-Prize Archaludon is registered, preserve the line and
  use Poké Pad or recovery to access it.
- Boss can bypass the wall only when a legal target and prize plan exist.
- An ex attack dealing more damage does not solve an effect that prevents all
  of that attack damage.

Fire Weakness

SOURCE: Archaludon ex and Mega Mawile ex are Fire Weak. Metal Defender removes
the attacking Archaludon's Weakness only during the opponent's next turn.

INFERENCE:

- Refresh Metal Defender every turn that Weakness removal is required.
- Do not switch to Raging Hammer for marginal damage if it turns survival into
  a one-hit knockout.
- Scoop a heavily damaged Archaludon only when the replacement lane is ready.
- Mega Mawile is not a safe alternate tank into Fire merely because it is a
  different attacker.

Mirror

INFERENCE:

- Track which player has the second live Duraludon and which has exhausted
  Assemble Alloy.
- A damaged Archaludon can cross 260 or 300 with Raging Hammer; Relicanth
  therefore changes the opponent's safe-damage calculations.
- Black Belt makes Metal Defender 260 into an Active ex, but not 300.
- A clean Huge Bite plus Black Belt reaches 300. Do not place incidental
  damage on the intended target first.
- Special Red Card becomes live when the opponent has three or fewer prizes.
  Pair disruption with a board state that limits immediate Dudunsparce redraw.


RECOVERY TREES

If the Active Archaludon is Knocked Out

1. Promote the Pokémon that preserves the most options, not automatically the
   next Duraludon.
2. Use Flip the Script only if Fezandipiti is already worth its prize risk or
   must be benched now for a recoverable turn.
3. Identify whether the bottleneck is Duraludon, Archaludon ex, or Basic Metal.
4. Night Stretcher that bottleneck.
5. Seed discard Metal if needed.
6. Evolve the standing Duraludon and resolve Assemble Alloy.
7. Use Philippe only if the Supporter attachment is better than draw, gust,
   disruption, or Black Belt.

If no Duraludon remains in play

INFERENCE: The deck cannot create an Archaludon attack immediately from a new
Duraludon because a newly played Basic normally cannot evolve that turn.
Recover and bench Duraludon, protect it, and choose a bridge attack or
disruption turn. This is why the second line is established before the first
knockout.

If no Basic Metal is in discard

Use a manual attachment if it completes the attack. Otherwise create only the
needed discard with Ultra Ball or Prism Tower. Do not play Philippe with no
valid Metal target in discard, and do not evolve solely to announce an empty
Assemble Alloy unless the evolution itself is required.

If the draw engine is gone

Night Stretcher Dunsparce or Dudunsparce according to the board. Poké Pad can
find either because neither has a Rule Box. Preserve Lillie's Determination
for a meaningful reset instead of spending multiple cards to reconstruct a
draw loop that will not affect the current turn.

If the board is full

Run Away Draw can remove Dudunsparce. Scoop Up Cyclone can remove a liability.
Tuck Tail can remove Meowth if funded and attacking is acceptable. Do not
discard a playable Pokémon and call the bench problem solved if the actual
needed card still has nowhere to enter.


COMMON PILOT ERRORS

1. Evolving the only Duraludon without a replacement seed.
2. Discarding more Metal than the next two attackers can use.
3. Evolving before deciding how Assemble Alloy will distribute Energy.
4. Treating Metal in hand as though Assemble Alloy can attach it.
5. Attaching both accelerated Metal to the new Archaludon when one should fund
   the replacement, or splitting when it leaves both attackers short.
6. Using Raging Hammer because its number is larger while forgetting that
   Metal Defender's Weakness protection will not be refreshed.
7. Benching Relicanth before Raging Hammer is relevant.
8. Benching Meowth ex without naming the Supporter and present-turn use.
9. Benching Fezandipiti ex before an opponent-turn knockout enables Flip the
   Script, or leaving it as a two-Prize gust target after its job.
10. Benching Mega Mawile early and compressing the opponent's prize route.
11. Using Hammer In before a planned Huge Bite and turning its base from 260
    to 30.
12. Treating Prism Tower as free draw and ignoring that the opponent may use
    it.
13. Replacing Battle Cage before the opponent's damage-counter turn.
14. Assuming Battle Cage prevents ordinary attack damage to the Bench.
15. Attaching Energy to Dudunsparce and then shuffling that Energy into deck
    without planning for it.
16. Using Run Away Draw when the resulting empty bench or tiny deck makes the
    endgame worse.
17. Scooping an Archaludon without another legal Duraludon evolution lane.
18. Forgetting that Scooped Energy returns to hand rather than discard.
19. Evolving every Duraludon against an ex-immune wall.
20. Playing disruption before using one's own exact searches and draw
    abilities, then failing to rebuild the hand.
21. Believing an aggregate matchup percentage guarantees the next decision.
22. Planning around a face-down Prize identity rather than a legal deck-search
    inference.

HEURISTIC BOUNDARY

The companion auxiliary teacher deliberately covers less than this human
guide. It can rank only a complete legal factorized stage whose current
options all resolve exactly: selected opening Basics; exact Fan Call, Poffin,
Poké Pad, Ultra Ball, Hilda, or Night Stretcher search choices; and safe
yes/no prompts for Run Away Draw or Flip the Script.

It masks attack choice, manual Energy attachment, Assemble Alloy routing,
discard costs, retreat, gust, Stadium replacement, Scoop Up Cyclone targets,
damage-dependent Raging Hammer choices, Mega Mawile prize math, and matchup
branches. Those decisions need broader planning. The teacher uses only the
acting player's current observable state and public board, never an opponent's
hidden hand, face-down Prize identities, future draws, future actions, or
eventual outcomes. It supplies auditable auxiliary labels and has no authority
to select an action or override the fused policy.
