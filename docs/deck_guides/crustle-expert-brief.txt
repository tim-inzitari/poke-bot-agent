CRUSTLE / MEGA KANGASKHAN EX
CURRENT HUMAN PILOT GUIDE

Guide identity: crustle
Research version: crustle-north-star-proposal-v1
Format reviewed: 2026 Standard, through Crimson Blaze (CRI)
Representative: Cooper Kull, 55th, NAIC 2026
Audience: advanced and championship-level pilots

======================================================================
1. WHAT THIS DECK IS
======================================================================

Crustle is a single-Prize wall deck with a deliberately lopsided game plan.
Crustle DRI 12 prevents all attack damage from opposing Pokémon ex. A large
part of the format therefore cannot take the Active Prize through its normal
attacker. The deck uses Mega Kangaskhan ex as an early draw engine and a real
alternate attacker, then converts time into healing, Energy denial, hand
pressure, and carefully chosen gusts.

That description contains the first important correction for new pilots:
Crustle is not "promote Crustle and pass." The wall buys turns, but you still
have to decide what the opponent's legal answer is, whether that answer deals
damage or places damage counters, whether Mist Energy matters, which wall can
be lost, when Kangaskhan must attack, and whether your real win condition is
six Prizes, resource exhaustion, or the clock.

Limitless identifies the archetype as Crustle / Mysterious Rock Inn. Its
current six-month results page showed 6.14% of points in the reviewed format,
and Rahul Reddy placed fifth at NAIC 2026. The current online matchup aggregate
was 53.01% over 3,290 recorded matches. These are observational results, not a
guarantee about a particular list or pilot. [S1][S2][S15]

The representative sixty in this guide is not Rahul's fifth-place sixty. It is
the exact Cooper Kull 55th-place NAIC list already archived in this project.
That distinction matters because Cooper's list uses four Crushing Hammer,
Psyduck, Super Potion, Festival Grounds, Team Rocket's Factory, and Lumiose
City. Rahul's list instead includes a different disruption and stadium package
and no Psyduck. General strategy transfers; individual card lines do not.
[S1][S2]

The deck's card roles are unusually clean:

- Dwebble is both the evolution base and the acceleration shortcut. Ascension
  evolves it directly from the deck for one Colorless Energy. [S4]
- Crustle is the wall. Mysterious Rock Inn prevents damage from attacks by
  Pokémon ex; it does not say that all effects are prevented, and it does not
  protect the Bench. Superb Scissors costs Grass-Colorless-Colorless, deals
  120, and ignores effects on the opponent's Active. [S3]
- Mega Kangaskhan ex is setup insurance and a three-Prize attacker. While
  Active, Run Errand draws two once per turn. Rapid-Fire Combo starts at 200
  for three Colorless and adds 50 per heads before the first tails. [S5]
- Psyduck is a narrow one-Prize utility Basic, not an automatic bench.
- Jumbo Ice Cream heals 80 from the Active only when it has at least three
  Energy attached. [S6]
- Growing Grass Energy supplies Grass and adds 20 HP while attached to a Grass
  Pokémon. Spiky Energy supplies Colorless and puts two damage counters on an
  attacker that damages its Active holder. Mist Energy supplies Colorless and
  blocks effects of opposing attacks, but expressly does not prevent damage.
  [S10][S11][S12]
- Hilda finds one Evolution Pokémon and one Energy. That is frequently the
  cleanest Crustle-plus-Energy bridge, but it consumes the Supporter for the
  turn. [S9]
- Team Rocket's Petrel finds any Trainer. It is the glue card: search the
  Trainer that solves the exact current problem, not the card that looked best
  before the opponent acted. [S8]
- Lillie's Determination draws eight while all six Prizes remain and six
  afterward, making the opening Supporter window uniquely valuable. [S7]

======================================================================
2. THE THREE-QUESTION GAME PLAN
======================================================================

At the start of every turn, answer these in order.

1. How can the opponent take a Prize through my intended Active?

Name the card, not the archetype. Separate:

- damage from a Pokémon ex attack, which Crustle prevents;
- damage from a non-ex attacker, which Crustle does not prevent;
- damage counters or other effects, which require their own rules check;
- gust around Crustle to a benched liability;
- Ability-based damage or Knock Out effects;
- Ability suppression, which can turn off Mysterious Rock Inn;
- retreat lock, Special Energy removal, or Tool removal that does not take a
  Prize immediately but breaks the next-turn plan.

If you cannot name the answer, do not over-bench speculative liabilities.

2. What does my next attacker need?

Crustle needs Grass plus two Colorless. In this sixty, Growing Grass is the
clean Grass source, while Mist and Spiky are Colorless. Kangaskhan accepts any
three. A Dwebble that must Ascend now needs one attachment; a Crustle expected
to attack later needs a three-attachment schedule. Do not scatter Energy
because all three Special Energy types look useful. First complete the line
that must act.

3. Which clock am I playing?

- Prize clock: attack, gust, and race.
- resource clock: heal and remove Energy until their answer disappears.
- board clock: deny a second copy of their answer and force them through the
  wall.
- tournament clock: take actions briskly, track turns, and know when a draw is
  unacceptable.

The right clock can change after one Hammer flip, one revealed answer, or one
Prize card.

======================================================================
3. SETUP AND OPENING SEQUENCING
======================================================================

Opening board targets

A strong generic start has:

- one Dwebble that can become Crustle;
- a second Dwebble when the opponent can gust or remove the first;
- one Active Kangaskhan only when Run Errand materially improves setup or when
  it is your planned first attacker;
- no unnecessary three-Prize Pokémon on the Bench;
- one attachment whose purpose you can state.

Do not turn "two Dwebble is good" into "fill every bench slot." A compact board
is a defensive resource. Boss's Orders and Lisia's Appeal punish liabilities,
and opposing spread can turn harmless Basics into a prize map.

Before searching

1. Check all three Dwebble and all three Crustle.
2. Check Hero's Cape, all four Growing Grass, and the stadiums.
3. Count how many Mist and Spiky Energy are accessible.
4. Identify whether Psyduck is prized or simply absent from the opening hand.
5. Decide whether this matchup asks for a wall or immediate Kangaskhan damage.

Prize checking need not be perfectly exhaustive on turn one, but the evolution
line and Energy mix are non-negotiable. With only three Dwebble and three
Crustle, prizing two copies changes how aggressively you expose the remaining
line.

Search ordering

- Use Buddy-Buddy Poffin before a shuffle-draw if the Dwebble is definitely
  needed.
- Use Hilda when the exact pair — usually Crustle plus the Energy that
  completes the plan — is worth spending the Supporter.
- Use Petrel only after naming the missing function. Petrel can become Poffin,
  Ultra Ball, Pokégear, Hammer, Ice Cream, Switch, or a stadium; "get a good
  Trainer" is not a plan.
- Preserve Ultra Ball if its discard cost would consume a unique recovery,
  stadium, Tool, or Energy resource. This list has no Night Stretcher.
- Use Pokégear before committing to a weaker Supporter line when the top seven
  can find the Supporter that makes the whole turn coherent.
- Draw before searching only when the search target is flexible and drawing
  can save the search card. Search before drawing when the target is mandatory
  and a shuffle-draw might strand it.

Run Errand sequencing

Run Errand requires Kangaskhan in the Active Spot. If you intend to move it,
draw first unless drawing risks decking out or a known ordering constraint
matters. Do not burn Switch, retreat Energy, or a gust Supporter merely to
access two cards unless those two cards are necessary to establish the wall or
win the game. Kangaskhan has a retreat cost of three; promoting it casually can
be expensive. [S5]

======================================================================
4. GOING FIRST
======================================================================

Default first-turn objective: establish two Dwebble if the opponent can remove
one, exploit Lillie's eight-card window, and make an attachment that advances
a turn-two Crustle or Kangaskhan.

Preferred outline

1. Prize-check the 3-3 Crustle line and Energy mix.
2. Bench one Dwebble; bench a second when removal or gust is credible.
3. If Kangaskhan is Active, use Run Errand before moving it.
4. Use Lillie's Determination while six Prizes remain when it does not shuffle
   away a uniquely stronger prepared hand. The eight-card opening mode is a
   real tempo resource. [S7]
5. Attach Growing Grass to the Dwebble most likely to become the attacking
   Crustle, unless immediate survival requires Mist or the planned attacker is
   Kangaskhan.
6. End with a turn-two evolution path and a contingency if the first Dwebble
   is removed.

Why not always attach Mist first? Because Mist provides Colorless, not Grass.
A Crustle with three Special Energy but no Grass cannot use Superb Scissors.
Likewise, why not always lead Spiky? Its counters trigger only when the Active
holder is damaged by an opposing attack. If Mysterious Rock Inn prevents all
damage from an ex, Spiky's condition was not met. Save it for the Active and
matchup where damage will actually happen. [S3][S11][S12]

Your second turn

Evolve normally when Crustle is in hand. If not, Hilda is the cleanest
evolution-plus-Energy bridge. Decide whether to:

- promote Crustle immediately and begin the wall;
- keep Kangaskhan Active for another Run Errand and/or attack;
- leave a sacrificial one-Prize Active while finishing a three-Energy Crustle;
- attack with Superb Scissors if the 120 meaningfully changes the prize map.

Do not promote the wall automatically. Against a known non-ex answer that can
take Crustle immediately, forcing that answer to act may still be right, but
you should have a second wall or a race plan.

======================================================================
5. GOING SECOND
======================================================================

Going second gives Dwebble access to Ascension immediately. This is the deck's
fastest wall line: attach any Energy to an Active Dwebble, attack, and evolve
from the deck. [S4]

Default outline

1. Prefer an Active Dwebble when Ascension is safe and useful.
2. Establish a second Dwebble before investing in optional support.
3. Attach with turn-two attack math in mind. Growing Grass is ideal if the
   Active Crustle will attack; Mist can be correct when an attack effect is the
   opponent's answer; Spiky is correct when retaliation matters.
4. Use the opening Lillie's eight-card mode before taking a Prize.
5. Ascend only after completing all useful Trainer actions and checking that
   Crustle remains in deck.

Do not Ascend blindly into a known non-ex one-hit knockout. Sometimes the
correct second-turn plan is Kangaskhan: Run Errand, attach toward three, and
force the opponent to answer a 300-HP attacker while the Dwebble develops on
the Bench. Kangaskhan concedes three Prizes when Knocked Out, so this pivot
must buy more than it gives away.

Going-second decision:

- If the opponent's normal attacker is a Pokémon ex and has no visible bypass,
  Ascension is high priority.
- If their answer attacks through Crustle, preserve a second Dwebble and plan
  Kangaskhan damage or disruption.
- If the opponent can gust a benched Kangaskhan for three Prizes, do not bench
  it just for hypothetical future draw.
- If the deck is likely to win by resource denial, start attaching to the wall
  that will eventually satisfy Jumbo Ice Cream.

======================================================================
6. ENERGY, HEALING, AND ATTACK PLANNING
======================================================================

Energy ledger

Keep a mental four-column ledger:

1. Growing Grass remaining.
2. Mist remaining.
3. Spiky remaining.
4. Energy already committed to a Pokémon likely to be gusted or stranded.

Growing Grass on Crustle is structural: it supplies the required Grass and adds
20 HP. Mist is tactical: it blocks effects of attacks, not damage. Spiky is
punitive: it adds counters only after the Active holder is damaged. All three
count toward the three Energy needed for Jumbo Ice Cream. [S6][S10][S11][S12]

A typical attack-ready Crustle has one Growing Grass and two Colorless-providing
Special Energy. The last two slots should reflect the opponent's answer:

- prefer Mist when their plan depends on an attack effect;
- prefer Spiky when their answer deals ordinary damage and the two counters
  improve a knockout breakpoint;
- prefer additional Growing Grass when the extra HP is the difference between
  surviving and being Knocked Out, provided you are not starving the next
  attacker.

Jumbo Ice Cream math

Ice Cream heals 80 only from the Active Pokémon with at least three Energy.
Therefore:

- attaching the third Energy can turn every retained Ice Cream into 80 healing;
- healing before the opponent reaches a clean knockout breakpoint is often
  stronger than waiting for maximum damage;
- healing zero or trivial damage simply because the Item is playable wastes
  the resource;
- moving the damaged Pokémon to the Bench shuts off Ice Cream until it is
  Active again;
- a Tool or Growing Grass that raises effective HP can alter how many Ice
  Creams are needed across two attacks.

Super Potion is a separate one-of. Count its Energy cost and the attack it may
turn off before using it. Healing that prevents a Knock Out but strands the
only attacking Crustle can lose the same Prize one turn later.

Hero's Cape

Cape belongs on the Pokémon whose extra HP changes the opponent's number of
attacks, not automatically the first Crustle. Check Tool removal and stadium
answers. Against decks that cannot damage Crustle with their ex attacker, Cape
may be more valuable on Kangaskhan or may be unnecessary until the opponent
reveals a bypass.

Attack selection

Superb Scissors is not filler. It deals 120 through effects on the opponent's
Active. Use it to:

- remove a single-Prize answer before it gets a second attack;
- create a Boss's Orders knockout next turn;
- force a damaged ex into the exact Kangaskhan range;
- keep the Prize clock moving when passive walling lets the opponent assemble
  multiple answers.

Kangaskhan's Rapid-Fire Combo starts at 200, then depends on coin flips. Plan
around 200 as the reliable floor. Treat extra heads as upside, not as a prize
map you are entitled to receive. [S5]

======================================================================
7. BENCH AND PRIZE MAPPING
======================================================================

Bench discipline

Each Pokémon must have a current job:

- Active or next Crustle;
- second Dwebble against removal;
- Kangaskhan drawing now or attacking on schedule;
- Psyduck addressing a known problem now.

If a Pokémon has no job, do not bench it. A benched Kangaskhan is a three-Prize
Boss target. A benched Dwebble can become a Prize that lets the opponent skip
the wall. A benched Psyduck is useful only if its Ability is relevant and not
itself the easiest route through the board.

Prize-map templates

Pure wall map:

- concede one Crustle;
- replace with one Crustle;
- deny the final Prizes while taking six single-Prize or gusted targets.

Kangaskhan pivot map:

- force the opponent to spend turns on Crustle;
- attack with Kangaskhan when its 200 floor wins the race;
- accept the three-Prize liability only if the remaining map is favorable.

Sacrifice map:

- give one Dwebble or Psyduck to finish three Energy on the real wall;
- promote Crustle with healing online;
- use Boss or Lisia to remove the opponent's only answer.

At every promotion, state both prize maps aloud: "If they take this, they go to
N; if I take my intended target, I go to M." Do not evaluate only HP. A 300-HP
Kangaskhan can be strategically frailer than a 70-HP Dwebble because of the
three-Prize payout.

Opponent bench mapping

Mark:

- their non-ex attacker;
- their Ability suppressor;
- their Tool or Energy-removal engine;
- their draw engine;
- their lowest-HP two-Prize or three-Prize closer.

Boss's Orders is often better used to remove the only Crustle answer than to
take the largest immediate Prize. Lisia's Appeal can pull a Basic and Confuse
it, buying a turn even when the target is not a knockout. [S13]

======================================================================
8. DISRUPTION AND RESOURCE CONTROL
======================================================================

Crushing Hammer

Four Hammer makes Energy denial a plan, but each individual flip is stochastic.
Sequence guaranteed actions before optional flips when the result would change
your search, Supporter, or attack line. After a heads, recalculate; do not keep
playing the old turn.

Target Energy by function:

1. the only Energy on the opponent's Crustle answer;
2. an irreplaceable Special Energy;
3. the attachment that preserves retreat into their answer;
4. the largest pile only when no functional target exists.

Do not assume heads. A line that loses immediately on tails was already risky.

Petrel

Petrel is a Trainer search Supporter. Its strongest use is not a fixed card but
turn compression:

- Poffin when a second Dwebble is mandatory;
- Ultra Ball when Crustle is missing;
- Pokégear when another Supporter solves the actual turn;
- Hammer when one removed Energy buys a full turn;
- Ice Cream when 80 changes the knockout count;
- Switch when a trapped Active breaks the plan;
- Festival Grounds, Factory, or Lumiose City when the stadium war matters.

Because Petrel consumes the Supporter, compare the found Trainer with simply
playing Hilda, Lillie's, Boss, Lisia, Xerosic, or another Supporter now. [S8]

Hand pressure

Xerosic reduces the opponent to three cards. It is strongest after they have
committed search and before they can use a large draw engine. Do not play it
just because their hand is large; compare it with Boss removing the answer or
Lillie's repairing your own hand. Eri appears in Rahul's current list and can
discard up to two revealed Items, but it is not in Cooper's representative and
must not be assumed available. [S14]

Stadiums

The three one-of stadiums are not interchangeable. Keep track of which stadium
has been used, which remains in deck, and whether replacing the current
stadium helps the opponent more than you. Festival Grounds has a current
status-clearing role; Factory is a Team Rocket engine card; Lumiose City is a
different utility stadium. Exact text and matchup interaction should be
confirmed during event preparation. With only one of each and no generic
recovery in the sixty, blind early stadium play is expensive.

9. MATCHUP PLANS
======================================================================

These plans use current aggregate evidence as testing priorities, not causal
proof. The reviewed CRI online table recorded Crustle at 59.28% versus
Dragapult, 64.04% versus N's Zoroark, 61.08% versus Alakazam, and 87.50%
versus Beedrill. It was poor into Slowking (33.68%), Rocket's Honchkrow
(20.18%), Mega Starmie (36.26%), Festival Lead (39.33%), Rocket's Mewtwo
(39.34%), Mega Lopunny (22.95%), and Ethan's Typhlosion (23.91%). Samples,
variant mixing, event strength, and list changes all limit those figures.
[S15]

Dragapult

Primary plan: establish Crustle before Dragapult takes easy bench Prizes, keep
the Bench narrow, and protect key Pokémon from attack effects when Mist
Energy applies. Mysterious Rock Inn prevents attack damage from Dragapult ex,
but Dragapult variants may have non-ex or Ability-based answers and can route
damage counters around the Active. Identify the exact variant before assuming
the matchup is free.

- Two Dwebble is enough; five liabilities is not.
- Remove the answer, not merely the largest Dragapult.
- Track damage-counter math on every benched Basic.
- Preserve Mist when it blocks the exact attack effect being used.
- Against straight Dragapult with no answer, resource preservation matters
  more than early Kangaskhan damage.

Slowking

This is a bad aggregate matchup because a single-Prize attacker can copy
attacks that bypass the ex wall. Your goal is active disruption, not passive
immunity.

- Use Boss or Lisia to interrupt the Slowking line or remove its setup Basic.
- Hammer the Energy that makes the copied attack available.
- Pressure with Superb Scissors; 120 forces real healing or a two-hit clock.
- Preserve the second wall, but do not expect walling alone to win.
- Kangaskhan can race, but exposing three Prizes must shorten the game.

Rocket's Honchkrow, Festival Lead, and other non-ex attackers

Mysterious Rock Inn does not protect against non-ex attack damage. Treat these
as ordinary prize races with healing and disruption.

- Spiky Energy is more valuable when the attacker actually deals damage.
- Ice Cream breakpoint math is central.
- Boss the engine or the next attacker before it becomes active.
- Do not spend turns admiring the wall while the opponent takes single-Prize
  knockouts.

N's Zoroark

The aggregate is favorable, but the opponent can use non-ex attackers and
status pressure.

- Identify the exact attacker and whether it damages or applies effects.
- Festival Grounds can matter for status management.
- Mist does not prevent damage, so attach it only for the relevant effect.
- Remove the Energy or support that lets the answer repeat.

Alakazam

Crustle's aggregate was favorable in the reviewed online data, but Alakazam
lists can contain single-Prize or Dudunsparce-based routes.

- Keep a second Dwebble without overfilling the Bench.
- Remove the non-ex answer before it is repeated.
- Count whether Superb Scissors plus Spiky counters reaches a key breakpoint.
- Do not rely on the matchup label; inspect the revealed sixty-card signature.

Mega Lopunny, Mega Starmie, and high-pressure bypass decks

These are poor aggregate matchups. Ask whether Crustle buys even one turn. If
not, pivot toward Kangaskhan attack, Hammer, hand disruption, and gust.

- Hammers target the minimum Energy that turns off an attack.
- Cape goes where it changes an attack count.
- Use Lisia to Confuse a Basic when that can buy the missing attachment turn.
- Accept that the correct route may be a six-Prize race, not a wall game.

Mirror

The online aggregate contains many ties. The mirror is about attack access,
Energy preservation, gust, and time.

- Superb Scissors ignores effects on the opponent's Active but still deals
  only 120; plan the two-hit sequence and healing response.
- Kangaskhan can pressure, but it is worth three Prizes.
- Do not waste Hammers on Energy that does not change the next attack.
- Play at a legal, decisive pace. Know early whether you need a win rather than
  a draw.

10. RECOVERY LINES
======================================================================

There is no Night Stretcher or Lana's Aid in the representative. Once a Pokémon
or Energy is discarded, assume it is gone unless the current board proves
otherwise. Recovery therefore means rebuilding the plan, not retrieving every
lost card.

If the only Dwebble is Knocked Out:

1. promote the Pokémon that gives the safest search turn;
2. Poffin the second Dwebble;
3. Hilda for Crustle plus Energy if available;
4. decide whether another Ascension turn is safe;
5. do not bench Kangaskhan unless Run Errand or the race justifies three
   exposed Prizes.

If Crustle is Knocked Out through the wall:

1. identify the exact bypass;
2. decide whether Mist, healing, Cape, Hammer, or Boss stops repetition;
3. if it does not, stop repeating the same wall line and pivot to damage;
4. preserve the last Crustle only if it buys a real turn.

If Growing Grass is scarce:

1. count all remaining copies;
2. reserve one for every Crustle expected to attack;
3. do not attach multiple Growing Grass for HP unless the extra 20/40 changes
   the current knockout breakpoint;
4. use Kangaskhan, whose attack is Colorless, as the alternate sink for Mist
   and Spiky.

If the Active is trapped:

- check Switch before discarding three Energy to retreat;
- check whether attacking is better than retreating;
- use Petrel for Switch only when no stronger Supporter line exists;
- remember that moving a damaged three-Energy Pokémon to the Bench turns off
  Ice Cream until it returns Active.

If the hand is dead:

- Run Errand if Kangaskhan is already Active;
- Pokégear for Lillie's or the exact Supporter;
- Petrel for Pokégear only when the extra Supporter-search layer is worth the
  turn;
- preserve a usable hand rather than shuffling automatically.

11. COMMON MISTAKES
======================================================================

1. Reading Mysterious Rock Inn as complete immunity. It prevents attack damage
   from Pokémon ex; it does not write "prevent all effects," protect the Bench,
   or stop non-ex attackers. [S3]

2. Benching every Basic. Kangaskhan is a three-Prize gust target and Psyduck is
   not mandatory in every matchup.

3. Missing the Grass requirement. Mist and Spiky are Colorless. At least one
   Growing Grass is needed for Superb Scissors in this sixty.

4. Attaching Spiky where damage will be prevented. Its counters require the
   holder to be damaged by an attack. [S11]

5. Treating Mist as damage prevention. Mist blocks effects of attacks, not
   damage. [S12]

6. Healing before three Energy. Jumbo Ice Cream is dead unless the Active has
   at least three attached. [S6]

7. Spending Hilda without using both halves. If you cannot name the Evolution
   and Energy you need, another Supporter may be stronger.

8. Using Petrel without a target. Search is only as good as the exact Trainer
   function it supplies.

9. Planning Kangaskhan damage above 200. Additional Rapid-Fire Combo damage is
   stochastic. [S5]

10. Repeating a failed wall line. Once the opponent reveals a reliable bypass,
    adapt with Boss, Hammer, healing, or Kangaskhan.

11. Failing to prize-check the 3-3 line. Two prized Crustle changes the entire
    wall schedule.

12. Holding Boss only for large Prizes. Removing the opponent's only Crustle
    answer can be worth more than taking two Prizes now.

13. Ignoring the clock. Control turns require many micro-decisions. Practice
    the search and Energy ledgers until they are fast.

[ ] Which Pokémon must attack?

[ ] Does Mist block an exact attack effect?
[ ] Will Spiky's damage condition actually occur?
[ ] Does Growing Grass change a knockout breakpoint?
[ ] Does this become the third Energy for Ice Cream?
[ ] What happens if this Pokémon is gusted?

Before promoting Crustle

[ ] What exact opposing card gets through Mysterious Rock Inn?
[ ] Is that answer currently in play, discard, or only hypothetical?
[ ] Can Boss, Lisia, Hammer, or Xerosic stop it?
[ ] Is a second wall ready?
[ ] Is Superb Scissors online or am I only buying a turn?

Before healing

[ ] Does the Active have three Energy for Ice Cream?
[ ] How much damage is actually removed?
[ ] Does healing change the number of attacks to Knock Out?
[ ] Does Super Potion's Energy cost turn off my attack?
[ ] Is Cape needed on this Pokémon?

Before attacking

[ ] Is 120 from Superb Scissors changing a prize or answer map?
[ ] Am I counting Kangaskhan's reliable floor as 200?
[ ] What is the opponent's best promotion?
[ ] What Prize total follows on both sides?
[ ] Would gusting the only answer be stronger?
[ ] Is resource denial now better than damage?

[ ] How many Dwebble and Crustle remain?
[ ] How many Growing Grass, Mist, and Spiky remain?

    clock?

APPENDIX A. SPARSE CAUSAL HEURISTIC PROPOSAL
======================================================================

The accompanying teacher proposal is deliberately much smaller than this human
guide. Human strategy depends on matchup knowledge, inferred resources,
stochastic Hammer and Kangaskhan outcomes, Energy schedules, future healing,
and prize mapping. Those judgments must not be encoded as exact labels when
the observation does not prove them.

The proposal labels only complete, origin-resolved legal stages for:

- establishing two visible Dwebble during Bench setup;
- Poffin or Ultra Ball filling a visible Dwebble/Crustle gap;
- Dwebble's exact Ascension search;
- Run Errand Yes/No when current deck count proves a conservative draw reserve.

It masks opening Active choice, Energy, Hilda, Petrel, attack, healing, Hammer,
gust, stadium, Tool, retreat, disruption, prizes, matchups, unknown prompt
origins, unresolved options, partial candidate sets, and low-margin stages.
It never reads the opponent's hidden hand, either deck, face-down Prizes,
future draws, future random outcomes, eventual game result, or unselected
private cards. A missing label returns no target; it is not a zero target.
The serving bias function returns exact zeros, and the proposal remains
