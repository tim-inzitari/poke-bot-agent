HOW TO READ THIS PILOT

This guide teaches the exact deck in the owner's supplied image. It does not
replace it with the more common June 2026 Slowking/Metagross list. The owner's
list uses two Conkeldurr, two Annihilape, and two Smoochum, plus Counter Gain;
it has no Metagross, Lillie's Clefairy ex, Powerglass, Brave Bangle, Switch,
Boss's Orders, or other gust card.

The screenshot omits set codes and collector numbers. Public tournament lists
make the intended identities highly likely, but not all of them are literally
proved by the image. This pilot therefore uses the following identities:

- Slowking SCR 58 and Slowpoke SCR 57;
- Annihilape SSP 100, with Destined Fight;
- Conkeldurr TWM 105, with Gutsy Swing;
- Kyurem SFA 47, with Trifrost;
- Smoochum SSP 75, with Delightful Kiss;
- Latias ex SSP 76, Mega Kangaskhan ex MEG 104, Fezandipiti ex SFA 38,
  and Meowth ex POR 62; and
- Telepathic Psychic Energy POR 88, which the screenshot abbreviates as
  “Telepath Psychic Energy.”

Those identities should be confirmed when a set-coded export becomes

EXECUTIVE IDENTITY

SOURCE: Slowking SCR 58 is a 120-HP single-Prize Stage 1. Seek Inspiration
costs one Psychic and one Colorless Energy. It discards the top card of the
deck and, if that card is a Pokémon without a Rule Box, lets Slowking use one
of that Pokémon's attacks. [S2, S5]

SOURCE: Slowking became a genuine 2026 tournament archetype. Limitless records
a Turin Special Event runner-up and a fourth-place NAIC finish, and its
archetype overview records multiple major Top 8s. Public June and July guides
describe the deck as an aggressive combo deck whose normal opening objective
is an early Trifrost. [S3, S4, S19, S31]

LIST FACT: This exact version is a top-deck toolbox with four distinct attack
families:

- Kyurem's Trifrost spreads 110 damage to three opposing Pokémon and discards
  all Energy from the attacking Slowking.
- Conkeldurr's Gutsy Swing supplies a clean 250-damage hit.
- Annihilape's Destined Fight Knocks Out both Active Pokémon, regardless of
  their remaining HP, unless an applicable effect prevents it.
- Slowking, Slowpoke, and Smoochum provide lower-damage or recovery/acceleration
  attacks when those jobs are actually better.

INFERENCE: The deck is best understood as an exact sequencing deck, not a
roulette deck. Academy at Night and Ciphermaniac's Codebreaking make the top
card deliberate. Mega Kangaskhan ex, Latias ex, Telepathic Psychic Energy,
Smoochum, Wondrous Patch, and Boomerang Energy make repeated attacks possible.
The pilot wins by answering four questions every turn:

1. Which copied attack produces the best current prize map?
2. How will the next Slowking receive one Psychic plus one Colorless?
3. Which draw, search, and shuffle actions must happen before the top card is
   locked?
4. Which multi-Prize support Pokémon are truly worth exposing?

This version trades the 300-damage Metagross ceiling and common defensive
Tools for redundancy in Gutsy Swing, Destined Fight, and Delightful Kiss. That
makes its plans different enough that a generic Metagross guide is unsafe.

QUICK START

If time is short, pilot from these rules:

1. Build two Slowpoke lines. One line is too easy to remove or strand.
2. Prefer going first when the priority is the earliest legal Seek
   Inspiration: attach to Slowpoke on turn one, evolve and attach again on
   turn two.
3. Prefer the going-second Smoochum plan when two Basic Psychic attachments
   and a first-turn Supporter are worth delaying the first Slowking attack.
4. Open Mega Kangaskhan ex when possible only if Latias ex or another clean
   pivot can move it later. Use Run Errand before locking the top card.
5. Use all searches, shuffles, Lillie's Determination, Flip the Script, and
   Run Errand before Academy at Night puts the attack source on top.
6. With Ciphermaniac plus active Mega Kangaskhan, stack two exact cards, draw
   them with Run Errand, then use Academy to put the chosen attacker back on
   top before pivoting into Slowking.
7. Choose Kyurem when 110 to three visible targets creates immediate prizes
   or a clearly superior multi-target damage map.
8. Choose Conkeldurr when 250 takes the Active knockout. Preserve
   Annihilape for targets that damage cannot cleanly remove.
9. Use Destined Fight only after checking both players' remaining prizes,
   Bench, effect protection, and simultaneous-win consequences.
10. Treat Boomerang Energy as the preferred Colorless half of a planned
    Trifrost. It returns only when an attack effect discards it; it does not
    return after Hammer, retreat, hand discard, or a Knock Out.
11. Load the next Slowking on the Bench. Wondrous Patch cannot attach to the
    Active Pokémon and can recover only Basic Psychic Energy.
12. Do not bench a second Mega Kangaskhan, Fezandipiti, Meowth, or extra
    Latias merely because a slot is open. Name its same-turn or mapped job.
13. Against Team Rocket's Watchtower, replace it with Academy before relying
    on Run Errand or Last-Ditch Catch.
14. Against a visible Benched Bastiodon PBL 62, damage from a Slowking with
    two or fewer Energy attached is prevented. Reach three attached Energy
    cards or use a valid non-damage route such as Destined Fight.
15. Before ending the turn, name the next attacker, its Energy route, the
    attack source that must reach the top, and the opponent's shortest prize
    route through your Bench.


THE CORE ENGINE

Seek Inspiration

SOURCE: Seek Inspiration costs Psychic-Colorless. It discards the top card,
and only a Pokémon without a Rule Box can supply the copied attack. Slowking
itself remains the attacking Pokémon. Rule-Box Pokémon in this list—Latias ex,
Mega Kangaskhan ex, Fezandipiti ex, and Meowth ex—are setup or direct attackers,
not Seek Inspiration targets. [S2, S5]

INFERENCE: A successful attack turn requires four independent resources:

- an attacking Slowking;
- enough Energy to pay Seek Inspiration itself;
- a legal non-Rule-Box Pokémon on top; and
- an Active or Bench damage map that makes that copied attack useful.

Never call the turn “set up” merely because Slowking has two Energy. The
top-card route and the post-attack Energy route matter just as much.

Academy at Night

SOURCE: Once during each player's turn, Academy lets that player put a card
from hand on top of their deck. [S14]

INFERENCE: Academy is the cleanest final lock because it acts after all
shuffles and draws. Hold the selected Kyurem, Conkeldurr, or Annihilape in
hand, complete every other action, use Academy, and then attack. The opponent
may use Academy too, so do not mistake the Stadium for one-sided value.

Ciphermaniac's Codebreaking

SOURCE: Ciphermaniac searches any two cards, shuffles, and puts the chosen
cards on top in either order. [S15]

INFERENCE: Ciphermaniac has two principal modes:

1. Attack lock: place the copied attacker on top and the desired next-turn
   card underneath, then use Seek Inspiration.
2. Kangaskhan tutor: with Mega Kangaskhan Active, stack two setup cards, draw
   both with Run Errand, use Academy to restack the copied attacker, and pivot
   to Slowking.

The second mode converts a Supporter plus Run Errand into two exact cards
without sacrificing the attack lock. It requires Academy access and a legal
pivot. If Academy is absent, Run Errand after Ciphermaniac consumes the attack
source and ruins the lock.

The ordering rule

SOURCE: Ultra Ball, Poké Pad, Telepathic Psychic Energy, Ciphermaniac, and
other deck searches shuffle. Run Errand draws two. Lillie's Determination
shuffles the hand into the deck and draws. [S11, S13, S15, S16]

INFERENCE: Use this order unless the exact state proves a reason not to:

1. normal draw;
2. prize/deck inventory during a legal search;
3. bench and evolution actions;
4. all ordinary search and shuffle effects;
5. Lillie's Determination or Ciphermaniac;
6. Run Errand and Flip the Script;
7. Energy acceleration and retreat/pivot planning;
8. Academy at Night as the final top-card placement;
9. promote or pivot to Slowking;
10. Seek Inspiration.

A shuffle after Academy destroys the guarantee. A draw after Academy consumes
the target. A Run Errand after Ciphermaniac is correct only when the plan
explicitly draws both stacked cards and restacks the target with Academy.


THE COPY TOOLBOX

Kyurem SFA 47

SOURCE: Trifrost discards all Energy from the attacking Pokémon and deals 110
damage to three opposing Pokémon. Weakness and Resistance are not applied to
Benched Pokémon. [S6]

INFERENCE: Trifrost is the tempo ceiling. Use it for immediate multi-KOs,
three relevant setup targets, or a damage spread that turns later Gutsy Swings
into multiple clean prizes. Do not use it merely because Kyurem is the famous
line.

The key thresholds are:

- 110 remaining HP: immediate Trifrost knockout;
- 120-130 HP: Trifrost misses by 10-20, so confirm whether evolution or
  healing will erase the setup before spending all attached Energy;
- 250 remaining HP: clean Gutsy Swing;
- 251-360 HP: one prior 110 plus Gutsy Swing can reach 360 before other
  modifiers; and
- any HP: Destined Fight can be the effect-based answer if legally safe.

When Slowking copies Trifrost, the Energy discarded is Slowking's Energy.
Boomerang Energy can return after the attack because Trifrost itself discarded
it. Basic and Telepathic Psychic Energy do not return.

Do not assume all three targets take damage. Shaymin's Flower Curtain protects
Benched non-Rule-Box Pokémon. Bastiodon's Ancient Bulwark can prevent all
attack damage when the attacking Pokémon has two or fewer Energy attached.
Other current effects can also alter the map. [S20, S22]

Conkeldurr TWM 105

SOURCE: Gutsy Swing deals 250. Its printed cost is Fighting plus three
Colorless; its conditional text lets a Special-Conditioned Conkeldurr ignore
that cost. Seek Inspiration pays its own Psychic-Colorless cost and uses the
copied attack rather than building a Stage 2 Conkeldurr. [S7, S31]

INFERENCE: Gutsy Swing is the default single-target damage attack in this
sixty. Use it whenever 250, after current Weakness, Resistance, Tools, and
effects, takes the needed knockout. It is superior to Destined Fight when both
take the same prizes because Slowking survives and keeps its attached Energy.

The copied attack is used by Psychic-type Slowking, not by a Fighting-type
Conkeldurr in play. Do not apply Fighting Weakness just because the source
card is Conkeldurr.

Annihilape SSP 100

SOURCE: Destined Fight Knocks Out both Active Pokémon. Tantrum deals 130 and
makes the attacking Pokémon Confused. [S8]

INFERENCE: Destined Fight is the high-HP and damage-prevention breaker. A
single-Prize Slowking trading into a two- or three-Prize Active can be the
best route in the deck. It is not free:

- the opponent also takes a Prize for Slowking;
- all Energy and Tool cards on Slowking are lost through the Knock Out;
- Boomerang Energy is not being discarded by an attack effect and therefore
  does not earn its return;
- a simultaneous final-Prize turn can trigger the official simultaneous-win
  procedure; and
- an effect that prevents effects of attacks may stop the intended Knock Out.

Use Tantrum when 130 is exactly valuable enough to justify leaving Slowking
Confused. A Slowking that survives while Confused and has Retreat Cost three
is often worse than spending a different copy target.

Slowking, Slowpoke, and Smoochum as copy targets

SOURCE: Slowking's Super Psy Bolt deals 120. Slowpoke's Dangle Tail returns a
Pokémon from the discard pile to hand. Smoochum's Delightful Kiss searches up
to two Basic Psychic Energy and attaches both to one Benched Pokémon. [S5,
S9, S10]

INFERENCE:

- A top-decked Slowking can supply Super Psy Bolt for an exact 120. This can
  matter in the mirror, but it consumes an evolution card from the deck.
- A top-decked Slowpoke can use Dangle Tail to recover a Pokémon, including a
  critical copy target or evolution line. It is a recovery turn, not a damage
  turn.
- A top-decked Smoochum can copy Delightful Kiss, but attacking directly with
  a Smoochum is normally cheaper. Copy it only when direct access is absent
  and two Basic attachments are worth the Slowking attack turn.

SME CHECK: Seek Inspiration copying another Seek Inspiration appears in
community ruling questions, but no clear current official answer was found in
the reviewed public sources. This pilot never depends on recursive Seek
Inspiration. If Slowking is the revealed source, Super Psy Bolt is the
unambiguous default pending an event judge ruling.


ENERGY AND RECOVERY

Basic Psychic Energy

LIST FACT: There are only four. They are the sole Energy that Smoochum can
search, Wondrous Patch can accelerate, and Night Stretcher can return.

INFERENCE: Track all four across deck, hand, discard, prizes inferred from a
legal search, and play. Deliberately discarding one with Ultra Ball or Secret
Box can turn on Wondrous Patch, but only when the resulting Bench attachment
is more valuable than preserving the manual attachment.

Telepathic Psychic Energy POR 88

SOURCE: It provides Psychic while attached. When attached from hand to a
Psychic Pokémon, it searches up to two Basic Psychic Pokémon directly to the
Bench. [S11, S23]

INFERENCE: In this list its search targets are Slowpoke, Smoochum, and Latias
ex. It cannot search Slowking, Mega Kangaskhan, Kyurem, Fezandipiti, Meowth,
Conkeldurr, or Annihilape. Prefer attaching it to a Slowpoke or another
Psychic Pokémon when the two-Basic setup and deck thinning matter. It is
Special Energy: Wondrous Patch, Night Stretcher, and Delightful Kiss cannot
recover or search it.

Boomerang Energy

SOURCE: It provides Colorless. If an attack effect used by its attached
Pokémon discards it, it reattaches to that Pokémon after attacking. [S12]

INFERENCE: Boomerang is the clean Colorless partner for Trifrost, but its
protection is narrow:

- Trifrost discards it: it returns.
- Crushing Hammer discards it: it does not return.
- retreat discards it: it does not return.
- Ultra Ball or Secret Box discards it from hand: it does not return.
- Slowking is Knocked Out: it does not return.
- Destined Fight Knocks Out Slowking: it does not return.

After Trifrost, a returned Boomerang plus a manual Psychic attachment can
restore Seek Inspiration on the next turn. If Slowking is expected to be
Knocked Out, spend Items before attacking to prepare the next Benched line
rather than trying to protect an Active line that will disappear.

Wondrous Patch

SOURCE: Wondrous Patch attaches a Basic Psychic Energy from the discard pile
to a Benched Psychic Pokémon. [S17]

INFERENCE: It can accelerate a Benched Slowpoke, Slowking, Smoochum, or Latias
ex. It cannot attach to the Active Pokémon, cannot target non-Psychic
Pokémon, and cannot recover either Special Energy. The best use completes a
visible next attacker this turn or establishes the next Slowking before the
Active is lost.

Smoochum

SOURCE: Delightful Kiss costs no Energy, searches up to two Basic Psychic
Energy, and attaches both to one Benched Pokémon. Smoochum has free Retreat.
Grant Manley's July analysis specifically identifies Smoochum as a major
reason Slowking can produce more early Trifrost attacks and resist Crushing
Hammer pressure. [S9, S19]

INFERENCE: A strong going-second pattern is:

1. start or pivot to Smoochum;
2. establish two Slowpoke;
3. attach Telepathic Psychic Energy to one Slowpoke, using it to find Basics;
4. use Delightful Kiss to attach two Basic Psychic Energy to the other
   Slowpoke; and
5. on the next turn, evolve the two-Basic Slowpoke into an attack-ready
   Slowking while the Telepathic Slowpoke remains the backup line.

Delightful Kiss may attach to any Benched Pokémon, not only Psychic Pokémon.
That creates a real but expensive anti-Shaymin line: put two Basic Psychic
Energy on Fezandipiti ex, attach a third Energy later, and use Cruel Arrow for
100 to Knock Out an 80-HP Shaymin on the Bench. This is an inference unique
to the owner's list, not a published matchup guarantee. It exposes a
two-Prize attacker and consumes most of the Basic Energy engine, so calculate
the full prize map first.

Night Stretcher

SOURCE: Night Stretcher returns either a Pokémon or a Basic Energy from the
discard pile to hand. [S18]

INFERENCE: Its jobs compete:

- recover Slowking or Slowpoke to preserve the attack chain;
- recover Kyurem, Conkeldurr, or Annihilape so Academy can put it back on top;
- recover Smoochum for a second acceleration turn;
- recover Fezandipiti or another needed support Pokémon; or
- recover Basic Psychic Energy for a manual attachment.

Do not spend Stretcher on the first available target. Identify which finite
resource currently blocks the next prize-taking attack.

Counter Gain

SOURCE: While the player has more Prize cards remaining than the opponent,
Counter Gain reduces the attached Pokémon's attack cost by one Colorless.
[S32]

INFERENCE: On Slowking, active Counter Gain turns Psychic-Colorless into one
Psychic while behind. This is the deck's cleanest recovery from a lost
Boomerang or an Energy-denial turn. It stops helping if the prize counts cease
to satisfy its condition. Attach it for an exact attack or mapped follow-up,
not merely because the player is currently behind.

Secret Box

SOURCE: Secret Box discards three other cards from hand, then searches an
Item, Tool, Supporter, and Stadium. [S24]

INFERENCE: The exact sixty can turn Secret Box into a complete combo bridge:

- Item: Poké Pad, Ultra Ball, Night Stretcher, or Wondrous Patch;
- Tool: the lone Counter Gain;
- Supporter: Ciphermaniac or Lillie's Determination; and
- Stadium: Academy at Night.

A particularly coherent line discards a Basic Psychic Energy, searches
Wondrous Patch plus Counter Gain plus Ciphermaniac plus Academy, accelerates a
Benched Psychic attacker, and finishes with the required top lock. It is not
automatically correct: the three-card discard can consume the next attack
source, a second Slowking line, or irreplaceable Special Energy.


SETUP AND BENCH GEOMETRY

Opening priorities

INFERENCE: A stable first board normally wants:

- two Slowpoke;
- one mobility/draw bridge, usually Mega Kangaskhan plus Latias;
- Smoochum when Delightful Kiss will be used; and
- at least one open Bench slot for Meowth, Fezandipiti, or a recovered
  attacker.

Do not fill all five Bench slots just because Telepathic Psychic Energy can.
The exact list contains five different multi-Prize support cards and two
fragile Smoochum. Every Bench slot changes both the setup ceiling and the
opponent's prize route.

Starting Pokémon

Mega Kangaskhan ex

SOURCE: Run Errand draws two once per turn while Mega Kangaskhan is Active.
Latias ex gives Basic Pokémon in play free Retreat. [S25, S26]

INFERENCE: Mega Kangaskhan is the best blind Active when Run Errand plus a
Latias pivot is live. It is a dangerous permanent Bench resident because a
Mega Pokémon ex gives up three Prizes when Knocked Out. One copy in play is
usually enough; the second copy is consistency, not permission to expose six
Prizes.

Smoochum

INFERENCE: Smoochum is the preferred going-second Active when Delightful Kiss
will power a Benched Slowpoke and the opponent cannot take a more damaging
immediate line. Its 30 HP makes it an easy spread Prize after the setup turn.

Slowpoke

INFERENCE: Slowpoke is an acceptable Active only when another Slowpoke is
safe on the Bench and the evolution/retreat line is known. Losing the sole
Slowpoke ends the engine. Latias can retreat it for free while it remains
Basic.

Latias, Fezandipiti, and Meowth

INFERENCE: Avoid blind-starting narrow two-Prize support Pokémon. Latias is
the mobility bridge; Fezandipiti is the post-Knock-Out draw bridge; Meowth is
the one-time Supporter bridge. Each should enter play because its printed job
changes the current or mapped next turn.


GOING FIRST

INFERENCE: Going first gives the earliest legal Stage 1 attack:

Turn one

- establish two Slowpoke if possible;
- attach Psychic or Boomerang to the intended first Slowpoke;
- use Telepathic Psychic Energy on a Psychic Pokémon if its Basic search is
  valuable;
- place Mega Kangaskhan Active only with a pivot plan; and
- preserve the attack source in hand for Academy.

Turn two

- evolve the intended Slowpoke;
- finish the Psychic-Colorless requirement;
- perform every shuffle and draw;
- use Run Errand before locking the top;
- use Ciphermaniac or Academy to guarantee the chosen source; and
- attack with the line that changes the prize map, not automatically
  Trifrost.

The cost of going first is no first-turn Supporter and no Delightful Kiss.
Hands that cannot establish two attachments or find Slowking may value going
second more.


GOING SECOND

SOURCE: The published July expert analysis calls Smoochum consistent,
efficient, and important into Hammer-heavy Dragapult. [S19]

INFERENCE: Going second is the deliberate acceleration/consistency line:

Turn one

- use Lillie's Determination for eight while exactly six Prizes remain, or
  use Ciphermaniac for an exact Run Errand setup;
- use Telepathic Psychic Energy to establish Slowpoke and another Basic;
- pivot to Smoochum;
- use Delightful Kiss to put two Basic Psychic Energy on a Benched Slowpoke;
  and
- leave a second Slowpoke with at least one attachment when possible.

Turn two

- evolve the two-Energy Slowpoke;
- use the Supporter and draw sequence;
- stack the attack source last; and
- attack while preparing the second line.

The tradeoff is initiative: the opponent receives two turns before the first
Seek Inspiration. Choose second only when the two Basic attachments,
first-turn Supporter, and Hammer resilience outweigh that delay.

SME CHECK: No controlled current-format first-versus-second study for this
exact sixty was found. The default above is a reasoned plan, not a proven
universal choice.


PRIZE AND BENCH MAPPING

SOURCE: Grant Manley's July testing found that Slowking can struggle against
two-Prize decks because its engine exposes Mega Kangaskhan, Latias, and
Fezandipiti. He identifies the opponent's 3-2-1 route—Mega Kangaskhan, a
two-Prize support, then Slowking—as a structural problem. [S19]

INFERENCE: Recalculate the opponent's shortest route before every support
Pokémon:

- Mega Kangaskhan ex: three Prizes;
- Latias ex, Fezandipiti ex, or Meowth ex: two Prizes;
- Slowking, Slowpoke, Smoochum, Kyurem, Conkeldurr, or Annihilape if somehow
  in play: one Prize.

The common exposed route is exactly 3-2-1. Avoid giving the opponent multiple
equivalent Boss targets:

- bench one Mega Kangaskhan, not two;
- bench Meowth only when the searched Supporter matters;
- bench Fezandipiti only when Flip the Script materially repairs the hand;
- preserve the second Latias in hand unless mobility must survive a first
  knockout; and
- after using Smoochum, remember that its 30 HP is still an easy one-Prize
  target.

Your attacking prize maps

Against three-Prize Mega Pokémon ex:

- Destined Fight can trade one Prize for three;
- Trifrost plus Gutsy Swing reaches 360 before modifiers; or
- direct Mega Kangaskhan can attack only when the coin-dependent line is
  actually superior.

Against ordinary two-Prize Pokémon ex:

- Gutsy Swing takes those at 250 or less;
- Trifrost softens higher-HP targets; and
- Destined Fight is the clean HP-independent trade when the opponent cannot
  exploit the simultaneous knockout.

Against single-Prize decks:

- prioritize multi-KO Trifrost;
- avoid Destined Fight for a fair one-for-one unless it removes an otherwise
  unbeatable Active;
- minimize support ex; and
- recognize that a Shaymin-protected board can invalidate the normal prize
  advantage.


MATCHUP PLANS

Evidence caveat

SOURCE: The public POR online aggregate contains 1,846 reported Slowking
matches at 49.02%, but it mixes lists, players, event structures, and a
pre-Pitch-Black card pool. It reports materially different results across
Dragapult variants, 34.44% into Alakazam/Dudunsparce, 82.35% into Crustle,
45.56% into Rocket's Mewtwo, and 60.98% into Raging Bolt/Ogerpon. These are
signals, not exact-list causal estimates. [S27]

The owner's screenshot reports 66.2% over 136 matches for this exact-looking
Conkeldurr list, but the underlying rows and filters are unavailable. Do not
combine those percentages.

Dragapult variants

SOURCE: Current published assessments disagree by build. The online aggregate
reports 44.72% into straight Dragapult, 57.01% into Dragapult/Dusknoir, and
41.94% into Dragapult/Blaziken. After further testing, Grant Manley judged
many Dragapult builds even or slightly favorable for Slowking, while stressing
that Hammer, Watchtower, Special Red Card, Shaymin, and pilot errors change
the matchup sharply. [S19, S27]

Plan:

1. Establish two Slowpoke. Dusknoir and Phantom Dive punish a lone line.
2. Use Smoochum early when Hammer pressure makes Basic attachments more
   reliable than one exposed Boomerang.
3. Seek the earliest high-value Trifrost into Dreepy, Drakloak, Budew, or
   other low-HP setup Pokémon.
4. Check Shaymin before declaring the spread. Flower Curtain protects
   Benched non-Rule-Box Pokémon, though it does not protect Rule-Box Pokémon
   or the Active. [S20]
5. Replace Team Rocket's Watchtower with Academy before using Run Errand;
   Watchtower removes Abilities from Colorless Pokémon, including Mega
   Kangaskhan and Meowth. [S21]
6. Use every Item before moving an Energy-empty Slowking Active under Budew
   Item lock. The exact list lacks Powerglass and Switch, so a stranded
   Active is worse here than in common guide lists.
7. After Trifrost, expect only Boomerang to return. Prepare the next Benched
   Slowking with Wondrous Patch before attacking.
8. When the opponent reaches three or fewer Prizes, Ciphermaniac can put the
   next attack source on top and a useful card beneath it. Seek discards the
   source; the useful card remains above hand disruption such as Special Red
   Card. [S28]

Do not claim a generic favorable matchup. Identify the opposing variant and
its visible techs.

Alakazam/Dudunsparce and other single-Prize engines

SOURCE: The online aggregate reports 34.44% for Slowking into
Alakazam/Dudunsparce. Published guides identify Shaymin as the central
problem: Flower Curtain blocks Trifrost damage to Benched non-Rule-Box
Pokémon, and single-Prize opponents can then win the fair exchange. [S19,
S20, S27, S31]

Plan:

1. Trifrost before Shaymin reaches play when the visible board offers
   multiple immediate prizes.
2. If Shaymin is present, stop pretending Trifrost hits protected targets.
   Gutsy Swing the Active or use Super Psy Bolt/Tantrum for exact small
   knockouts.
3. Avoid Destined Fight for ordinary single-Prize trades.
4. Keep Mega Kangaskhan, Latias, Meowth, and Fezandipiti exposure to the
   minimum functional board.
5. Consider the direct Fezandipiti Cruel Arrow line only when Smoochum or
   manual attachments can reach three Energy and removing Shaymin unlocks
   enough future prizes to compensate for exposing Fezandipiti.
6. With no gust card in the exact sixty, accept that the opponent may keep
   Shaymin Benched and that the matchup can remain structurally poor.

Crustle

SOURCE: Crustle DRI 12 prevents damage done to itself by attacks from Pokémon
ex. Slowking is not a Pokémon ex, so its copied attacks are not stopped by
Mysterious Rock Inn. The public aggregate reports 82.35% over 51 matches for
Slowking, though lists vary. [S22, S27]

Plan:

1. Use Trifrost early on Dwebble or other small setup targets.
2. Attack Crustle with Slowking, not a direct Pokémon ex attacker.
3. Gutsy Swing's 250 cleanly clears ordinary 150-HP Crustle and reaches an
   exact 250 if a +100 HP Tool is present, before any other modifiers.
4. Preserve Academy against competing Stadiums.
5. Use Destined Fight only when damage modifiers or effects make Gutsy Swing
   fail; the normal damage route preserves Slowking.

Big-Basic and multi-Prize box decks

SOURCE: Public expert testing warns that these decks can map 3-2-1 through
Slowking's support board even though Slowking itself is single-Prize. The POR
aggregate reports 60.98% into Raging Bolt/Ogerpon but only 16.67% into the
small Ogerpon Box sample, illustrating why the exact opposing composition
matters. [S19, S27]

Plan:

1. Expose the smallest functional support board.
2. Map a Destined Fight into every Active two- or three-Prize Pokémon that
   cannot be cleanly removed by 250.
3. Use Trifrost when three Rule-Box targets are available; Shaymin does not
   protect Benched Rule-Box Pokémon.
4. Use one 110 spread plus Gutsy Swing to reach 360 when the opponent cannot
   heal, evolve out of range, or change the target.
5. Do not bench the second Mega Kangaskhan. A three-Prize setup Pokémon is a
   liability if the opponent can one-shot it.
6. Avoid a Destined Fight that gives the opponent its last Prize unless the
   exact simultaneous-win resolver says the outcome is acceptable.

Rocket's Mewtwo and Watchtower decks

SOURCE: Team Rocket's Watchtower turns off Abilities of Colorless Pokémon.
The public aggregate reports 45.56% for Slowking into Rocket's Mewtwo.
[S21, S27]

Plan:

1. Preserve an Academy to replace Watchtower before Run Errand or Last-Ditch
   Catch.
2. Trifrost low-HP Team Rocket setup Pokémon when visible protection does not
   stop it.
3. Use Gutsy Swing for targets within 250 and Destined Fight for Mewtwo-sized
   targets beyond the clean threshold.
4. Keep the second Slowpoke line out of easy multi-target damage when the
   opposing build includes Spidops or other spread branches.

Pitch Black Bastiodon shells

SOURCE: Bastiodon PBL 62's Ancient Bulwark prevents all damage done to the
opponent's Pokémon by attacks from an opposing Pokémon with two or fewer
Energy attached, while Bastiodon is on the Bench. [S29]

Plan:

1. Count attached Energy cards, not the attack's reduced cost.
2. Attach a third Energy to Slowking before using Trifrost, Gutsy Swing,
   Tantrum, or Super Psy Bolt through Ancient Bulwark.
3. Counter Gain makes Seek Inspiration cheaper but does not make a
   two-Energy Slowking pass the three-Energy check.
4. If three attachments are unavailable, Destined Fight is a Knock-Out
   effect rather than damage; verify no separate effect protection applies.
5. Remember that Trifrost discards all three Energy. Boomerang may return,
   but the reload cost increases.

Slowking mirror

Plan:

1. Initiative and redundant Slowpoke matter more than raw support Pokémon.
2. Trifrost 110 misses an undamaged 120-HP Slowking by 10 but clears many
   Slowpoke and Smoochum.
3. Super Psy Bolt copied from a top-decked Slowking reaches 120 exactly; use
   it only when spending an evolution card is better than Gutsy Swing.
4. Tantrum reaches 130 but leaves Slowking Confused.
5. Avoid giving the opponent three clean Trifrost targets.
6. Preserve Night Stretcher for the Slowking line if both players exchange
   attackers every turn.


RECOVERY LINES

Academy is bumped

- Play another Academy after all desired searches.
- If Academy is unavailable, Ciphermaniac can lock the top directly, but do
  not draw or shuffle afterward.
- Do not use Seek Inspiration on an unknown top card when a deterministic
  recovery turn is available unless the game state forces the gamble.

The copied attacker is in the discard

- Night Stretcher it to hand, then Academy it to the top.
- If Night Stretcher is more urgently needed for Slowking, use the second
  physical copy of Kyurem, Conkeldurr, or Annihilape from deck/hand.
- Dangle Tail can recover a Pokémon through an attack when Items are locked,
  but it sacrifices the damage turn.

No second Energy

- If behind, Counter Gain plus one Psychic pays Seek Inspiration.
- Use Wondrous Patch on a Benched Psychic Pokémon, never the Active.
- Use Smoochum to put two Basics on one Benched attacker.
- If the Active Slowking has returned Boomerang after Trifrost, a manual
  Psychic attachment restores the attack.

Boomerang is removed

- Do not expect it to return after Hammer.
- Build with two Psychic Energy, one of which may be Telepathic.
- Use Counter Gain while behind.
- Prefer Basic Psychic on the Bench because Patch, Stretcher, and Smoochum
  can interact with it.

The hand is disrupted

- Before Special Red Card is live, use Ciphermaniac to place the attack
  source on top and a useful next draw beneath it.
- Use Meowth's Last-Ditch Catch only after Watchtower is gone and only if the
  chosen Supporter changes the current turn.
- Bench Fezandipiti after an opponent's Knock Out when drawing three is worth
  exposing two Prizes.

Slowking is stranded Active

- Latias does not give Stage 1 Slowking free Retreat.
- Wondrous Patch cannot attach to the Active.
- A returned Boomerang plus manual Psychic can restore Seek after Trifrost.
- Paying three Retreat can be correct only if the replacement attack and
  remaining Energy map justify losing all three cards.
- Under Item lock, prioritize manual attachment and a productive attack over
  a speculative retreat plan.

TURN CHECKLIST

Before setup

- Which Basic is the safest Active?
- Is this a going-first evolution race or a going-second Smoochum plan?
- Can two Slowpoke be established?
- Is one Bench slot reserved?
- Which multi-Prize support Pokémon has an actual job?

At the first legal deck search

- How many Slowpoke and Slowking are accessible?
- Are both Kyurem, both Conkeldurr, and both Annihilape accounted for?
- Where are the four Basic Psychic, four Telepathic, and two Boomerang?
- Are Counter Gain, Secret Box, and enough Academy available?
- Which apparent absences can be inferred as Prized only from this legal
  search?

Before a draw or shuffle

- Is the top card currently known?
- Has Academy already been used?
- Will Run Errand consume a needed copy target?
- Is Ciphermaniac in attack-lock mode or Kangaskhan-tutor mode?
- Are all Ultra Ball, Poké Pad, Telepathic, and Stretcher actions complete?

Before attaching Energy

- Does Telepathic trigger on this target?
- Is Basic Psychic more valuable in discard for Wondrous Patch?
- Is Boomerang reserved for Trifrost?
- Is Counter Gain currently active by Prize count?
- Is a visible Bastiodon demanding three attached Energy cards?
- Which Benched Slowking attacks next?

Before choosing the copied attacker

- How many immediate Prizes does Trifrost take?
- Which three targets are legal and unprotected?
- Does Gutsy Swing's adjusted 250 take the Active knockout?
- Is Destined Fight necessary?
- What Prize does each player take after Destined Fight?
- Does effect prevention apply?
- Is a 120 or 130 attack sufficient without spending a more important copy
  target?

Before benching an ex

- What printed Ability or attack is used now?
- How many Prizes does it expose?
- Does it create a 3-2-1 route?
- Can the same job be done by an existing Pokémon?
- Is Watchtower disabling it?

Before Seek Inspiration

- Is the top card known and a non-Rule-Box Pokémon?
- Were all draws and shuffles completed?
- Is the correct attack selected?
- Were Weakness, Resistance, Tools, and prevention effects applied to the
  attacking Slowking?
- What Energy is discarded?
- Does Boomerang actually meet its return condition?
- Is the next attacker ready?

At end of turn

- What is the opponent's shortest visible prize route?
- Which Slowpoke survives the next public damage map?
- Which attack source must return to hand?
- Which Basic Psychic can be patched or stretched?
- What top card remains after Seek for the next draw?
