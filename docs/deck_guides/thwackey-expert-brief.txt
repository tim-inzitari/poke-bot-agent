THWACKEY / FESTIVAL LEAD
PRACTICAL PILOT GUIDE FOR SUBJECT-MATTER-EXPERT REVIEW

Guide identity: thwackey
Compatible physical matchup route and corpus identity: festival-lead
Guide version: thwackey-festival-lead-north-star-v1
Prepared: 2026-07-27
Status: research draft for world-champion SME review

PURPOSE AND SCOPE

This is a how-to-play guide for the current Thwackey / Festival Lead
single-Prize deck. It uses the current post-rotation shell documented by
Natalie Millar, recent tournament lists collected by Limitless, the exact card
text, and current Festival Lead analysis. It is not a claim that every flex
slot is solved. The list changes rapidly, so the pilot should preserve the
engine principles while updating the protection and damage-modifier package
for the event.

The project calls the logical specialist "thwackey" because Thwackey is the
deck's repeatable search engine. The actual attacking shell is conventionally
called Festival Lead. Those names refer to the same strategy here. Do not
confuse this with a generic Grass pile or Hydrapple deck that happens to play
Dipplin.

THE CORE PLAN

The deck wins by combining three effects:

1. A Festival Lead Pokémon is Active.
2. One or more Thwackey use Boom Boom Groove to search exact combo pieces.
3. Festival Grounds is in play, allowing Festival Lead attackers to attack
   twice.

Dipplin is the main attacker. Do the Wave does 20 damage for each of your
Benched Pokémon. A full five-Pokémon Bench therefore gives 100 damage before
modifiers. Festival Lead allows Dipplin to attack twice while Festival Grounds
is in play. If the first attack Knocks Out the opposing Active, the opponent
promotes and Dipplin can attack the new Active. This creates two distinct
prize-taking modes:

- concentrate both attacks into one large target; or
- Knock Out a smaller Active with the first attack and use the second attack
  against the newly promoted Pokémon.

This is a one-Prize tempo deck. Your ideal game is not to build one durable
attacker. It is to present a new one-Prize Festival Lead attacker every turn,
take one- or two-Prize knockouts efficiently, and force a multi-Prize opponent
to win an unfavorable exchange. A developed Thwackey engine converts each turn
from "draw the combination" into "search the exact missing cards."

CORE CARDS AND WHAT THEY DO

Grookey and Thwackey

Thwackey's Boom Boom Groove may be used once during your turn if your Active
Pokémon has the Festival Lead Ability. It searches any one card. Multiple
Thwackey can each use their Ability once. A two-Thwackey board usually gives
enough precise search to assemble the attacker, Energy, Stadium, damage
modifier, recovery, or gust effect needed that turn.

Protect the engine, but do not preserve it at the cost of never attacking.
Thwackey is also a liability when gusted Active because it has Retreat Cost
two. Millar specifically identifies Boss's Orders on Thwackey as a major way
opponents buy time. Air Balloon, Kieran, an Energy attachment plan, or another
switching out should be mapped before committing the turn.

Applin and Dipplin

Dipplin's Do the Wave is the primary attack. Its damage depends on your own
Bench, so each empty Bench space costs 20 damage per attack, or 40 total damage
over the doubled attack. Do not treat Bench space as free. A support Pokémon
must justify both its slot and any damage it displaces.

Keep a replacement Applin or Dipplin available whenever the opponent can
Knock Out the Active. The deck is strongest when the next attacker, not just
the current attacker, is already represented on the board.

Festival Grounds

Festival Grounds is what permits the doubled attack. It is not merely a
generic Stadium. Before attacking, verify that the correct Stadium is still in
play. Because opposing Stadium replacement turns off the double-attack plan,
preserve access to another Festival Grounds when the matchup can remove it.

The Stadium's printed effect also clears and prevents Special Conditions on
Pokémon with Energy attached. That can matter, but it is secondary to enabling
Festival Lead.

Goldeen and Seaking

Goldeen has Festival Lead while still a Basic. This matters because it can
turn on Boom Boom Groove before Dipplin is established. It is often a clean
opening Active and can bridge awkward setups. Current lists also use Seaking
as a backup Festival Lead attacker. The exact Seaking print varies by list;
confirm its attack text before the event rather than assuming an old list's
math.

Search package

Buddy-Buddy Poffin builds the low-HP Basic board. Bug Catching Set looks at the
top seven cards and can take up to two Grass Pokémon and/or Basic Grass Energy,
which makes it strong but non-deterministic. Boom Boom Groove is deterministic
once active.

Secret Box is the leading ACE SPEC in the cited strategy guide. It turns one
Boom Boom Groove search into access to an Item, Tool, Supporter, and Stadium,
at the cost of discarding three other cards. Millar's guide emphasizes that
this can assemble an entire knockout turn, especially when only one Thwackey
is available. Plan all four categories before paying the discard cost. Do not
discard a recovery card or the only replacement attacker merely because Secret
Box can find the current-turn damage package.

Damage modifiers

Brave Bangle adds damage against Pokémon ex when attached to a Pokémon without
a Rule Box. Kieran and Black Belt's Training can also add damage against
eligible opposing Pokémon. Because Dipplin attacks twice, a modifier that
applies to attacks can contribute on both attacks. This multiplication is the
deck's key knockout mechanism.

Use exact math. With five Benched Pokémon, Do the Wave begins at 100. A +30
modifier makes each attack 130; a +40 modifier makes each attack 140. Multiple
legal modifiers can raise the number further. Weakness, Resistance, the
opponent's remaining HP, and whether the first attack changes the Active all
matter. Never call a two-attack knockout from the printed base number alone.

OPENING SETUP

Before choosing the opening Active, inspect the hand for:

- a Festival Lead Active now or an immediate Dipplin route;
- at least one Grookey route;
- at least one Applin route;
- a way to fill the Bench;
- a Festival Grounds route;
- the first attacker and its Energy;
- whether a prized or missing piece changes the plan.

Goldeen is generally the cleanest opening Active because Festival Lead is
already online, which makes future Thwackey immediately useful. An Applin start
is acceptable if it becomes Dipplin on schedule. Avoid opening Thwackey's line
Active when possible; Grookey does not turn on Boom Boom Groove, and a stranded
Thwackey later costs two to retreat.

The default early Bench is built around two engine lines and two attacker
lines:

- two Grookey that can become Thwackey;
- two Applin that can become Dipplin;
- the remaining space reserved for an additional attacker, Festival Lead
  pivot, or matchup-specific protector.

This is a target, not an automatic law. If the opponent has immediate spread
or multi-Knock-Out pressure, exposing four fragile Basics may be worse than
staging one protected line at a time. If the opponent cannot punish the Bench,
filling it raises Do the Wave damage and improves redundancy.

GOING FIRST

Going first is the natural default for an evolution-heavy engine: establish
Grookey and Applin, then evolve into Thwackey and Dipplin on the next turn.
Your first turn cannot be evaluated only by hand size. The success criterion is
whether the next turn can produce:

- a Festival Lead Active;
- at least one live Boom Boom Groove;
- Festival Grounds;
- one Energy on the attacker; and
- enough Bench Pokémon and modifiers for the required damage.

Attach Energy with the next turn's pivot in mind. If the current Active is a
likely Boss's Orders or retreat liability, preserve the switching route.

GOING SECOND

Going second gives access to an attack on the first turn, but the central
Stage 1 engine still cannot evolve immediately. Use the extra action only when
it improves the real setup. A low-value attack should not consume the Energy or
Active position needed for the first meaningful Dipplin turn.

Against a slow opponent, prioritize the same redundant board as when going
first. Against immediate Item lock or aggressive Basic pressure, use Poffin and
other Item search before the lock arrives, and consider whether the first
attack protects tempo. There is not enough public evidence to claim a universal
first/second choice for the current list; the first-turn default should be
confirmed by SME testing and matchup data.

TURN SEQUENCING

Use this sequence as a baseline:

1. Read the current prize map and opponent's possible response.
2. Count available Festival Lead attackers, Grookey, Thwackey, and recovery.
3. Confirm whether a Festival Lead Pokémon can remain or become Active.
4. Use non-search information first when it can change what Boom Boom Groove
   should find.
5. Activate every Thwackey that is legal and useful before evolving it away or
   changing the Active to a Pokémon without Festival Lead.
6. Search the most constrained piece first.
7. Establish or replace Festival Grounds.
8. Attach Energy and Tool to the intended attacker.
9. Play the Supporter only after confirming whether it is needed for damage,
   gust, pivoting, or recovery.
10. Recalculate damage for the first and second attacks separately.
11. Preserve the next attacker and at least one route out of a gusted
    Thwackey.

The "most constrained piece" is the card with the fewest remaining routes. If
Festival Grounds is absent and only Boom Boom Groove can guarantee it, search
the Stadium before spending that search on a card Bug Catching Set or Poffin
can find. If Secret Box is available, identify its four search categories
before choosing the first Boom target.

ATTACK AND PRIZE PLANNING

At the start of every attack turn, write the prize plan in concrete numbers:

- prizes remaining for both players;
- first-attack damage;
- second-attack damage;
- prizes available from the current Active;
- likely promoted targets if the first attack Knocks Out;
- whether Boss's Orders or another gust changes the shortest route.

When both attacks are needed to Knock Out one target, compare that exchange
against taking a smaller Knock Out first. Festival Lead explicitly permits a
second attack after the opponent promotes, so a two-target line can outperform
overkilling a large Active.

Do not assume the opponent will promote the target you want. If the second
attack must hit a particular Pokémon, gusting before the first attack or
changing the prize order may be necessary. Conversely, if every promotion
loses a Prize, attacking the current small target is powerful.

Maintain the one-Prize exchange. Benching a multi-Prize tech changes the
opponent's route and should be justified by a concrete matchup need, not by
generic power.

RESOURCE MANAGEMENT AND RECOVERY

The deck's Energy count is low in current lists, but each main attack costs
only one Grass Energy. Track Energy in the board, discard, deck, and prizes.
Do not strand an Energy on Thwackey unless it is part of the retreat plan.

Track these resources explicitly:

- remaining Festival Grounds;
- remaining Applin and Dipplin;
- remaining Grookey and Thwackey;
- recovery cards such as Night Stretcher or Lana's Aid;
- remaining damage modifiers;
- remaining gust effects;
- Secret Box and the cards it would need to discard;
- pivot cards for a gusted Thwackey.

Recovery is a role choice. In most games, recovering the next Dipplin line is
more important than recovering a support Pokémon. In spread matchups, Rabsca
or Shaymin may become the critical recovery target. Decide from the opponent's
actual route to six Prizes.

BENCH MANAGEMENT

Every Bench slot carries three values:

- 20 base damage for each Do the Wave;
- the function of the Pokémon occupying it;
- the risk that the opponent can take a Prize from it.

Two Thwackey plus two Dipplin lines is a strong stable core. The fifth slot can
be a replacement attacker, Goldeen, Seaking, Shaymin, Rabsca line, Psyduck, or
another tech depending on the matchup. Do not fill the final slot before
checking whether a protector or pivot will be mandatory.

Protection pieces overlap but are not identical. Rabsca prevents both damage
and effects of attacks to the Bench. Shaymin prevents attack damage to Benched
Pokémon without Rule Boxes. Millar's cited Dragapult plan uses Psyduck plus
Rabsca, while also warning that the package constricts the board to roughly
two Dipplin and two Thwackey lines. Confirm the exact card texts and current
opposing threats before selecting the protection package.

MATCHUP PLANS

Dragapult variants

The matchup turns on preserving the fragile Bench while maintaining enough
slots for damage. Rabsca is the broadest attack-based protection. Shaymin is
useful against attack damage to non-Rule-Box Benched Pokémon, but it does not
cover every effect. The cited TCGplayer guide identifies Psyduck plus Rabsca as
a strong wall against Dragapult/Dusknoir, with the explicit cost of a cramped
Bench.

Build the protection line before the opponent's spread turn, not afterward.
Keep two attacker and two engine lines where possible. Use exact modifiers to
one-shot Dragapult ex; the one-Prize-for-two-Prize exchange is how Festival
Lead compensates for its fragile board.

Be aware of Thwackey gust traps. A Dragapult player can buy a turn by bringing
Thwackey Active, and some lines can pressure two Dipplin at once. Air Balloon,
Kieran, or a mapped Energy retreat prevents a search engine from becoming a
tempo loss.

Two-Prize decks

This is the deck's preferred exchange. Develop the board quickly, calculate
the modifier needed to Knock Out an ex, and trade one-Prize Dipplin for two
Prizes. Do not bench an unnecessary multi-Prize Pokémon. If the opponent cannot
take multiple Prizes in a turn, a continuous stream of attackers should force
them to win three attacks after you need only three effective knockout turns.

Single-Prize decks

The prize advantage disappears. Focus on double-knockout possibilities and
engine efficiency. A first attack that Knocks Out a small Active followed by a
second attack into the new Active can create the tempo advantage that raw prize
value does not provide.

Item lock

Use Poffin, Bug Catching Set, Secret Box, and other critical Items before the
lock when possible. Thwackey remains valuable because Boom Boom Groove itself
is an Ability, but the card it finds must still be playable. Search for the
piece that remains legal under the current lock rather than a theoretical
future combo.

Spread and bench-snipe decks

Identify whether the threat is attack damage, an attack effect, an Ability
effect, or damage counters. Rabsca and Shaymin cover different subsets. Never
assume "Bench protection" is universal. If protection is ineffective against
the actual text, reduce exposed Basics and prioritize the prize race.

RECOVERY LINES

If the Active Dipplin is Knocked Out:

1. Promote a pivot that preserves access to Boom Boom Groove if possible.
2. Count remaining attacker lines before searching.
3. Use Thwackey to find the scarcest missing component.
4. Recover only the piece needed for the next complete attacker.
5. Restore Festival Grounds if it was removed.
6. Attach Energy and rebuild the next replacement before attacking.

If a Thwackey is gusted:

1. Use every legal Boom Boom Groove before moving it if the Active still has
   Festival Lead; if Thwackey is Active, the condition is normally off.
2. Check Air Balloon, Kieran's switch mode, Switch, or manual retreat.
3. Do not spend the turn building damage until the Active is solved.
4. After pivoting, recalculate whether the remaining search and Supporter use
   can still reach the knockout.

If Festival Grounds is removed:

1. Treat the replacement Stadium as the first constrained combo piece.
2. Use Boom Boom Groove for Festival Grounds if no other guaranteed route
   exists.
3. Do not announce a double-attack knockout until the Stadium is actually in
   play.

COMMON MISTAKES

- Starting or benching too many Grookey while leaving no Applin replacement.
- Evolving the only Festival Lead Active without preserving another active
  Festival Lead Pokémon.
- Forgetting that Boom Boom Groove requires a Festival Lead Pokémon Active.
- Treating Festival Grounds as optional after calculating two attacks.
- Leaving Bench slots empty and then using damage numbers from a full Bench.
- Using a damage modifier without applying its target restriction.
- Spending both attacks on one target when the first attack could take a Prize
  and unlock a second target.
- Assuming the opponent will promote the desired second target.
- Discarding irreplaceable recovery or attackers to Secret Box.
- Using all Boom Boom Groove searches before gathering information from the
  hand or top-deck effects.
- Allowing Thwackey to be trapped Active without a pivot route.
- Benching every protection Pokémon and losing the space required for damage
  and replacement attackers.
- Assuming Rabsca, Shaymin, or Psyduck covers a threat without reading whether
  it is damage, effects, Abilities, or counters.
- Recovering a support Pokémon when the immediate loss condition is having no
  attacker.

TURN CHECKLIST

Before search:

- Is a Festival Lead Pokémon Active?
- How many Boom Boom Groove uses are available?
- What information can I reveal before choosing search targets?
- What is the one card no other effect can guarantee?

Before committing the Bench:

- How many Grookey/Thwackey lines are live?
- How many Applin/Dipplin lines are live?
- Does the fifth slot need to remain open?
- What exact spread or gust punish can the opponent use?

Before attacking:

- Is Festival Grounds in play?
- Is the correct attacker Active with the correct Energy?
- What is Do the Wave's base damage from my current Bench?
- Which modifiers legally apply to attack one and attack two?
- Can attack one take a Knock Out?
- What can the opponent promote?
- Is there a higher-prize two-target route?
- Is next turn's attacker protected?

After attacking:

- What piece is most likely to be removed?
- What Stadium, Energy, attacker, and recovery remain?
- Can a gusted Thwackey be moved?
- What is the opponent's shortest remaining prize route?

EVIDENCE GAPS FOR SME REVIEW

The following should be resolved by expert testing rather than asserted:

- universal choice to go first or second in the current format;
- ideal two-versus-three Thwackey board across matchups;
- exact current flex package after the newest set;
- whether Secret Box remains optimal over every competing ACE SPEC;
- protection package by current Dragapult variant;
- damage-modifier counts for each expected field;
- which Seaking print and count should be canonical;
- matchup-specific prize maps against the current top field.

SHORT TRAINING-HEURISTIC APPENDIX

The machine guide should supervise only choices that are exact from the current
legal stage: choose a visible Festival Lead Basic as opening Active, create
redundant Grookey and Applin lines, evolve visible lines, deploy Festival
Grounds when a Festival Lead Active needs it, use a legal Thwackey Ability, and
fill exact Poffin or Boom Boom Groove engine gaps.

It should mask attack math, modifier selection, recovery, gusting, protection,
and matchup tech decisions unless the complete legal stage exposes all
required identities, damage, prize, and effect information. It must never use
future outcomes, hidden opposing information, or whether the game was won to
construct a target.
