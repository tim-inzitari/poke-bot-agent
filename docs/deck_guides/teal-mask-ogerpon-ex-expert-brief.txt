TEAL MASK OGERPON EX / OGERPON BOX
Current expert pilot brief
Reviewed 2026-07-28


STRATEGIC IDENTITY

This is the Ogerpon Box specialist, not every deck that uses Teal Mask Ogerpon
ex. The representative is Toshiyuki Otake's first-place list from the 2026
Japan Championships: four Teal Mask Ogerpon ex plus a Basic-Pokémon toolbox,
Energy Switch, Prism Energy, and Area Zero Underdepths. The official winner
interview describes Teal Mask as the trusted core and Pecharunt as an important
comeback card. Limitless records Ogerpon Box separately from Raging Bolt,
Ogerpon Meganium, and other Teal Mask engines.

The deck converts Grass Energy in hand into tempo and cards through Teal Dance,
then moves or reinterprets that Energy to unlock whichever Basic attacker fits
the present board. It is nonlinear: its best attacker, bench shape, Energy
route, and prize plan change together. Do not pilot it as a simple Myriad Leaf
Shower deck and do not fill the eight-space bench merely because Area Zero
permits it.

CARD ROLES AND INTERACTIONS

Teal Mask Ogerpon ex is both accelerator and draw engine. Each unused Teal
Dance can attach one Basic Grass from hand to that Teal Mask and draw a card.
Multiple copies create multiple independent uses. Myriad Leaf Shower starts at
30 and gains 30 for every Energy attached to both Active Pokémon. Count the
current Energy on both sides before committing to it.

Mega Kangaskhan ex is a durable colorless attacker and an Active-only draw
engine. Run Errand draws two while it is Active. Rapid-Fire Combo has a
guaranteed 200 base; coin heads add damage, but a plan is sound only at the
200-damage floor.

Lillie's Clefairy ex makes opposing Dragon Pokémon Psychic Weak and attacks for
20 plus 20 per Benched Pokémon across both boards. Area Zero can raise your
bench capacity and therefore its deterministic ceiling. Recount both benches
after every knockout, Stadium change, or pickup.

Latias ex gives every Basic Pokémon in play free retreat. It turns an Active
Kangaskhan draw into a no-Energy pivot and lets the deck select attackers after
using Active-only effects. Protect it when mobility is central, but do not bench
a redundant second copy without a concrete reason.

Meowth ex searches a Supporter when played from hand to the Bench. Treat the
bench slot as part of the Supporter cost. Boss is correct only when the gust
changes prizes; Lillie's Determination is correct when the hand needs repair;
Ciphermaniac needs a named immediate draw effect; N's Plan needs a named Energy
movement line.

Wellspring Mask Ogerpon ex threatens 100 to the Active and, after returning
three Energy, 120 to one Benched Pokémon. Build the Energy route and target
before announcing this line. The best use is usually an exact two-target prize
conversion, not casual spread.

Pecharunt's Poison Chain poisons and prevents retreat during the opponent's next
turn. While Pecharunt is Active, Toxic Subjugation adds five more damage
counters during Pokémon Checkup. The most valuable line is a deterministic
between-turn knockout that changes the opponent's response or denies a
knockout-triggered draw/disruption window. Do not count multiple future
checkups as guaranteed.

Munkidori moves up to three damage counters from one of your Pokémon to an
opponent's Pokémon when it has Darkness Energy attached. Prism Energy supplies
the required type only while attached to a Basic and provides one Energy at a
time. Move counters toward an immediate knockout or exact attack threshold.

Chi-Yu's relevant attack reaches 120 when a Stadium is in play and then
discards that Stadium. Discarding an opposing Stadium may be pure upside.
Discarding your own Area Zero can force an immediate bench collapse, so name
the discarded bench Pokémon before choosing the attack.

Fezandipiti ex supplies Flip the Script only after an opponent-turn knockout.
It is a recovery valve, not an automatic early bench commitment.


SETUP AND OPENING PRIORITIES

Before placing Pokémon, answer four questions:

1. Which attacker has a deterministic useful attack first?
2. How many Teal Dance uses can the opening hand support?
3. Is Latias needed to pivot after an Active-only ability?
4. Which toolbox Basic would become stranded if the bench fills?

Mega Kangaskhan is usually a comfortable Active because it can use Run Errand
and retreat for free once Latias is present. Teal Mask is a functional Active
when it will attack or when no better opener exists. Avoid opening a fragile
single-purpose support Basic by choice.

Bench the first Teal Mask early. A second Teal is strong when Grass in hand can
feed both uses or when it creates the next attacker. Latias is valuable when it
unlocks an actual pivot. Meowth should enter when the searched Supporter and
its use are already named. Leave at least one bench space until the first
attacker and recovery route are clear.


GOING FIRST

Use the no-attack turn to build an Energy and mobility advantage. Prioritize a
Teal Mask, an attachment that survives the likely response, and access to the
next attacker. If Kangaskhan is Active, Run Errand before a shuffle effect
unless Ciphermaniac has deliberately set the top cards. Avoid exposing several
two-Prize Pokémon before knowing which ones advance the next turn.

The goal is not the largest board; it is a board that can choose between at
least two legal pressure lines on the next turn.


GOING SECOND

Identify the best deterministic attack available now. A modest Teal Mask
attack that accelerates the board can be better than spending every resource
on an uncertain Kangaskhan ceiling. Clefairy is valuable into a visible Dragon
Active or when both benches make its damage exact. Pecharunt is valuable only
when poison and retreat denial change the opponent's next action.

Use Teal Dance before committing an Energy Switch unless the move must happen
first to preserve a legal ability target. Count the deck and hand after each
draw; the new card can legitimately change the branch.


TURN-BY-TURN SEQUENCING

At the start of each turn:

- Count Prizes remaining and public prize values.
- Count Energy on both Active Pokémon for Myriad Leaf Shower.
- List every unused Teal Dance and the Basic Grass actually in hand.
- Confirm whether Latias currently makes retreat free.
- Check the Area Zero condition and the consequence if it leaves play.
- Name the current attacker, next attacker, and recovery target.

Resolve deterministic information before destructive actions. Run Errand or
Flip the Script can reveal options before Ultra Ball discards. Ciphermaniac is
the exception: if it has intentionally fixed the top two cards, use the named
draw effect after it. Use Meowth only when the Supporter search is worth its
bench slot.

Teal Dance attaches only to the Teal Mask using it. Energy Switch moves a Basic
Energy, not Prism Energy. Prism can satisfy typed attacks on a Basic but still
counts as only one Energy. Recompute every legal attack after each movement.


ENERGY AND RESOURCE PLANNING

Separate the Energy plan into three layers:

1. Energy that enables the current deterministic attack.
2. Energy that preserves a second attacker after a knockout.
3. Surplus Energy that increases Teal Mask damage.

Do not move the only Grass off a Teal Mask merely to create a cosmetic damage
increase elsewhere. Do not attach Prism where a Basic Grass would do the same
job if Prism is the only path to Clefairy, Wellspring, Pecharunt, or Munkidori.
Before Wellspring returns three Energy, verify which attacker remains usable.

Night Stretcher can recover either a Pokémon or Basic Energy. Name the unique
missing resource before playing it. A glamorous attacker is not the right
target if the present board instead needs the one Energy that makes an attack
legal.


ATTACK SELECTION AND PRIZE MAPPING

Rank attacks by guaranteed prizes and board state, then by pressure:

1. A guaranteed game-ending knockout.
2. A guaranteed multi-Prize knockout.
3. A current Active-plus-Bench conversion from Wellspring.
4. A knockout that preserves the next attacker.
5. A denial line that forces the opponent to miss an attack.
6. Damage scaling that depends on future coin heads or future draws.

Kangaskhan is evaluated at 200 before Weakness, Resistance, and current effects.
Teal Mask uses exact current Energy counts. Clefairy uses exact current bench
counts and Dragon identity. Wellspring uses its current legal target. Pecharunt
uses only the next guaranteed checkup. Do not use the identities of face-down
Prize cards in any plan.


BENCH MANAGEMENT

Area Zero allows up to eight Benched Pokémon only while a Tera Pokémon is in
play. More capacity is not permission to bench every available Basic. Assign
each slot a role: Energy engine, current attacker, next attacker, mobility,
search/draw, damage manipulation, or matchup tool.

Before playing or discarding Area Zero, choose which Pokémon leave if the bench
must contract. Preserve a powered attacker, the next attacker, and unique
mobility or engine pieces. A Meowth that already used its ability may be more
dispensable than an unused Teal Mask; a stranded support Pokémon may be more
dispensable than the only Prism-powered attacker.


RECOVERY LINES

Low Energy hand:

Use Bug Catching Set before committing to an attacker that requires several
Basic Grass. Teal Dance only with Energy actually in hand. Preserve Prism for
typed toolbox routes.

No Latias:

Plan retreat costs explicitly. Avoid using an Active-only draw ability if it
strands the wrong attacker. Keep an Energy or switching route available.

Area Zero removed:

Resolve the bench contraction immediately from public state. Keep the current
and next attack lines; then keep the unique engine or matchup tool. Rebuild
capacity only after the Stadium condition is restored.

Behind on prizes:

Look for a deterministic dual-target Wellspring line, a Clefairy Weakness
knockout, or a Pecharunt between-turn knockout that disrupts response timing.
Do not chase Kangaskhan coin heads.

Ahead on prizes:

Reduce variance. Use guaranteed damage, preserve Latias mobility and a second
attacker, and avoid unnecessary two-Prize bench liabilities.


MATCHUP PLANS

The exact winner list does not yet have a defensible public matchup matrix.
Use causal board identities rather than named-deck folklore.

Against Dragons, Clefairy's Fairy Zone changes current Weakness to Psychic.
Confirm the opposing Pokémon's type and current effects, then calculate Full
Moon Rondo from both benches.

Against low-HP benches, Wellspring can create two prizes across Active and
Bench. Confirm protection effects and the exact target prompt.

Against Energy-heavy Active attackers, Teal Mask's damage scaling may turn the
opponent's investment against them. Count both Active Energy totals rather
than assuming the attack is large.

Against disruption, diversify Energy and attackers so one hand reset or
knockout does not remove every legal line. Fezandipiti is strongest after the
trigger is actually live.


COMMON MISTAKES

1. Calling every Teal Mask engine an Ogerpon Box deck.
2. Filling eight bench spaces without a collapse plan.
3. Treating Prism Energy as multiple Energy or moving it with Energy Switch.
4. Planning Kangaskhan damage above its guaranteed 200 floor.
5. Using every Teal Dance before deciding which Teal Mask should attack.
6. Benching Meowth without naming the Supporter and its immediate use.
7. Using Ciphermaniac without preserving an immediate draw effect.
8. Returning three Energy with Wellspring while abandoning the next attacker.
9. Counting more than the next guaranteed poison checkup.
10. Moving Munkidori counters without an exact current threshold.
11. Discarding Area Zero without naming the bench contraction.
12. Guessing face-down Prize identities.
13. Exposing Fezandipiti before Flip the Script has material value.
14. Confusing generic Ogerpon aggregate results with this exact list.


DECISION CHECKLIST

Opening:

- Best Active at the deterministic floor?
- First and second attacker?
- First two Teal Dance targets?
- Is Latias needed now?
- Which bench slot stays open?

Before abilities:

- Is the ability currently legal?
- Does Ciphermaniac require a specific draw order?
- Which new observation can change the branch?

Before Energy movement:

- Basic Energy or Prism?
- Current attack still legal afterward?
- Next attacker still live?
- Does the move change guaranteed prizes?

Before attacking:

- Deterministic damage after all current effects?
- Guaranteed Prizes now?
- Bench target exact and unprotected?
- Retreat and next attacker preserved?
- Area Zero collapse risk named?

SHORT TRAINING-AUDIT APPENDIX

The sparse machine teacher labels only exact opening placement and currently
legal resolved Teal Dance, Run Errand, Flip the Script, or Adrena-Brain ability
actions. The authoritative fused policy learns the nonlinear Energy, target,
prize, attack, bench, and recovery branches.

The teacher masks Energy Switch routes, Prism type choices, attacks, coin
outcomes, Wellspring targets, Pecharunt future checkups, Munkidori targets,
Area Zero collapse, broad Supporter/search/discard choices, face-down Prizes,
hidden opponent information, future draws, and incomplete legal stages. It has
no serving authority.
