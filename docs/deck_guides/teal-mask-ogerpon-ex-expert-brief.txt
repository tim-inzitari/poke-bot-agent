SLOP BOX
Teal Mask Ogerpon ex / Mega Kangaskhan ex specialist pilot guide

Identity

This guide is for the exact public PTCGReplay archetype-151 deck shown by the
owner, known locally as “Slop Box.” The stable training ID remains
teal-mask-ogerpon-ex. Slop Box is the Teal Mask Ogerpon ex plus Mega
Kangaskhan ex deck shell. “Raging Bolt Ogerpon” remains a useful secondary
search/taxonomy alias because Raging Bolt ex is one of the deck's attackers,
but it is not the deck's primary identity and no alias permits substituting a
different list.

It is not the Toshiyuki Otake Japan Championships Ogerpon Box deck. That older
list has a different Pokemon suite, Energy system, and game plan. The defining
Slop Box shell is three Mega Kangaskhan ex, three Meowth ex, three Teal Mask
Ogerpon ex, two Raging Bolt ex, four Area Zero Underdepths, four Crispin, four
Energy Switch, two Glass Trumpet, and five Basic Energy types.

What the deck is trying to do

Slop Box converts a wide Basic-Pokemon bench and a hand full of typed Basic
Energy into whichever attacker, pivot, or prize route is best now. Teal Mask
Ogerpon ex supplies Grass acceleration and cards. Energy Switch turns that
distributed Energy into attack access. Crispin, Glass Trumpet, and Night
Stretcher make the non-Grass types and discarded resources matter. Area Zero
creates enough bench space for several engines without forcing the pilot to
choose the whole game plan on turn one.

Raging Bolt ex is the scalable damage branch. Mega Kangaskhan ex is a durable
setup and attacking branch. Meowth ex, Latias ex, Fezandipiti ex, Clefairy,
Passimian, and Wellspring are not generic bench decorations: each must justify
its bench slot through a current or mapped future job. The deck succeeds by
preserving options until the public board and prize map reveal which branch
converts most cleanly.

Opening and setup

Before placing Pokemon, inventory the opening hand by function:

1. Which Pokemon can safely occupy the Active Spot?
2. How many Teal Mask Ogerpon ex can actually use Grass Energy this turn?
3. Which attacker can be powered without consuming the types another branch
   needs?
4. Is Latias required to make the next pivot legal and inexpensive?
5. Which bench slots must remain open for a searched attacker or recovery
   Pokemon?

Mega Kangaskhan ex is usually a preferable blind opener to a one-of support
Pokemon. Teal Mask Ogerpon ex is an acceptable opener when Teal Dance and a
clean pivot are available. Avoid exposing a narrow one-of merely because the
deck can bench many Pokemon.

Bench Teal Mask Ogerpon ex only when there is a realistic Grass attachment for
it. A second Ogerpon can be excellent because it adds acceleration, cards, and
Energy Switch reach; a third can be wasteful if Grass is scarce or Area Zero
is vulnerable. Latias earns its slot when mobility changes the attack sequence.
Do not fill all eight Area Zero slots before deciding which attacker and
recovery piece the next turn may require.

Going first

The first-turn objective is infrastructure, not speculative damage. Establish
one or two usable Teal Dance engines, preserve typed Energy, and make the
future Active flexible. Ultra Ball and Cyrano should build a coherent branch,
not simply maximize the number of Pokemon in play.

Because a Supporter cannot solve every type and board problem at once, plan the
next turn before spending Crispin or discarding with Ultra Ball. If Raging Bolt
is the intended attacker, count the Energy that can actually reach the board
and the Energy that can be discarded for its attack. If Kangaskhan is the
intended attacker, do not strip the rest of the board so aggressively that a
return knockout ends the attack chain.

Going second

Going second increases the value of an immediate Supporter and may expose an
attack line, but speed is not automatically correct. Compare:

- the prizes or tempo gained by attacking now;
- the Energy and switching resources consumed;
- the opponent’s likely return knockout from public information;
- whether a rushed attacker strands the bench;
- whether keeping the Energy distributed creates a stronger two-turn line.

Crispin is strongest when both Energy types have a defined job. Energy Switch
can complete the attacker after Teal Dance or Crispin, but each copy is a
finite routing resource. Take an immediate attack only when its prize or tempo
value exceeds the future flexibility being spent.

Energy and resource planning

Track all five Basic Energy types separately. Grass is abundant, fuels Teal
Dance, and is the main transferable bank. Fighting, Lightning, Water, and
Psychic are scarce and should not be discarded casually. Before every Ultra
Ball, Raging Bolt attack, or recovery decision, write the remaining legal
attack costs in terms of exact types, not merely total Energy.

Use this sequence:

1. Choose the desired attacker and attack.
2. Count its exact typed requirement.
3. Count Energy already attached and Energy legally available this turn.
4. Reserve scarce types needed by a later attacker.
5. Decide which Energy Switch routes are necessary.
6. Only then use acceleration, discard, or recovery effects.

Glass Trumpet and Night Stretcher create recovery lines, but they do not make
discarding free. A card in the discard is useful only when the recovery card,
legal target, bench slot, and timing all exist.

Attacker selection and prize mapping

Raging Bolt ex is preferred when its current attached-Energy discard math
reaches a decisive knockout without destroying the next attack. Count only
Energy visibly attached now; never assume future topdecks or hidden Prizes.

Mega Kangaskhan ex is the steadier branch when its present attack value
advances the prize race while preserving Energy elsewhere. Treat random bonus
damage as upside, not as the required premise of a line.

Wellspring Mask Ogerpon ex is a targeted branch. Use it only when its attack
and targets create more prize or tempo value than the single-target line, and
when the Water requirement does not cut off a better follow-up.

Clefairy and Passimian are matchup and board-state tools. Bench them because a
specific public interaction matters, not because they appear in the list.
Fezandipiti ex is recovery draw after the opponent takes a knockout. Meowth ex
and Latias should likewise be evaluated by the immediate function they unlock.

At the start of each turn, identify the prize route as a sequence of public
targets. Compare a direct knockout line with a two-turn spread or gust line.
Boss’s Orders and Prime Catcher are scarce; save them when the natural Active
already advances the same prize route.

Area Zero and bench discipline

Area Zero is an engine and a liability. Each extra bench slot must have a job:
Energy engine, current attacker, next attacker, pivot, draw recovery, or
matchup-specific effect. Before replacing or losing Area Zero, choose which
Pokemon are discarded by the bench collapse. Never let the interface or an
implicit ordering make that strategic choice.

When the opponent can remove the Stadium, value low-commitment benches and
recoverable Pokemon more highly. Keep at least one route that functions with a
normal bench whenever the matchup makes Stadium removal credible.

Disruption and recovery

Xerosic’s Machinations is a tempo card, not a ritual. Use it when reducing the
opponent’s visible hand meaningfully lowers the quality of their next turn and
your own board can still execute. Do not trade away the Supporter for a turn
that fails to develop or attack.

Night Stretcher should restore the exact Pokemon or Basic Energy that completes
the mapped line. Glass Trumpet should be planned alongside the Basic Pokemon
that can receive the Energy. Recovery is strongest when prepared one turn
before the knockout rather than improvised after every resource is gone.

Common mistakes

- Using the older Ogerpon Box guide or representative for this deck.
- Treating “Teal Mask Ogerpon ex” as any deck containing Teal Mask.
- Benching every Basic simply because Area Zero permits it.
- Spending scarce non-Grass Energy without counting all remaining attack costs.
- Treating Energy Switch as generic acceleration instead of a finite route.
- Building Raging Bolt damage from hoped-for future Energy.
- Requiring favorable Kangaskhan randomness for a line to work.
- Removing Area Zero without explicitly selecting the bench-collapse losses.
- Benching one-of tools without a matchup-specific job.
- Using gust or disruption when the natural board already yields the same
  prize or tempo result.

Turn checklist

Before actions:

- What is the current prize route?
- Which attacker acts now, and which attacker must be ready next?
- Which exact Energy types are required?
- Which bench Pokemon have no current or mapped future job?
- What changes if Area Zero disappears?

Before attacking:

- Is the damage deterministic enough for the line?
- Which attached Energy is consumed or moved?
- Does the return knockout leave a legal follow-up?
- Is gusting worth more than saving the gust effect?
- Does a different attacker preserve more future branches?

End of turn:

- Is the next pivot legal?
- Is the next attacker powered or recoverable?
- Are scarce Energy types protected?
- Can the bench survive Stadium loss?
- Did the turn improve the prize race rather than merely increase board size?

Training appendix

This guide is auxiliary scaffolding. The learned 17-input fused policy remains
the action authority. Exact current legal-option labels may guide opening
placement and resolved legal draw or acceleration abilities. Attack selection,
Raging Bolt discard choices, Energy Switch routes, Crispin type routes, Glass
Trumpet targets, prize identities, future draws, and hidden opponent
information remain masked unless the exact causal state exposes them. Missing
labels are masked, never converted to false zero targets. Guide weight changes
require separate training-ineligible guide-on/guide-off evidence and anneal
toward zero when the learned policy internalizes or surpasses the guide.
