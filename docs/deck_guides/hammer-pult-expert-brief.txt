HAMMER DRAGAPULT
PRACTICAL PILOT GUIDE FOR EXPERT REVIEW

Specialist ID: hammer-pult
Guide version: hammer-pult-north-star-v1
Prepared: 2026-07-28
Audience: world-champion and equivalent Pokémon TCG subject-matter experts
Status: research pilot; not yet expert-approved


SCOPE AND EVIDENCE LABELS

This is a human how-to-play guide for the disruption-heavy Dragapult ex deck
that combines Crushing Hammer, Budew, Munkidori, and Unfair Stamp. The project
calls that card-signature family Hammer Dragapult or Hammer-Pult. It is not the
same specialist as Dragapult/Dusknoir, Dragapult/Blaziken, or a generic
Dragapult list merely because all of them use Dreepy, Drakloak, and Dragapult
ex.

The concrete project representative is the modal 60-card Hammer-Pult list from
the official Pokémon TCG AI Battle top-episode export for July 12, 2026. It has
four Dreepy, four Drakloak, three Dragapult ex, two Munkidori, two Budew, one
Fezandipiti ex, three Crushing Hammer, Unfair Stamp, and ten Basic Energy. The
closest strong public physical-tournament reference is Cássio Moraes's
fourth-place Campinas Regional list, which used four Crushing Hammer plus a
2-2 Dunsparce/Dudunsparce engine. The official 2026 NAIC Masters page also
publishes a successful 4-Hammer Dragapult/Dudunsparce list. Confirm your exact
registered 60 before using any LIST CHECK line.

public aggregate results, or player-authored strategy directly supports the

INFERENCE means practical play reasoning from those facts. It is offered for

THE DECK IN ONE SENTENCE

Build several Dreepy into a Drakloak draw engine, use Budew and Hammer to steal
development turns, then make every Phantom Dive solve the current Active while
placing six counters that predetermine a later multi-Prize turn.

Hammer Dragapult wins through the interaction of three clocks:

1. Development clock: how quickly you reach a powered Dragapult while retaining
   another evolving Dreepy.
2. Denial clock: how many useful opposing turns are lost to Itchy Pollen,
   Crushing Hammer, Judge, Special Red Card, Xerosic, or Unfair Stamp.
3. Prize clock: how quickly 200 Active damage, six placed counters, and
   Adrena-Brain produce six prizes.

Do not maximize one clock while ignoring the others. Four Hammers do not make
a weak board good. A fast Phantom Dive that places counters without a prize
map may be less valuable than a setup turn. Conversely, endless disruption
without preparing two typed Energy on Dragapult eventually gives the opponent
time to draw out.

QUICK START

If you have only one minute before a round:

1. Prefer going second when a meaningful turn-one Itchy Pollen is likely to
   deny opposing Items. Reconsider against a matchup where evolving first or
   attacking Budew changes little.
2. Open Budew when the lock matters and you can bench Dreepy. Otherwise open a
   low-liability single-Prizer and pivot; avoid donating Fezandipiti ex.
3. Establish two Dreepy before optional support Pokémon. Three is appropriate
   against gust, spread, or a matchup that can remove one before it evolves.
4. Attach every turn with the next two attacks in mind. Phantom Dive needs one
   Fire and one Psychic; Darkness belongs on Munkidori only when Adrena-Brain
   will earn real damage or repair an attacker.
5. Evolve through Drakloak whenever practical. Recon Directive is the engine,
   and a Drakloak left behind a Dragapult is your next attacker seed.
6. Play Hammer when a heads result removes Energy that matters. Do not treat a
   coin flip as part of your plan until the coin has actually resolved.
7. Before Phantom Dive, map all six counters. Ask which exact later attack,
   Adrena-Brain, or Boss turn converts each counter into a prize.
8. Use Unfair Stamp after the opponent takes a knockout, ideally when your board
   can immediately attack or lock Items. Disruption without pressure is merely
   a smaller hand that gets another turn.
9. Expose Fezandipiti ex only when Flip the Script materially changes the turn.
   It is a two-Prize liability in a deck otherwise capable of forcing awkward
   prizes.
10. End every turn naming the next attacker, both Energy it needs, and the
    intended recipient of the next six Phantom Dive counters.


SETUP AND SEQUENCING

Opening board

SOURCE: Current public strategy describes the early game as establishing Dreepy
and using Budew's Itchy Pollen to Item-lock the opponent. Current aggregate
lists consistently play four Dreepy, four Drakloak, and multiple search cards.

INFERENCE: A normal desirable opening is Budew Active, two Dreepy Benched, and
an attachment that advances a future Dragapult. A third Dreepy is valuable when
the opponent can gust, place Bench damage, or take a quick single Prize. One
Dreepy is a failure point. Four Dreepy can crowd out Munkidori and Fezandipiti,
so fill the final spaces only after planning the matchup.

Prioritize Poffin targets by missing board function, not by a fixed memorized
order:

- first live Dreepy;
- second live Dreepy;
- Budew when it will be Active and lock useful Items;
- third Dreepy when the opponent can break a single line;
- another Budew only when a second lock turn or easy pivot matters.

Do not bench Fezandipiti ex merely because it is available. Its Ability matters
only after one of your Pokémon was Knocked Out during the opponent's previous
turn, and it gives up two prizes. Munkidori is also not an automatic opening
bench. It needs Darkness attached and damage on your own Pokémon before
Adrena-Brain produces value.

Evolution order

SOURCE: Drakloak's Recon Directive looks at the top two cards and puts one into
hand; Dragapult ex is the 320-HP Stage 2 attacker whose Phantom Dive deals 200
and places six counters on the opposing Bench.

INFERENCE: Evolve enough Dreepy to Drakloak that your hand can continue, then
promote only the Drakloak that can attack or must become durable. A common
mistake is evolving every Drakloak immediately and deleting the draw engine.
Another is leaving a powered line as fragile Dreepy while using Recon Directive
elsewhere. Each evolution decision should answer:

- Does this become the current attacker?
- Do I still have a Drakloak to draw?
- Is this Dreepy at risk before next turn?
- Does my hand need Recon Directive before I play a shuffle-draw Supporter?

Use every legal, useful Recon Directive before replacing that Drakloak with
Dragapult or ending the turn. If a later shuffle effect is planned, draw first
when seeing an extra card improves the decision; sequence differently only
when preserving known top cards or hand size has an exact reason.


GOING FIRST AND GOING SECOND

Going second

SOURCE: Budew's attack is the deck's immediate pressure option, and public
player discussion and current strategy articles describe turn-one Itchy Pollen
as a reason to choose second.

INFERENCE: Default to second against Item-dependent evolution and search decks.
The target turn is not "attack with Budew at all costs." It is:

- bench two Dreepy;
- attach toward Dragapult;
- thin or refresh the hand without discarding irreplaceable pieces;
- play any Hammer whose heads result affects the next opposing attack; and
- use Itchy Pollen when the opponent's next Item denial is worth more than
  passing or an alternative attack.

Going first

INFERENCE: Going first trades away immediate Budew pressure for the earliest
natural Drakloak turn. Choose it when the matchup develops mainly through
Supporters or Abilities, when the opponent can ignore Item lock, or when
evolving before their first attack is essential. Build redundant Dreepy and
attach. Avoid benching a two-Prizer simply to improve an otherwise complete
setup.

SME CHECK: Exact first/second selection should be reviewed matchup by matchup
for the current event. The generic aggregate does not isolate Hammer lists or
randomized choice, so it cannot establish a causal going-first win rate.


ENERGY AND ATTACK PLANNING

SOURCE: Phantom Dive costs Fire plus Psychic. Munkidori's Adrena-Brain requires
a Darkness Energy attached. The public competitive lists use a small
three-type Basic Energy package and Crispin.

Treat each attachment as one of three jobs:

- first Phantom Dive color on the current line;
- second Phantom Dive color on the current or next line;
- Darkness enabling one Munkidori.

Never attach Darkness speculatively when doing so delays Phantom Dive. Never
attach both attack colors to one vulnerable Dreepy if splitting them across
Drakloak preserves two credible attackers and Crispin can finish either. But do
not split Energy so widely that no attacker comes online.

Before Crispin, count the exact Basic Energy still accessible, the types in
hand, and whether the attachment portion has a legal and useful destination.
Before Ultra Ball, identify whether discarding an Energy leaves enough copies
for two attacks and a Munkidori. Before Night Stretcher, decide whether the
needed recovery is a Pokémon or Basic Energy.

Attack choice

Jet Headbutt's 70 is a fallback. Phantom Dive is the plan because its 200 plus
six counters creates simultaneous pressure. Yet Phantom Dive is not
automatically correct if the Active damage is blanked, the Bench is protected,
or another attacker wins immediately. Verify all effects before declaring the
line.


PHANTOM DIVE AND PRIZE MAPPING

SOURCE: Phantom Dive places six damage counters on opposing Benched Pokémon in
any way. Damage-counter placement is not the same as attack damage. Munkidori
can move up to three counters from one of your Pokémon to one opposing Pokémon
when Darkness Energy is attached.

Count in tens, but plan in knockouts.

Useful six-counter patterns include:

- 60 on one evolving Basic that will be Knocked Out by the next spread or
  Adrena-Brain;
- 30/30 on two targets that each become future 200+30 thresholds;
- 40/20 when one target needs exactly four counters and the remainder has a
  named future use;
- all six on a support Pokémon that must evolve or retreat from danger.

The best placement is the one that survives the opponent's legal responses.
Ask whether the target can evolve out of range, heal, be recovered, gain HP,
leave play, gain Bench protection, or become inaccessible. Prefer counters that
create more than one live prize route, forcing the opponent to solve several
problems.

Prize map before attacking:

1. Record both players' prizes.
2. Identify every opposing one- and two-Prize target.
3. Calculate Active damage after Weakness, Resistance, Tools, and effects.
4. Name the target of each placed counter.
5. State the next attack or Adrena-Brain that cashes those counters.
6. Check whether Boss's Orders is required; if so, preserve it.
7. Check whether taking the current prize enables or disables a comeback card.

INFERENCE: Phantom Dive often seeks a 2-2-2 route against Pokémon ex, but
Hammer-Pult's stronger lines frequently interleave a one-Prize Bench knockout
with Active damage. Do not insist on three clean ex knockouts when spread can
remove an engine Pokémon and reduce the opponent's future options.


DISRUPTION PLANNING

Crushing Hammer

SOURCE: Crushing Hammer flips a coin; on heads it discards an Energy attached
to an opposing Pokémon.

The correct pre-flip question is: "If heads, which visible Energy produces the
largest immediate loss of options?" Usually prioritize:

- the only Energy on the next attacker;
- a difficult-to-replace type;
- a Special Energy carrying multiple units or a protective effect;
- Energy on a pivot whose Retreat plan matters; then
- surplus Energy only when no better target exists.

Do not condition your sequence on heads before the flip. Play search, draw, or
attachments in the order that remains legal and useful under both outcomes.
After tails, rebuild the turn from the actual state. After heads, exploit the
tempo by attacking, locking, or disrupting the hand; otherwise the opponent
may simply reattach.

Unfair Stamp and hand disruption

SOURCE: The successful public lists pair Hammer with Unfair Stamp and other
hand disruption. Public strategy describes Dragapult as pressure plus
disruption, not damage alone.

INFERENCE: The premium Stamp window is after the opponent takes a knockout and
before you apply a board state they must answer immediately. Combine it with
Phantom Dive or Itchy Pollen where possible. Judge, Special Red Card, or
Xerosic should be evaluated against the visible hand size and board, not played
merely because they are "disruption." A large hand is not automatically a good
target if it contains known dead cards; a small hand may still be perfect.

Risky Ruins

LIST CHECK: The project representative and Campinas list use Risky Ruins.
Sequence Stadium replacement around both players' likely damage. Do not play
it automatically if it provides the opponent the decisive counter math before
it creates yours.


BENCH MANAGEMENT AND RECOVERY

A six-space plan should assign jobs:

- Active Budew or Dragapult;
- current Drakloak/Dragapult attacker;
- next Dreepy/Drakloak;
- draw-engine Drakloak;
- one Munkidori when live;
- one flexible slot for a third line, second Munkidori, or Fezandipiti ex.

This is not a prescription to fill all spaces. Preserve one slot when Night
Stretcher, an emergency Dreepy, or Fezandipiti may become necessary.

Recovery priorities:

1. The only card that restores the next legal attacker.
2. The missing typed Energy for Phantom Dive.
3. A Dragapult line piece needed for the remaining prize map.
4. Munkidori when an exact three-counter move changes prizes.
5. Budew when another Item-lock turn is actually meaningful.

Night Stretcher creates either/or tension. Name the target before playing
search and discard effects. Do not recover a glamorous Stage 2 while lacking a
Basic to evolve, or recover Darkness for Munkidori when the next Dragapult
cannot attack.


MATCHUP PLANS

These plans combine public aggregate results with mechanical inference.
Aggregate Dragapult data mixes variants, pilots, list choices, and event
conditions. Treat percentages as context, never as causal claims about this
exact Hammer list.

Dragapult mirror

Public commentary identifies Hammer as especially impactful in the mirror.
Both players need typed attachments every turn, and both threaten Bench
counters.

- establish three Dreepy when possible;
- attach so one Hammer does not strand the only attacker;
- preserve Darkness only when Munkidori can reverse counter math;
- spread counters onto Drakloak/Dreepy that cannot all evolve away;
- avoid volunteering Fezandipiti ex unless its draw wins the current race;
- save Stamp for a knockout turn that also presents pressure.

Alakazam/Dudunsparce

Current CRI aggregate Dragapult data is favorable against Alakazam Dudunsparce,
but that is not Hammer-only evidence.

- pressure Abra/Kadabra before the Stage 2 engine stabilizes;
- map Bench counters around Dudunsparce draw cycling and recovery;
- Hammer an Energy that changes an immediate attack, not a harmless spare;
- preserve Boss for an exposed engine target;
- avoid over-benching two-Prizers into Powerful Hand prize math.

Cynthia's Garchomp

Current CRI and POR aggregates show Dragapult performing poorly into Cynthia's
Garchomp.

- use Budew to delay Item-driven setup when relevant;
- establish extra Dreepy against fast pressure;
- Hammer the attachment that breaks the next attack or acceleration chain;
- place counters on evolving support lines, not merely the largest HP target;
- accept that a denial turn must advance your own attacker to matter.

Marnie's Grimmsnarl/Froslass

Aggregate results are format-dependent; the current CRI page is slightly
favorable for generic Dragapult, while older data differs.

- limit Abilities and bench slots that donate Froslass counters without value;
- use Munkidori to move exact public counters into prize thresholds;
- recognize that opposing Munkidori can undo careless spread;
- attack the Energy and evolution engine, not only the current wall;
- recalculate after every damage-moving Ability.

Crustle and defensive walls

Older broad data was unfavorable, and player discussion emphasizes Mist or
other Special Energy and protection interactions.

- Hammer protective Special Energy when that makes damage placement legal;
- do not assume Active damage will land; verify the current prevention text;
- use Phantom Dive's Bench counters and Munkidori only where effects allow;
- preserve alternate single-Prize attacks if the registered list has them;
- avoid deck-out by using optional draw deliberately.

Festival Lead and other low-HP single-Prize boards

- Item-lock early before multiple evolution lines appear;
- Phantom Dive should create two future knockouts whenever protection is absent;
- attack the Thwackey search engine or evolving Applin, not a replaceable Active
  by habit;
- track whether Bench-protection Pokémon invalidate placement;
- do not lose the six-prize race by exposing unnecessary Pokémon ex.

Rocket's Mewtwo and Energy-dense decks

- Hammer Energy whose removal crosses the exact Erasure Ball or retreat
  threshold;
- spread onto low-HP Team Rocket support Pokémon when Articuno or other
  protection does not stop the effect;
- preserve Boss for a two-Prize target that actually closes the map;
- expect Energy movement and recompute after every public attachment.


RECOVERY LINES FROM BAD STARTS

Budew prized or unavailable:

Build two Dreepy immediately. Use Hammer and hand disruption as substitute
tempo, but do not pretend they recreate Item lock. Prioritize Drakloak draw.

Only one Dreepy:

Bench another before optional support Pokémon. Evolve the threatened line when
possible, but retain enough Drakloak access to continue drawing.

Missing one Phantom Dive color:

Use Recon Directive before shuffle-draw, search with Crispin if legal, and
count every remaining copy. Attach the color that keeps both current and next
turn live. Jet Headbutt is acceptable when it preserves the board and the
follow-up is named.

Behind on prizes:

Seek a combined Active-plus-Bench prize turn. Stamp after a knockout, then
force an answer. Boss only if the gust improves the total prize clock, not
merely because a damaged target is available.

Ahead on prizes:

Reduce variance. Avoid unnecessary two-Prize benches, preserve the next
attacker, and place counters toward the simplest legal close. Hammer the
opponent's only comeback attacker rather than a remote resource.


COMMON MISTAKES

1. Keeping a one-Dreepy board because Budew bought one turn.
2. Evolving every Drakloak and losing Recon Directive.
3. Attaching Darkness before two Phantom Dive colors are secured.
4. Playing Hammer as if heads were guaranteed.
5. Removing the Energy with the largest printed value instead of the Energy
   that changes the next action.
6. Placing six counters without naming their later conversion.
7. Spreading damage into protection or a clean evolution escape.
8. Benching Fezandipiti ex before Flip the Script is live.
9. Playing Stamp without immediate attack or lock pressure.
10. Using Boss for a modest hit and losing the final gust.
11. Recovering a Stage 2 without a Basic or Stage 1 line.
12. Filling the bench before deciding whether Munkidori or a replacement Dreepy
    will be needed.
13. Treating generic Dragapult aggregate percentages as exact Hammer-Pult
    matchup truth.
14. Forgetting that this project representative has no Dudunsparce despite the
    closest physical-event reference lists using it.


DECISION CHECKLISTS

Opening:

- First or second, and what exact turn is gained?
- Can I make two Dreepy?
- Does Budew deny meaningful Items?
- Which Energy begins the first Phantom Dive?
- Which bench slot must remain open?

Before each main phase:

- Use Recon Directive before evolving it away?
- Current and next attacker?
- Exact Fire/Psychic/Darkness count?
- Does Hammer heads change the next opposing attack?
- Is hand disruption stronger before or after other thinning?
- What happens under both Hammer outcomes?

Before Phantom Dive:

- Is 200 damage modified or prevented?
- Where do all six counters go?
- Which later action converts each group?
- Can the targets evolve, heal, gain HP, or gain protection?
- Does this attack preserve a second attacker?
- What is the full remaining prize map?

End of turn:

- Opponent's best gust target?
- Opponent's best way to remove an Energy?
- Next attacker and both Energy?
- Remaining Boss, recovery, and Stamp?
- Am I offering an unnecessary two-Prize target?


SHORT TRAINING-AUDIT APPENDIX

The machine teacher is deliberately much narrower than this human guide. It
may label only fully observed legal stages: Budew/Dreepy opening preferences,
redundant Dreepy from an exact Poffin prompt, visible Drakloak/Dragapult
evolutions, a legal Recon Directive activation, and playing Crushing Hammer
when an opposing attached Energy is publicly visible.

It masks attacks, Phantom Dive counter placement, Adrena-Brain targets,
Supporter choice, recovery, matchup plans, coin outcomes, future draws, hidden
cards, and any incomplete legal stage. It never treats a future heads result or
eventual win as a label. It has no serving authority and cannot override the
learned policy.
