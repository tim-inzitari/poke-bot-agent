TEAM ROCKET'S SPIDOPS
PRACTICAL PILOT GUIDE FOR EXPERT REVIEW

Specialist ID: team-rockets-spidops
Guide version: team-rockets-spidops-north-star-v1
Prepared: 2026-07-28
Audience: world-champion and equivalent Pokémon TCG subject-matter experts
Status: research pilot; not yet expert-approved


HOW TO READ THIS PILOT

This guide covers the dedicated Team Rocket's Spidops specialist, not every
deck that happens to use the Tarountula/Spidops engine. The defining shell is
a 4-4 Spidops line, two Team Rocket's Mimikyu, two Team Rocket's Articuno, one
Team Rocket's Sneasel, and no more than one Team Rocket's Mewtwo ex. Spidops
is the normal attacker. Sneasel is a public-damage conversion finisher.
Mewtwo is a one-copy threshold breaker, not the deck's default center.

The concrete project representative is Pedro Henrique Pereira Calda's
226th-place list from the Campinas Regional on May 16, 2026. Its exact
60-card multiset is pinned by the project at
data/training_mixes/specialist_representatives.v1.json under
decks/team-rockets-spidops. Newer online lists preserve the same 15-Pokémon
core but change Trainer slots, so a pilot must confirm the registered list
before applying any LIST CHECK advice.

Labels used throughout:

SOURCE: directly supported by printed card text, a rulebook, a published deck
list, or public results in the source list at the end.

INFERENCE: practical reasoning from those sources. It is a proposal for expert
review, not an official ruling or a controlled causal result.

LIST CHECK: applies only if the registered 60 cards contain the named card.

SME CHECK: intentionally unresolved and suitable for expert correction.

Use the current cards and tournament rulings for exact legality. This pilot
paraphrases card text and arithmetic; it does not replace either.


EXECUTIVE IDENTITY

SOURCE: The pinned Campinas representative and two strong 2026 online lists
share the same Pokémon skeleton: four Tarountula, four Spidops, two Mimikyu,
two Articuno, one Sneasel, one Mewtwo ex, and one Lillie's Clefairy ex.
Nathaniel Alaniz placed second in a 213-player online event in July, and Nathan
Schaal won a 323-player online event in April with that skeleton.

SOURCE: The strongest official-event evidence for the broader engine is Juho
Kallama's seventh-place finish at the 2026 North America International
Championships. That list also played a 4-4 Spidops line, but it played two
Mewtwo and no Sneasel and was classified as Rocket's Mewtwo. It demonstrates
that the engine can succeed at the highest level. It must not be misreported
as a standalone Spidops result.

SOURCE: Standalone physical-event evidence is much weaker. Limitless's
dedicated Spidops overview records no Regional or International Top 8 for the
archetype at review time. The pinned Campinas list finished 226th of 1,725.
Current online results include excellent individual finishes, but aggregate
performance is closer to even and changes materially by format.

INFERENCE: Treat the specialist as a high-skill, fringe single-Prize tempo
deck, not a proven top-tier deck. Its edge is the number of different jobs
performed by the same compact board:

- every Team Rocket Pokémon adds 30 to Rocket Rush;
- every Spidops can recover one Basic Energy to itself each turn;
- Mimikyu is a zero-Retreat pivot and a conditional Tera attacker;
- Articuno protects Basic Team Rocket Pokémon from effects of attacks;
- Sneasel converts existing public Bench damage into a finishing hit;
- one Mewtwo can cash stored Benched Energy into 160, 220, or 280 damage; and
- the Team Rocket Supporter/Transceiver/Factory package turns board identity
  into search, draw, disruption, mobility, and gust.

The default prize plan is to make the opponent take six single-Prize
knockouts. Expose Mewtwo or Clefairy only when their exact present-turn value
is worth shortening that requirement.

QUICK START

If time is short, pilot from these rules:

1. Prefer going first unless a known matchup-specific attack on the second
   turn is stronger. Proton is explicitly legal on the first turn going first,
   and Spidops needs an evolution turn.
2. Start Mimikyu when possible. Its printed Retreat Cost is zero. Avoid
   starting Mewtwo or Clefairy unless forced.
3. Establish at least two Tarountula, preferably three when the matchup and
   prizes allow it. A lone Tarountula is a single gust or damage-placement
   failure point.
4. Fill the board with useful Team Rocket Pokémon. Rocket Rush is 30 times
   the current number of Team Rocket Pokémon in play: 30/60/90/120/150/180.
5. Put a Basic Energy in the discard deliberately, then use every relevant
   Charging Up before its Spidops leaves play or the turn ends.
6. Attack with Spidops by default. A six-Rocket board reaches 180. Brave
   Bangle reaches 210 against an Active Pokémon ex; Maximum Belt reaches 230.
7. Preserve a Team Rocket's Energy for Sneasel when a damaged Pokémon may
   retreat or be moved to the Bench. One Team Rocket's Energy supplies the
   two Darkness Energy for Strike the Sleeper.
8. Use Giovanni only after planning both switches. It must switch your Active
   Team Rocket Pokémon with your Benched Team Rocket Pokémon before it can
   bring one opposing Benched Pokémon Active.
9. Bench Mewtwo only for an exact extra 30 Rocket Rush damage, a planned
   Erasure Ball turn, or a prize-map reason worth exposing two prizes.
10. Bench Clefairy only for an exact Psychic-versus-Dragon line or its own
    attack. It is not Team Rocket, does not increase Rocket Rush, and turns
    Ariana's draw-to-eight condition off.
11. Play a Team Rocket Supporter before taking Team Rocket's Factory's
    optional draw two. Thin the hand before Ariana; preserve Archer for a
    legal post-knockout turn.
12. At the end of every turn, name the next attacker, the Energy it needs, and
    the number of prizes the opponent must still take.


CARD FACTS AND PRACTICAL ROLES

Team Rocket's Tarountula and Team Rocket's Spidops

SOURCE: Spidops is a 130-HP Stage 1. Charging Up may attach one Basic Energy
from the discard pile to that same Spidops once during the turn. Rocket Rush
costs one Grass and one Colorless and deals 30 damage for each Team Rocket
Pokémon in play.

The unmodified Rocket Rush table is:

1 Team Rocket Pokémon: 30
2 Team Rocket Pokémon: 60
3 Team Rocket Pokémon: 90
4 Team Rocket Pokémon: 120
5 Team Rocket Pokémon: 150
6 Team Rocket Pokémon: 180

INFERENCE: Tarountula is simultaneously an evolution seed and a current
30-damage increment. Spidops is simultaneously an attacker, Energy engine,
single-Prize sacrifice, and Rocket-body counter. The four-four count is not
decorative. Build multiple lines early enough that losing one does not end
both the attack stream and the Energy engine.

Charging Up has four constraints worth saying aloud:

- it retrieves Basic Energy, never Team Rocket's Energy;
- it attaches only to the Spidops that used the Ability;
- it needs a Basic Energy already in the public discard;
- an Ability not used before that Spidops leaves play is lost value.

INFERENCE: Two Spidops can each use Charging Up in one turn. That can prepare
the Active's next attack, a second Spidops, an Energy Switch line, or Benched
Energy for Mewtwo. Do not recover Energy automatically if attaching it creates
a liability or consumes the only intended Night Stretcher target, but the
default is to use a legal, useful activation now rather than count on a future
turn.

Team Rocket's Sneasel

SOURCE: Sneasel's Strike the Sleeper costs two Darkness Energy. It does 20
damage to one opposing Benched Pokémon for each damage counter already on that
Pokémon. Weakness and Resistance are not applied to that Benched damage.

SOURCE: One Team Rocket's Energy supplies two Darkness Energy while attached
to a Team Rocket Pokémon, so a single attachment can power Strike the Sleeper.

INFERENCE: Sneasel is the clearest mechanical distinction between this
specialist and the Mewtwo-centered shell. Suppose a Pokémon with maximum HP
H has already taken d damage and is now on the Bench. Strike the Sleeper adds
2d, so its total damage becomes 3d. Ignoring other effects, the target is
Knocked Out when:

3d >= H.

Examples:

- 70 existing damage becomes 210 total;
- 80 existing damage becomes 240 total;
- 90 existing damage becomes 270 total;
- 100 existing damage becomes 300 total;
- 110 existing damage becomes 330 total.

This creates a concrete two-step plan against a high-HP target: damage it with
Spidops, then finish it on the Bench if it retreats or if a switch effect puts
it there. Giovanni can help create that geometry: switch the Active Spidops
into a Benched Sneasel, then bring a different opposing Benched Pokémon
Active, leaving the previously damaged Active on the Bench for Strike the
Sleeper.

Count before committing. The Giovanni line requires:

- your Active to be Team Rocket;
- a Benched Team Rocket Sneasel to receive the first switch;
- an opposing Benched Pokémon that can be brought Active;
- the damaged target to remain on the opposing Bench;
- one Team Rocket's Energy, or another exact two-Darkness payment, on Sneasel;
- no prevention effect that invalidates the Benched damage.

The second attack must finish meaningful prizes or create a superior prize
map. A two-turn 90-plus-180 sequence is powerful against a 270-HP target, but
it is not automatically better than taking a clean Active knockout on each
turn. Sneasel is a one-copy resource; check whether it is prized or already
discarded before constructing the line.

LIST CHECK: Brave Bangle and Maximum Belt modify damage to an opposing Active
Pokémon ex. They do not increase Strike the Sleeper's damage to the Bench.

Team Rocket's Mimikyu

SOURCE: Mimikyu has no Retreat Cost. Gemstone Mimicry costs one Psychic and
one Colorless and chooses one attack of the opponent's Active Tera Pokémon to
use as its attack.

INFERENCE: Mimikyu is the preferred low-commitment opener and a reliable
between-knockouts pivot. One Team Rocket's Energy can pay the Psychic and
Colorless requirement because it supplies two Energy in any combination of
Psychic and Darkness. Gemstone Mimicry is matchup-specific, not free damage:
verify that the opponent is Tera and resolve every copied attack cost,
condition, target, and effect exactly. Do not assume a copied attack works
because its printed damage number looks attractive.

Team Rocket's Articuno

SOURCE: Repelling Veil prevents effects of attacks done to the player's Basic
Team Rocket Pokémon. Its text explicitly says damage is not an effect.

Articuno therefore:

- protects Basic Team Rocket Pokémon, including Tarountula, Mimikyu, Sneasel,
  Mewtwo, and Articuno;
- does not protect evolved Spidops;
- does not prevent ordinary attack damage;
- does not generally stop opposing Abilities, because it names effects of
  attacks.

INFERENCE: Bench one Articuno when the opposing attack text makes the Ability
material or when another Rocket body is needed. Do not bench both by habit.
The pinned list has no Basic Water Energy, so treat Articuno as support rather
than an attacker unless the registered variant can actually pay its attack
cost.

SME CHECK: Build an interaction sheet for the current field. "Effect of an
attack," damage, damage-counter placement, and Ability damage are often
conflated in casual discussion. Use official rulings for each disputed case.

Team Rocket's Mewtwo ex

SOURCE: Mewtwo has 280 HP and cannot attack unless at least four Team Rocket
Pokémon are in play. Erasure Ball costs two Psychic and one Colorless, starts
at 160, and may discard up to two Energy cards from Benched Pokémon for 60
more per card: 160, 220, or 280.

INFERENCE: In this list Mewtwo is a threshold breaker and closer. It is not
the default opener or first bench. Use it when one of these is true:

- 160, 220, or 280 takes prizes that Spidops cannot take efficiently;
- its 280 HP and resistance change the opponent's exchange;
- adding the sixth Rocket body raises Rocket Rush from 150 to an exact 180;
- a two-Prize liability no longer gives the opponent a shorter winning map;
- the board already holds expendable Benched Energy.

Choose the smallest discard count that takes the same current prizes. With
Maximum Belt against an Active Pokémon ex, the nominal values are 210, 270,
and 330 before Weakness, Resistance, or other modifiers. Do not discard a
second Energy for cosmetic damage.

One Team Rocket's Energy can supply Mewtwo's two Psychic requirement, but a
third Energy is still required for Colorless. Mewtwo also needs four Team
Rocket Pokémon in play at the moment it attacks. Clefairy does not count.

Lillie's Clefairy ex

SOURCE: Fairy Zone changes the Weakness of opposing Dragon Pokémon in play to
Psychic, applied as x2. Clefairy is itself a Psychic Pokémon. Full Moon Rondo
starts at 20 and adds 20 for each Benched Pokémon on both sides.

INFERENCE: Clefairy is an opt-in subplan. Spidops is Grass type, so Fairy Zone
does not make Rocket Rush hit Psychic Weakness. The main beneficiaries are
Psychic attackers such as Mewtwo, Mimikyu, or Clefairy itself. Before benching
Clefairy, compare the exact current threshold with four costs:

- Clefairy gives up two prizes;
- it occupies a bench slot;
- it contributes zero to Rocket Rush and Mewtwo's four-Rocket requirement;
- its presence makes Ariana draw to five rather than eight.

The pinned list has only one Basic Psychic Energy, and Team Rocket's Energy
cannot remain attached to a non-Team-Rocket Pokémon. Do not declare a Clefairy
attack without a legal attachment plan.


ENERGY ARCHITECTURE

SOURCE: Team Rocket's Energy is Special Energy. It can attach only to Team
Rocket Pokémon and is discarded if attached to anything else. While attached,
it provides two Energy in any combination of Psychic and Darkness.

This produces three different resource classes:

Basic Grass

- pays Rocket Rush's required Grass;
- is the normal Charging Up target;
- may pay Colorless requirements;
- can be moved by Energy Switch.

Basic Psychic

- can be recovered by Charging Up;
- pays Psychic costs on eligible Pokémon;
- can be moved by Energy Switch;
- is the only Psychic attachment that can remain on Clefairy.

Team Rocket's Energy

- cannot pay Rocket Rush's Grass requirement;
- can pay the Colorless portion of Rocket Rush;
- powers Sneasel's two-Darkness attack by itself;
- powers Mimikyu's Psychic-plus-Colorless attack by itself;
- supplies Mewtwo's two Psychic but not its third Energy;
- cannot be recovered by Charging Up;
- cannot be moved by an effect that specifies Basic Energy.

INFERENCE: Track Special Energy separately from energy units. Team Rocket's
Energy is one Energy card even while providing two Energy. Erasure Ball
discards Energy cards, not units, so discarding one Team Rocket's Energy is one
discard and adds 60, not 120.

The ideal early loop is often:

1. put Basic Grass into the discard through Ultra Ball or a natural discard;
2. evolve a safe Tarountula;
3. Charging Up that Grass to Spidops;
4. make the second attachment needed for Rocket Rush;
5. develop another Spidops and repeat.

LIST CHECK: Energy Switch moves Basic Energy. A recovered Grass or Psychic can
move from a Benched Spidops to the intended attacker. It does not move Team
Rocket's Energy. Decide whether the moved Energy is more valuable on the
current attacker than as future Erasure Ball fuel or a second Spidops attack.

Avoid these energy errors:

- attaching Team Rocket's Energy to Clefairy;
- counting Team Rocket's Energy as Grass;
- asking Charging Up to recover Special Energy;
- spending the only Team Rocket's Energy before a live Sneasel line;
- putting every Basic Energy on one gustable Spidops;
- discarding two Benched Energy to Mewtwo when one takes the same prizes.


OPENING AND BOARD CONSTRUCTION

Choosing first or second

SOURCE: Proton explicitly may be played during the first turn when its player
goes first and searches up to three Basic Team Rocket Pokémon into the hand.

INFERENCE: Prefer first as the unspecialized default. It lets Tarountula evolve
on the next turn and gives Proton a unique first-turn use. This is not backed
by a controlled first-versus-second study.

Going second can be reasonable when a known opponent and opening hand create
an immediate Mimikyu attack into a Tera Active with Team Rocket's Energy. It
may also offer extra draw and the first normal Supporter turn, but it gives the
opponent the first evolution/attack tempo. Do not choose second merely because
the deck contains attacks that one Special Energy can technically pay.

Preferred opening Active

INFERENCE:

1. Mimikyu: zero Retreat Cost, useful Rocket body, conditional attack.
2. Tarountula: advances the engine but is fragile and risks losing an
   evolution seed.
3. Sneasel or Articuno: single-Prize Rocket bodies, but each has a more
   valuable specialist role and nonzero Retreat Cost.
4. Mewtwo: two-Prize exposure and a three-Energy Retreat Cost.
5. Clefairy: non-Rocket, two-Prize exposure, and may disable Ariana's stronger
   draw before its ability matters.

Opening bench targets

INFERENCE: Aim for two Tarountula before luxury Basics; three is often better
against gust, spread, or a long game. Then choose among Mimikyu, Articuno, and
Sneasel for a real job. Do not automatically reveal Mewtwo or Clefairy.

Buddy-Buddy Poffin in this exact core can bench the 50-HP Tarountula and 60-HP
Mimikyu. It cannot bench Sneasel, Articuno, Mewtwo, or Clefairy because their
HP exceeds Poffin's printed limit. Proton searches Basic Team Rocket Pokémon
to hand, so it can find Tarountula, Mimikyu, Articuno, Sneasel, or Mewtwo but
not Clefairy and not Spidops.

Before finalizing a Proton search, ask:

- How many Tarountula are already visible?
- Is Mimikyu needed as the Active pivot?
- Does Articuno's attack-effect protection matter?
- Is Sneasel needed now or safer in the deck?
- Does Mewtwo create an exact threshold, or merely expose two prizes?
- Are there enough bench slots to play the cards searched?

Bench templates

All-single-Prize six-Rocket template:

Active Spidops
Benched Spidops or Tarountula
Benched Tarountula or Spidops
Benched Mimikyu
Benched Articuno
Benched Sneasel

This reaches 180 Rocket Rush without presenting a two-Prize Pokémon.

Threshold-breaker template:

Active Spidops or Mewtwo
Benched two Spidops-line bodies
Benched Mimikyu
Benched Articuno or Sneasel
Benched Mewtwo when it is not Active

This preserves the four-Rocket Mewtwo requirement and can reach six Rockets,
but Mewtwo changes the opponent's prize map.

Dragon template:

Reserve one slot for Clefairy only after calculating the Psychic Weakness
knockout. A full board containing Clefairy can have at most five Team Rocket
Pokémon under the ordinary five-Bench limit, so unmodified Rocket Rush falls
from 180 to at most 150 unless a different bench-expansion effect exists.


SUPPORTER, ITEM, AND STADIUM SEQUENCING

Team Rocket's Ariana

SOURCE: Ariana draws until the hand has five cards, or until it has eight if
all Pokémon in play are Team Rocket Pokémon.

INFERENCE: Thin the hand with useful Items, evolutions, attachments, and
Stadiums before Ariana. Confirm the whole board, including Clefairy. Ariana
does not shuffle dead cards away; if the hand already has five or eight cards,
it may draw nothing.

Lillie's Determination

SOURCE: It shuffles the hand into the deck and draws six, or eight when the
player has exactly six Prize cards remaining.

INFERENCE: It is strongest before taking the first prize and when the current
hand needs a reset rather than simple replenishment. Do not burn it before
playing reusable resources from hand unless shuffling those cards is the
point.

Team Rocket's Archer

SOURCE: Archer is legal only if one of the player's Team Rocket Pokémon was
Knocked Out during the opponent's last turn. Both players shuffle their hands
into their decks; the Archer player draws five and the opponent draws three.

INFERENCE: Archer turns the expected loss of a single-Prize Spidops into hand
disruption. Preserve it or a Transceiver route when the opponent has assembled
a large hand and a Rocket knockout is likely. It is illegal after Clefairy
alone was Knocked Out and unavailable on turns without the printed condition.

Team Rocket's Giovanni

SOURCE: Giovanni first switches the player's Active Team Rocket Pokémon with
a Benched Team Rocket Pokémon. If that switch occurs, it brings one opposing
Benched Pokémon Active. The Advanced Player's Rulebook confirms that the first
switch must be possible; Giovanni cannot be played with no eligible Benched
Team Rocket Pokémon.

INFERENCE: Giovanni is switch plus gust, not gust alone. Preserve an eligible
own Bench target. Plan the resulting Active, its Energy, Retreat state, attack,
and the opposing target before playing the Supporter. Its highest-value
Spidops-specific line often promotes an already powered Sneasel while moving a
damaged opposing Active to the Bench, but ordinary exact-prize gusts are just
as valid.

Team Rocket's Transceiver

SOURCE: Transceiver searches the deck for a Supporter with Team Rocket in its
name.

INFERENCE: Treat each copy as the best currently legal Rocket Supporter, not
as an automatic Ariana. Map the turn first:

- Proton for Basic setup;
- Ariana for conditional refill;
- Archer for legal post-knockout disruption;
- Giovanni for a fully planned switch/gust;
- LIST CHECK Petrel in variants that include it for an exact Trainer search.

Team Rocket's Factory

SOURCE: Once per turn, a player who played a Supporter with "Team Rocket" in
its name from hand that turn may draw two cards.

INFERENCE: Play the Rocket Supporter first, then use Factory after useful hand
thinning. Factory is symmetrical: an opponent who meets its condition may
also draw. In a Rocket mirror, do not assume the Stadium belongs only to you.
If another Stadium must be played, use an available Factory draw before
replacing it when legal.

Search and recovery

Ultra Ball is both an evolution search and a controlled Basic-Energy discard
outlet. Its two-card cost is real. Before discarding, check singleton Sneasel,
Mewtwo, Clefairy, Psychic Energy, Energy Switch, Sacred Ash, Energy Search,
and Maximum Belt.

Night Stretcher can return a Pokémon or Basic Energy from the discard to the
hand. Use it for the attack stream or exact attachment; it does not return
Team Rocket's Energy. Sacred Ash recycles up to five Pokémon into the deck,
which is stronger for a long stream but requires another search or draw to
access them.

INFERENCE: Recovery priority is state-dependent:

1. the Pokémon that creates the next legal attacker;
2. the Basic Energy that makes the current exact attack;
3. Sneasel when the public damage conversion is live;
4. Tarountula/Spidops redundancy for a longer single-Prize race;
5. Mewtwo only when its exact threshold remains necessary.


ATTACK AND PRIZE SEQUENCING

Spidops-first plan

Use this when 120-210 damage takes efficient prizes, the opponent must take
multiple single-Prize knockouts, or the board needs time to accumulate Energy.
The goal is not merely to attack every turn. The goal is to present a
replaceable attacker every turn while keeping the opponent's shortest prize
route at six.

Exact Tool thresholds from the pinned list:

- six Rockets, no Tool: 180;
- six Rockets, Brave Bangle versus Active Pokémon ex: 210;
- six Rockets, Maximum Belt versus Active Pokémon ex: 230;
- five Rockets, no Tool: 150;
- five Rockets, Brave Bangle versus Active Pokémon ex: 180;
- five Rockets, Maximum Belt versus Active Pokémon ex: 200.

Apply Weakness, Resistance, and all effects only after verifying the current
game state. Attach a Tool when it crosses a current or deliberately planned
threshold, not because the target happens to be a Pokémon ex.

Sneasel conversion plan

Damage a high-HP Active with Spidops, preserve Sneasel and one Team Rocket's
Energy, then exploit a retreat or create Bench geometry with Giovanni. The
public formula 3d >= H is a fast screening tool, not a substitute for effects.
For a 280-HP target, at least 100 existing damage is sufficient because
Sneasel adds 200. For a 330-HP target, 110 existing damage is sufficient.

Do not damage several targets without a reason. Concentrate damage where
Sneasel can convert it before recovery, devolution, healing, or Bench
protection changes the line.

Mewtwo closer plan

Build Mewtwo only after checking:

- four Team Rocket Pokémon will remain in play;
- its full three-Energy attack cost is paid;
- Benched Energy cards are available for the desired 160/220/280 result;
- the chosen discard count takes more useful prizes than Spidops;
- exposing Mewtwo does not let the opponent win with one fewer knockout.

Sometimes the correct Mewtwo attack discards zero. Sometimes the correct game
never benches Mewtwo. The singleton provides optionality; it does not impose a
script.

Clefairy/Dragon plan

Bench Clefairy only when Psychic Weakness makes an exact prize line superior.
Recalculate Rocket Rush after losing a Rocket body slot and recalculate Ariana
after the board is no longer all Rocket. If Clefairy attacks, count both
benches for Full Moon Rondo and verify its non-Rocket Energy payments.


MATCHUP FRAMEWORK

Evidence warning

SOURCE: In the reviewed current CRI online snapshot, Rocket Spidops was
91-93-3, 48.66%. Listed matchup samples included 9-4 into N's Zoroark, 2-9-1
into straight Dragapult, 6-6 into Dragapult/Dusknoir, 5-6 into Slowking,
3-6-1 into Mega Greninja, and 7-1 into Alakazam/Dudunsparce. The prior POR
snapshot was 483-399-10, 54.15%.

These are descriptive online records, not controlled matchup probabilities.
Lists, players, event structures, sample sizes, and even the format differ.
The movement from 54.15% to 48.66% is itself a warning against carrying old
percentages forward. Use the following plans as hypotheses for testing.

Against N's Zoroark and other evolving single-Prize engines

INFERENCE:

- prioritize multiple Tarountula so the engine survives targeted knockouts;
- force a six-knockout race and avoid Mewtwo/Clefairy unless they take a
  decisive multi-Prize knockout;
- use Archer after a Rocket knockout when reducing an assembled hand to three
  disrupts the next chain;
- use Giovanni for the highest-value evolution seed or support target that an
  exact Rocket Rush can remove;
- preserve Sacred Ash for a longer attacker stream.

The 9-4 current online record is encouraging but too small to declare favored.

Against Alakazam/Dudunsparce and low-HP support boards

INFERENCE:

- map which Basics and support Pokémon are already within 120-180;
- keep single-Prize attackers flowing rather than volunteering Mewtwo;
- Giovanni can convert board access into prizes when the first own switch is
  legal;
- Sneasel matters only after a worthwhile target carries public damage on the
  Bench;
- Archer can punish a large post-search hand after a Rocket knockout.

The current 7-1 record is capability evidence from eight games, not a stable
estimate.

Against Dragapult variants

INFERENCE:

- develop more than one Tarountula immediately; do not let one damaged Basic
  represent the whole future attack stream;
- Articuno protects Basics from effects of attacks, but not Spidops and not
  ordinary damage. Verify whether each damage-counter interaction is an
  attack effect or comes from an Ability;
- avoid benching a no-job low-HP Basic when it gives spread an efficient
  extra prize;
- consider Clefairy only when Psychic Weakness enables an exact Mewtwo,
  Mimikyu, or Clefairy knockout. It does not boost Grass-type Spidops;
- count whether a damaged Dragon can be finished on the Bench by Sneasel;
- do not overread the current 2-9-1 straight-Dragapult sample or the 6-6
  Dragapult/Dusknoir sample, but treat the pressure as a serious test case.

Against Dusknoir, Froslass, Starmie, Greninja, or other damage-placement decks

INFERENCE:

- separate attack effects from Ability damage. Articuno is not blanket Bench
  protection;
- bench only Pokémon with a current job;
- evolve vulnerable Tarountula when doing so changes a public damage-counter
  threshold, while recognizing that Spidops loses Articuno protection;
- preserve a second line outside the easiest multi-Prize damage pattern;
- use Night Stretcher/Sacred Ash before simultaneous losses exhaust the
  evolution stream;
- prefer immediate exact prizes over slow setup if the board will not survive
  another damage-placement cycle.

Against high-HP two-Prize attackers

INFERENCE: Choose among three threshold tools:

1. Spidops plus Brave Bangle or Maximum Belt for 180-230;
2. Spidops damage followed by Sneasel's triple-total conversion on the Bench;
3. Mewtwo's 220/280, optionally 270/330 with Maximum Belt against an Active
   Pokémon ex.

The best option is the one that wins the prize race with the least two-Prize
exposure and recoverable Energy loss. Plan two turns of Energy, not just the
current damage number.

Against Tera attackers

INFERENCE: Keep Mimikyu accessible and a Team Rocket's Energy available, then
read every public attack on the opposing Active. Gemstone Mimicry may offer a
ceiling the deck otherwise lacks, but copied conditions can make an attack
weak or unusable. Compare the fully resolved copy with Rocket Rush, Sneasel,
and Mewtwo; never rank it from printed base damage alone.

Against Fire attackers

SOURCE: Spidops is Fire-Weak.

INFERENCE: Avoid presenting a single fully loaded Spidops as the only
attacker. Build redundant lines, calculate Weakness before attaching a Tool,
and ask whether Sneasel or Mewtwo can change the exchange. A Fire label alone
does not determine the matchup; attack speed, prize value, gust, and Bench
pressure still matter.

Against Rocket mirrors

INFERENCE:

- both sides may use Factory after a qualifying Supporter;
- Archer is easier to enable in a single-Prize exchange;
- Articuno does not prevent normal Rocket Rush damage;
- target Tarountula when removing the future attacker is worth more than
  damaging a current Spidops;
- preserve Giovanni's legal own switch; mirror players can strand a poor
  Active if their mobility is exhausted;
- the dedicated list should not imitate the two-Mewtwo shell. Use its Sneasel
  and six-single-Prize options deliberately.


RESOURCE AND PRIZE CHECKS

The pinned list has several one-copy resources:

- Sneasel;
- Mewtwo ex;
- Clefairy ex;
- Basic Psychic Energy;
- Energy Switch;
- Sacred Ash;
- Energy Search;
- Maximum Belt.

At the first deck search, count what is accessible. You cannot inspect Prize
cards directly, but a legal search and known 60-card list let an expert infer
what may be missing. Do not build a line around a singleton until its location
is known enough to access it.

Track throughout the game:

- Tarountula and Spidops remaining;
- Basic Grass in deck, hand, discard, and play;
- Team Rocket's Energy remaining and attached;
- Night Stretcher and Sacred Ash access;
- Sneasel availability;
- Mewtwo availability and Energy fuel;
- both Tools and the one Maximum Belt;
- Team Rocket Supporters left for Transceiver;
- Factory availability and whether the opponent benefits from it.

INFERENCE: A recoverable Basic Grass and a nonrecoverable Team Rocket's Energy
are not interchangeable even when both pay a Colorless cost. Preserve the
resource whose unique printed role is still needed.


COMMON PILOT ERRORS

1. Calling the deck Rocket's Mewtwo and defaulting to Mewtwo-first play.
2. Keeping only one Tarountula in play and losing the whole evolution stream.
3. Filling the bench with Mewtwo or Clefairy before they have a job.
4. Counting Clefairy toward Rocket Rush or Power Saver.
5. Forgetting that Clefairy turns Ariana's draw-to-eight condition off.
6. Counting Team Rocket's Energy as Grass for Rocket Rush.
7. Trying to recover Team Rocket's Energy with Charging Up.
8. Forgetting that each Charging Up attaches to that same Spidops.
9. Leaving a useful Charging Up unused before evolving, switching, or ending.
10. Treating Articuno as prevention of damage, Ability damage, or effects on
    evolved Spidops.
11. Starting Giovanni without an eligible Benched Team Rocket switch target.
12. Moving the wrong opposing Pokémon Active and losing the planned Sneasel
    target on the Bench.
13. Forgetting that Strike the Sleeper needs existing damage counters and
    does not apply Weakness or Resistance.
14. Applying Brave Bangle or Maximum Belt to Sneasel's Benched damage.
15. Discarding a second Benched Energy to Mewtwo when one takes the same
    prizes.
16. Treating one Team Rocket's Energy as two Energy cards for Erasure Ball.
17. Attaching Team Rocket's Energy to Clefairy.
18. Playing Ariana before thinning the hand.
19. Forgetting Factory after a Team Rocket Supporter.
20. Assuming online matchup percentages prove a matchup.


TURN CHECKLIST

Before setup

- Who goes first, and what exact turn-two advantage follows?
- Which Basic is the safest Active?
- Can the board establish two or three Tarountula?
- Which specialist Basic has a job in this matchup?

At the first deck search

- Which singleton cards are accessible?
- Are enough Tarountula and Spidops available for two attack lines?
- Is Sneasel available?
- Is Basic Psychic available if a Mewtwo or Clefairy line is intended?
- Which Team Rocket Supporters remain for Transceiver?

Before playing a Supporter

- Does Proton create more useful Basics than a draw Supporter?
- Does Ariana draw to five or eight on this exact board?
- Is Lillie's Determination still at the six-Prize draw-eight window?
- Is Archer legal from the opponent's immediately previous turn?
- Can Giovanni complete its own switch, and what exact target follows?
- Will Factory be usable afterward?

Before each Charging Up

- Is the target Energy Basic?
- Which Spidops receives it?
- Does it complete a current or next known attack?
- Is Energy Switch planned?
- Is another Spidops a better recipient?

Before benching Mewtwo

- Does it cross a current Rocket Rush threshold?
- Can it legally attack with four Rockets and three Energy?
- Which Benched Energy cards would Erasure Ball discard?
- How many prizes does exposing it let the opponent take?

Before benching Clefairy

- Is the relevant opposing Pokémon Dragon?
- Which Psychic attacker actually uses the new Weakness?
- Does the exact knockout compensate for losing 30 Rocket Rush damage,
  Ariana's draw-eight, one bench slot, and a two-Prize target?

Before attacking

- Count Team Rocket Pokémon again.
- Calculate Rocket Rush from the current count.
- Apply Tool, Weakness, Resistance, and effects exactly.
- If using Sneasel, calculate 2d additional Bench damage.
- If using Mewtwo, compare 160/220/280 and discard the minimum needed.
- Name the next attacker and its attachments.

At end of turn

- Did every useful Charging Up happen?
- Was Factory used after the qualifying Supporter?
- Is Mimikyu or another pivot ready after a knockout?
- Is Sneasel protected until its conversion turn?
- How many knockouts must each player still take?

SHORT TRAINING-HEURISTIC APPENDIX

The companion machine teacher is intentionally much narrower than this human
pilot guide. It uses only the exact current observation and exact supplied
legal options. It never reads future draws, future outcomes, opponent hidden
cards, or hidden deck/prize contents.

Implemented sparse preferences are limited to:

- prefer Mimikyu as an exact initial Active over less flexible core Basics;
- establish redundant Tarountula from exact setup, Buddy-Buddy Poffin, or
  Proton options;
- prefer a visible legal Spidops evolution over unrelated main actions;
- prefer a visible Spidops Charging Up activation only when a Basic Grass or
  Basic Psychic Energy is already in the public discard.

The teacher deliberately masks:

- attack selection and all damage thresholds;
- Sneasel/Giovanni conversion turns;
- Tool attachment;
- manual Energy attachment and Energy Switch;
- recovery decisions;
- Supporter choice outside the narrow exact searches;
- Mimikyu copied-attack resolution;
- Mewtwo and Clefairy thresholds;
- matchup-specific prediction.

Every supplied legal combination in a supported stage must resolve and receive
a finite score. A malformed option, unknown prompt origin, unresolved card,
invalid combination, duplicate combination, or preference margin below 0.25
masks the whole stage. The artifact is an auxiliary training target only. It
has no selector, runtime-action, serving, or policy-override authority.

EVIDENCE GAPS

- No world-champion SME has approved this pilot.
- No controlled current-format first-versus-second study was found.
- Standalone physical-event evidence is sparse and has no major Top 8.
- Current online aggregates mix players, lists, event structures, and skill.
- Many individual matchup samples are too small for stable estimates.
- The pinned Campinas Trainer package predates newer July variants.
- A current judge-reviewed matrix of all Articuno interactions is still
  needed.
- No authoritative full matchup guide for the exact pinned list was found.
- The strongest official result belongs to a two-Mewtwo hybrid, not this
  one-Mewtwo Sneasel specialist.
