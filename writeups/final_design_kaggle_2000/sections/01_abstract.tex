\section*{Abstract}

Powerful Hand Alakazam turns Pok\'emon TCG play into a coupled counting and
control problem: hand size determines attack output, Prize values determine
the shortest winning route, and every optional draw changes deck-out risk.
The agent must solve that problem inside a variable, multi-stage legal action
space without reading hidden opponent information. We formulate the final
design as a constrained direct policy. A pinned simulator supplies the legal
set; an autoregressive decoder assigns probability to every ordered legal
selection; a public-rule semantic key prevents distinct choices from sharing
one representation; and a bounded residual augments, rather than replaces,
the learned option embedding. Training adds a masked three-action Prize-plan
advantage, while serving remains direct-policy inference. The resulting
design connects deck mathematics to model mathematics: exact knockout
thresholds, safe hand slack, forced-draw feasibility, and Prize routes become
causal quantities that the controller can represent and learn.
