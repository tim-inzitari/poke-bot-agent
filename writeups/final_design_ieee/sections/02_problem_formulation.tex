\section{Problem Formulation and Threat Model}

\subsection{Information set and legal action set}

Let $I_t$ be the complete information legally available to the acting seat at
decision time $t$. It includes the acting player's own hand and other
legitimately private state, the public board and zones, public action history,
legally revealed card information, and the current legal-option list. It does
not include the opponent's hidden hand identities, either deck order, or
unrevealed Prize identities. A deterministic observation builder produces
$x_t=f(I_t)$ and a causal history $h_t=(x_{\leq t},a_{<t})$.

The pinned simulator returns a variable set
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{A}_t = \mathcal{A}(I_t)=\{o_{t,1},\ldots,o_{t,n_t}\}.
\end{equation}
The policy chooses only among these current options. Some conceptual actions
span several engine prompts, so a complete action is a finite sequence
$a_t=(o_{t,i_1},\ldots,o_{t,i_K},\textsc{stop})$ in which the simulator
recomputes legality after each stage. The policy never caches an old option
index as a future command. This distinction matters because an index is an
address inside one transient list, not an action identity.

We use the terminal return $z\in\{-1,0,+1\}$ for loss, draw, and win. Auxiliary
targets may describe public resources, Prize potential, or short-horizon
outcomes, but they are masked when the required causal interval is incomplete
or ambiguous. Missing supervision is not encoded as a numerical zero.

\subsection{Threat model}

We consider four classes of failure.

\textbf{Representation collision.} Two publicly distinguishable options with
different simulator semantics share one feature key. Harmless permutations are
not actionable collisions; the criterion is a semantic difference, not byte
inequality.

\textbf{Privileged-input dependence.} A pre-transition choice changes when
only hidden opponent cards, deck order, unrevealed Prizes, or future events are
changed. Metamorphic pairs must leave sanitized observations, option features,
and policy logits identical.

\textbf{Authority inversion.} A learned rule or legality predictor adds,
removes, or rewrites simulator options. Such a model may be a diagnostic head,
but it is never an execution authority.

\textbf{Evidence conflation.} An integration receipt, training diagnostic,
public score, descriptive replay slice, or shadow-model metric is reported as a
formal policy result. We control this with visible claim labels---\Measured,
\Verified, \Configured, \Historical, and \NotMeasured---and with receipt-bound
tables whose disposition accompanies the number.

\subsection{Scope}

The active derivative uses a greedy, factorized policy. RTP, MCTS, online
search, and the recursive PokeRLM roadmap are excluded from the packaged action
path. A separately trained shadow planner may supply research diagnostics, but
its presence does not imply action authority. Likewise, the Prize-plan v2
sidecar is a trainer-only advantage provider. It is absent from the policy state
dictionary, runtime Fusion, selector, serving bundle, and Kaggle package.
