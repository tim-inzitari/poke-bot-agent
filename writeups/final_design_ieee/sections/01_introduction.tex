\section{Introduction}

A digital trading card game exposes a compact interface---a state and a list of
legal menu choices---but the underlying decision is neither a fixed discrete
action nor a fully observed Markov state. Search, recovery, target selection,
ordered multi-select, and optional stopping can create several prompts for one
conceptual action. The meaning of an option depends on its source, target,
count, effect, and the public state in which the engine produced it. Meanwhile,
each player knows private facts that the other does not. These properties make
the problem naturally related to partially observable sequential decision
making~\cite{kaelbling1998planning,sutton2018reinforcement}, but they also create
software-specific failure modes that a generic POMDP description does not
capture.

The first failure mode is \emph{legal-option aliasing}. A policy may receive an
option index or a lossy payload in which distinct public choices collapse to
the same representation. Increasing model capacity cannot recover a distinction
that is absent from the input. The second is \emph{information leakage}. Fields
that are useful as offline targets---future transitions, terminal outcomes,
opponent private cards, or unrevealed Prize identities---must not become
deployment features. The third is \emph{claim leakage}: training accuracy,
public leaderboard score, shadow-planner diagnostics, and formal held-out win
rate are measurements of different objects. Combining them into one apparent
performance result produces a paper that is stronger than its evidence.

This work describes the finalized design of an isolated Alakazam
rule-derivative controller governed by a versioned, typed contract
\cite{projectContract2026}. The controller is direct policy at deployment. The
official pinned simulator enumerates legal options and executes the selected
current index; no learned component can make an illegal option legal. A causal
state/history encoder and the legacy card representation are preserved. Beside
them, a separately versioned residual represents exact public rule semantics:
selection context, numeric value, count range, remaining resource budget,
effect, and stable source/target/area/slot bindings. New branches begin at exact
zero and become trainable only if a full corpus census supports them. This
turns representation migration into an empirical, reversible change rather
than an architectural assertion.

The resulting paper is organized around evidence boundaries. Its contributions
are:

\begin{itemize}
\item an acting-player information-set contract that separates runtime fields,
target-only fields, and forbidden hidden truth;
\item a factorized legal-action representation in which semantic repair is
additive, zero-bypassable, and subordinate to simulator legality;
\item a corpus-driven activation rule that accepts only the public-rule
projection and keeps unsupported metadata, repaired-head, and checklist gates
inert;
\item a checksum-bound training lifecycle covering a 25-epoch full-model
bootstrap, direct-policy self-play, expert refresh, and a trainer-only H3
Prize-plan canary;
\item paired evaluation and claim-status conventions that preserve failed,
waived, historical, and not-yet-measured results instead of relabeling them.
\end{itemize}

The strongest measured technical result is a collision repair over 18.4 million
option records. Gameplay observations are included because a systems paper
should expose its current evidence, but their scope is narrow. A Kaggle score is
not treated as a controlled ablation; a formal holdout whose broader gate failed
is not presented as promotion; and a waived holdout with zero games is not a
pass. This conservative separation follows reproducibility practice in which
the experimental unit, data lineage, and reporting decision are explicit
\cite{pineau2021improving}.
