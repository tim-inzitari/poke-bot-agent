\section{Reproducibility, Ethics, and Limitations}

The reproducibility unit is a digest-bound claim rather than a mutable filename.
The minimum identity set contains the typed goal contract, corpus and split
manifests, feature/target/checklist schemas, simulator and catalog build,
checkpoint ancestry, optimizer scope, deck multiset, runtime tree, opponent
packages, seed/seat schedule, and evaluation receipt. Package manifests bind
every archive member. Superseded checkpoints and failed attempts remain
immutable evidence and cannot be renamed into an active result. This approach
is consistent with the broader need for explicit experimental artifacts and
reporting decisions in machine learning~\cite{pineau2021improving}.

The official Kaggle simulation environment~\cite{kaggleSimulationOverview}
is pinned as the rules source. The project snapshot records Kaggle Environments
1.32.6 and a checksum-bound Linux x86-64 \texttt{libcg.so}. The paper archive
does not redistribute that binary, replays, private trajectories, model or
optimizer weights, Pok\'emon card images, or logos. Public strategy sources are
cited rather than copied. Own-hand information is legitimate for the acting
seat, but opponent hidden information remains excluded; for this reason we use
``acting-player-visible'' rather than the overly narrow phrase ``public only.''

Several limitations remain. First, zero repaired semantic collisions in this
corpus do not prove that every future simulator option is covered. Schema
versioning and fail-closed unknown handling remain necessary. Second, the
literal card-743 eligibility rule makes the evidence Alakazam-centered. The
causal encoder and factorized decoder may transfer, but the distribution of
selection contexts, targets, and Prize plans may not. Third, a 600.0 public
score entangles policy, deck, opponent pool, and platform variance. It is not
an adapter ablation. Fourth, the iteration-2 holdout was numerically strong but
failed a broader pipeline gate; the iteration-3 formal result is absent by
explicit waiver. Fifth, the H3 sidecar has prediction and ESS/clipping evidence
but no completed required multi-arm gameplay evaluation. Sixth, current
checklist gates are inert, and recursive planning/RTP remains outside the
packaged action path.

Finally, auditability carries cost. Exact 8,196-game iterations, full expert
refresh, paired seat balance, immutable artifacts, fleet parity, and isolated
formal evaluation slow experimentation and consume substantial compute and
storage. We consider this cost justified when the alternative is an
irreproducible strength claim whose legality, information boundary, or data
lineage cannot be reconstructed.
